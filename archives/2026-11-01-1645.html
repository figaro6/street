Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

No Wall Street open today: it is Sunday, and US and Tel Aviv markets are shut until Monday. Instead the weekend is about positioning after October's AI rout. Morgan Stanley's Mike Wilson says to buy any dip, even one to 7,100 on the S&P 500, while credit insurance on the biggest AI spenders is at its most expensive since 2021. OPEC+ confirmed it will keep December output steady, and Tower Semiconductor reports on Monday after losing 42% since June.

7,100

Wilson's "buy the dip" level for the S&P 500

Morgan Stanley's chief US equity strategist told Bloomberg there are a few "clearing events" ahead: the midterms on Tuesday, stress over AI spending and tighter liquidity. Together they could take the S&P 500 to about 7,100, roughly 9% below its record and 6% below Friday's close of 7,580. Wilson would buy that dip: once the correction that, by Wilson's account, has been running under the surface for months is done, Wilson sees the index reaching 8,000. Wilson notes stocks have always been higher six to twelve months after a midterm, but not always by year-end, as 2018 showed.

The stories behind the numbers

US · strategy
8,000

Morgan Stanley's Wilson: buy the dip, whoever wins Tuesday

Wilson sees the midterms, AI-spending stress and liquidity limits as "clearing events" that could push the S&P 500 down to around 7,100. After that, Wilson expects 8,000, and says this midterm carries extra uncertainty because the president has signalled a big, unspecified move after the vote.

US · credit
2021

Investors rush to hedge the AI boom

Default insurance on Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta and Oracle debt is at its highest since 2021, with Oracle's spreads up more than a percentage point. Put volume on CoreWeave hit a record in October; popular 2028 contracts pay off if the stock falls to $35 from about $55. Higher hedging costs could raise financing costs for data-center builders.

US · earnings
+33%

Goldman: earnings are booming, but the acceleration is over

With 60% of the S&P 500 reported, third-quarter EPS is on track to rise 33% from a year earlier, matching Q2. Goldman expects growth of nearly 30% in 2026 slowing to about 18% in 2027, as AI spending matures and leadership moves away from big tech. It still expects a fourth straight year of double-digit index returns.

What the analysts are arguing about

Takeaways from contributor analysis and SA News. These are the authors' opinions, summarized here, and they disagree with each other.

Bull case · Alphabet
<20x

"Market misunderstanding is baffling"

  • Shares are more than 30% below their 2026 high and trade under 20x forward earnings.
  • Margins are still strong: group operating margin rose to 31.8% in Q3 and Google Cloud's to 36.2%.
  • The capex ramp may delay a free-cash-flow recovery, but the author sees that as temporary, not structural.
  • Risk: execution on the AI build-out is substantial; the author argues the backlog and diversified business justify the bet.

Bull case · memory
6.1x

SK hynix: sold out through 2029

  • DRAM and HBM volumes are effectively sold out through 2029, and long-term contracts cover more than half of 2027–28 capacity.
  • Record operating margins; net cash of KRW 98.7 trillion.
  • Trades at about 6.1x forward earnings, and management plans to return over 50% of free cash flow.
  • Risk: the market is focused on rising capex, which has weighed on the stock since results.
Cain Lee · 1/11 · rated BuyRead →
Framework · big tech
$800bn

Forget the Mag 7: payers vs. the paid

  • The author splits big tech into AI spenders ("MAGMA") and the suppliers they pay ("BATMANS").
  • The four main hyperscalers will spend over $800 billion this year; Nvidia, TSMC, Micron, Broadcom and AMD booked roughly $722 billion of revenue.
  • The hyperscalers' operating-cash-flow-to-capex ratio has fallen since 2016, and the author says it crossed a new line in Q3, which the author links to how the market treated their results.
  • The author backtested switching between the two groups and now watches one signal for the timing.
