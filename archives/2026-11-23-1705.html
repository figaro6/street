Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

Nvidia's board reversed course and brought Jensen Huang back as chief executive, and Wall Street exploded higher at the open. Nvidia jumped about 29%, the Nasdaq 100 6.6% and the S&P 500 4.1%, erasing Friday's rout within half an hour. In Tel Aviv, the Bank of Israel held its rate at 3.25%, and the shekel bounced almost 2% to below 3.30 per dollar. The TA-35 briefly turned higher on the Nvidia news but slipped back into the red heading into the close.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · Nvidia
+29%

Nvidia's board reinstates Huang, and the stock soars

After a series of meetings and input from senior US officials, the board brought Huang back as president and CEO with immediate effect. Trump praised the move and said it poses no security risk. Huang had left after admitting affairs, one with a woman suspected of spying for China, who Bloomberg says is now in Beijing.

Bloomberg · 23/11Read →
US · stocks
+6.6%

Friday's rout erased in the first half hour

The Nasdaq 100 jumped as much as 7% at the open, wiping out Friday's 5.6% fall, and the S&P gained more than 4%. AI-linked energy and industrial names rallied with chipmakers. Morgan Stanley's Mike Wilson called the sell-off the buying chance his team had waited for; Yardeni cut his S&P target again but sees no bear market (headline).

Bloomberg · FT · 23/11Read →
US · data
54.6

US business activity grows at its slowest in four months

S&P Global's flash composite PMI fell 1.3 points in November but stayed well in expansion. Price gauges eased yet remain consistent with inflation well above 2%, and supplier delays lengthened the most since 2022. Hiring stayed strong, pointing to another solid payroll gain: not what doves at the Fed want to see.

Bloomberg · 23/11Read →
US · strategy
Cautious

JPMorgan's traders doubt the bounce will last

Written before Huang's return, the desk led by Andrew Tyler dropped its tactically bullish call, admitting it misjudged Nvidia as the floor for tech. It expects a bounce but not a durable rally while rate risk clouds 2027–28 earnings. It would turn bullish again on lower volatility, cyclicals leading and a Fed that delays its hike.

Bloomberg · 23/11Read →
US · deals
$44bn

CrowdStrike agrees to buy Zscaler in all-stock deal

It could be the cyber-security industry's biggest takeover, coming after a wave of outages at Microsoft, the FAA, banks and airlines that experts link to rushed fixes of AI-found bugs. Bloomberg reports Wall Street is heading for a record M&A year despite the sell-off.

Financial Times · Bloomberg · 23/11Read →
US · IPOs
$90

Anthropic shares miss out on the AI rally

ANT flipped between gains and losses in early trade and sits about 6–8% above its $83 IPO price, lagging other AI names. Bloomberg notes its committed spending of nearly $901bn through 2045 makes the stock a referendum on the sector's ambitions; SpaceX also dipped below its IPO price weeks after listing.

Bloomberg · 23/11Read →
Israel · rates
3.25%

Bank of Israel holds despite 1.1% inflation

Governor Amir Yaron's committee kept the rate unchanged for a second meeting, as almost all economists expected. The bank flagged high geopolitical uncertainty and the shekel's sharp fall, which could push up import prices. Hapoalim and Leader see no cut soon, as Fed and ECB hikes would widen the rate gap.

Globes · Calcalist · 23/11Read →
Israel · shekel
3.29

Shekel rebounds as Wall Street surges

The dollar fell below 3.30 shekels after the rate decision and the Nvidia news, from a high of 3.36 last week, the weakest shekel since September 2025. Calcalist explains the link: when US stocks rise, Israeli pension funds' dollar exposure overshoots its target, so they sell dollars, and the reverse happened in last week's rout.

Calcalist · Bloomberg · 23/11Read →
Israel · tech deals
$7.5bn+

Amazon confirms its purchase of Israel's Decart

Amazon will buy most of Decart through Annapurna, its chip arm, to make AI run more efficiently on its Trainium and Inferentia processors. The deal is in shares and tied to five-year targets; the three 8200-veteran founders will stay five years. Annapurna itself was an Israeli start-up bought for $375m a decade ago.

Globes · Bloomberg · 23/11Read →
Israel · TASE
−0.3%

Tel Aviv fails to hold on to the Nvidia bounce

The TA-35 turned positive after the Huang news, but by 16:45 it was back below Friday's close, weighed by real estate and small caps (Globes' SME60 −1.1%). Nvidia's Israeli unit produces about 18% of its revenue, so the reversal matters locally. Harel's quarterly profit was dented by market losses (headline).

Energy & metals
$90.75

Oil slides 2% while gold jumps almost 4%

Brent fell to about $90.75 and US gas lost 7%, even as Calcalist cites analysts' worries about tankers hit near Oman. Gold rose 3.7% to around $3,670, extending its rebound from its worst week in eight months. Congo, the top cobalt miner, plans to ban exports again as prices fall (headline).

+3.5%

Bitcoin climbs with stocks after a $1.8bn ETF exodus

Bitcoin rose to about $63,000 and Ether to $1,776 as risk appetite returned. Bloomberg reports $1.8bn of outflows from bitcoin ETFs removed a key source of support, and that Strategy's market value now trails its bitcoin hoard, though its shares jumped about 10% today (headlines).

Financial Times · key reads

FT · Nvidia
+30%

Huang's return reignites the AI trade

The FT's markets team reports the Nasdaq 100 jumped 7% soon after the open, reversing Friday. Strategists at Raymond James and Marshall Wace call the reversal a real surprise to consensus, noting few companies depend so much on one person, and expect it to ease some of the AI panic.

Financial Times · 23/11Read →
FT · AI finance
Circular

The problem with the circular AI economy

The columnist examines Nvidia's loans and equity stakes in customers that buy its chips. This vendor financing creates demand but also layers of linked fragility, and blurs real demand with speculation. The risk: fewer chip orders mean less financing, which could ripple through the whole AI ecosystem.

Financial Times · 23/11Read →
FT · opinion
50–70%

What happens when bubbles burst

Absolute Strategy Research's co-founder argues AI stocks meet every classic bubble test, and history says bubble leaders fall 50–70% within two years. Part of that has already happened: CoreWeave is down 70% from its peak, Intel 61%, Oracle 56% and Micron 50%. The past 50 years suggest more weakness may come.
