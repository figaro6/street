Good night, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street closed at its lowest since July: the S&P 500 fell 1.0% and the Dow 1.6%, as a new round of tech outages, this time at the NSA and Apple, added to AI nerves. Then, minutes after the bell, Nvidia delivered relief. Its first report since Jensen Huang's ouster beat on revenue ($114.8bn) and guided the next quarter to about $130bn, well above the $125bn expected, and the stock rose about 4% after hours. Treasuries rallied as a haven, the 10-year back down to 5.11%. In Jerusalem, Eisenkot's camp won the Knesset speakership with 66 votes; Tel Aviv itself ended flat, with Elbit up 6.5%.

Number of the day
$130bn

Nvidia's revenue guide for next quarter, beating the $125bn forecast

In its first report since Jensen Huang's ouster, Nvidia guided fiscal fourth-quarter revenue to $130bn, plus or minus 3%, against an average estimate of $125.3bn, with gross margin around 71.5%. Third-quarter revenue was $114.8bn against $109.1bn expected, with $107.6bn from data centres, and EPS of $2.68 beat $2.48. The shares, which fell 0.4% in regular trade to end a four-day, 11% slide, rose about 4% after hours, lifting CoreWeave, Nebius, AMD and Broadcom. The release did not mention Huang or the CEO question; that is left for the call.

The stories behind the numbers

US · earnings
$130bn

Nvidia beats and raises in its first post-Huang report

Nvidia guided next-quarter revenue to $130bn, about $5bn above estimates, after a third quarter of $114.8bn, with hyperscalers driving the upside. Bloomberg Intelligence notes AI-cloud, industrial and enterprise sales slightly missed, and the release did not reaffirm the 70% growth target for fiscal 2028. Supply commitments, mostly multi-year memory deals, rose to $390bn. The stock rose about 4% after hours.

−1.0%

Outages and AI doubts push the S&P to a July low

The S&P 500 fell 1% and the Dow 1.6% as another round of technical outages hit the NSA's classified network and Apple's internal tools; both denied a cyber-attack. A Bank of America survey found fund managers' net overweight in tech fell to 12% from 37%, the biggest cut since 2006. Fed officials leaned hawkish: Musalem backed several hikes, while Williams ruled out "dramatic action".

SEC

SEC opens a probe of Nvidia's disclosures

The Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia, including whether its disclosures misled investors, the Wall Street Journal reported. Huang said he would be honoured to return as CEO, but the board is reportedly not considering it; he has agreed to testify to Congress next month. US officials suspect the woman at the centre of the scandal was a Chinese intelligence asset; Beijing denies spying.

Israel · politics
66

Kahana elected Knesset speaker with 66 votes

Matan Kahana of Eisenkot's party won the speakership late tonight with 66 votes from the change bloc and Arab parties, against 52 for Likud's Ofir Katz, after a nearly seven-hour filibuster. In exchange for cutting the debate short, new committees to reverse the judicial overhaul laws will start work only next Monday. United Torah Judaism voted for Katz but says coalition talks with Eisenkot continue.

Financial Times · key reads

FT · Nvidia
$104bn

Nvidia stopped disclosing Singapore sales as they soared

Nvidia's Singapore subsidiary booked $104bn of revenue in 2025, up 72%, close to half the group total, according to local accounts the FT has seen. Nvidia changed its reporting method last year, so Singapore no longer appears separately. Most of the business is invoicing for chips shipped elsewhere, but US authorities are probing whether Singapore firms, including Megaspeed, helped smuggle chips to China.

Financial Times · 17/11Read →
FT · currencies
+1.7%

The dollar has erased its "liberation day" losses

The dollar index is up 1.7% this month, back to where it stood before Trump's April 2025 tariff shock, and the euro briefly fell below $1.10. Traders now price another Fed hike in December and two more next year, after a hot 0.4% October CPI and Waller floating a half-point move. Strategists call it a macro-driven rally, helped by falling energy prices that limit hikes elsewhere.

Financial Times · 17/11Read →
FT · Opinion
80%

Ruchir Sharma: the whole world is long America

Some 80% of global equity flows this year have gone into US stocks, double the long-run average, much of it from retail investors at home and abroad. US households own over 40% of the market and foreigners nearly 20%, while hedge funds hold barely 1%. Money has rotated out of frothy AI names but not out of America, so when the love affair ends, the fallout will be global.

FT · banks
15mn

Deutsche Bank leak exposes systemic failures

Whistleblowers leaked more than 15mn internal Deutsche Bank documents. The FT says they show bad conduct, broken reform promises and client secrets, and portray CEO Christian Sewing pushing for more risk and railing at compliance while keeping some problems from shareholders. The investigation also points to accounting tricks. The shares fell about 6% on Monday and another 3% today.
