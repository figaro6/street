Tuesday 3 Nov 2026 · 23:40 Tel Aviv
Night edition · Wall Street close 23:00, AMD after hours, TASE close 17:25

Good evening, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street closed mixed on US election day: chip stocks rebounded to lift the S&P 500 0.4% and the Nasdaq 0.8% while the Dow fell 0.4% and Treasury yields rose about 5 basis points. After the bell AMD beat on data-center sales and Super Micro gave a strong forecast. For Israel the shock of the day came from Washington: President Trump said US military aid, about $4 billion a year, will end at the close of 2026. The shekel slid to about 3.17 per dollar, its weakest since late March, while Tel Aviv closed slightly higher and Nice fell 7%.

Key numbers

Night: chips lift Wall Street, shekel at a seven-month low

Number of the day
$4bn

Trump says US military aid to Israel will stop at the end of 2026

In a Fox News interview on election morning, Trump said Israel "no longer needed" the aid and claimed he and Prime Minister Netanyahu agreed months ago to end it. About $4 billion a year flows under a ten-year agreement that runs to 2028, and Congress appropriates the money, so it is unclear whether he can stop it alone. Calcalist reports the news surprised Israel's Treasury, which had hoped for a gradual phase-out and has committed to buy about $25 billion of US equipment over the coming decade. The dollar climbed to about 3.17 shekels the same day, though FX desks did not tie the move only to this.

US · close
+0.4%

An AI rebound lifts the S&P 500 while most members fall

Chipmakers halted a selloff driven by doubts about AI spending, which lifted the index even though most of its stocks fell. Strategists note the S&P 500 has never been lower 12 months after a midterm election since 1950. Edward Jones calls the recent rotation a reassessment of the price investors will pay for AI rather than a rejection of it.

$13.38bn

AMD beats on data center; Super Micro lifts its forecast

AMD reported adjusted earnings of $2.09 a share, $0.15 above forecasts, on revenue of $13.38 billion, up 45%, with data-center sales doubling to $8.5 billion. Seeking Alpha headlines say the shares popped after the bell, though the move size was not captured. Super Micro also rallied on a strong AI data-center outlook. Both help explain the chip rebound.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 3 November

  • MidtermsVoting is under way for all 435 House seats and a third of the Senate. The Economist model gives Democrats a 93% chance of flipping the House and 52% for the Senate. Results are expected from 6pm Eastern.
  • Israel aidTrump said America will stop sending aid to Israel at the end of the year and that he and Netanyahu "made a deal months ago". No details were given; the existing agreement provides roughly $4bn a year until 2028. Netanyahu recently lost his parliamentary majority.

  • Britain and EULondon and Brussels struck a provisional deal to remove post-Brexit checks on food and farm exports, ahead of a meeting of diplomats on Wednesday and a summit later this month.
  • UkraineAmerica arranged Russia–Ukraine–US talks in Abu Dhabi next week, the first trilateral meeting in nine months. Russian strikes killed 33 people at an aid-distribution centre.
  • Air travelSeveral European flights received bomb threats; a plane with 181 passengers from London to Warsaw diverted to Modlin. It is not clear whether the threats are linked.
