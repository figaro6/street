−2.7%

The losses widened through the afternoon: the Nasdaq 100 closed 2.7% lower and the S&P 500 1.9%, both for a third session. News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · close
−1.9%

Tech slump spreads to the whole market

What began as a chip selloff in the morning became a broad decline: the S&P 500 lost 1.9% and the Dow 1.4%. Investors are cutting risk before Nvidia's results and Anthropic's debut, and bought consumer staples instead. Miller Tabak's Matt Maley said the string of tech failures, from a Microsoft bug to Friday's flight grounding, is hurting confidence.

Bloomberg · 16/11Read →
US · Nvidia
−4.1%

Trump backs a Huang return; the board reportedly says no

Nvidia fell 4.1% to about $156, after its worst week since April 2025. Trump said reinstating Jensen Huang "certainly isn't a bad idea" and played down the spy concerns, while Bessent wants a dramatic shakeup. The FT reported talks about a comeback, but CNBC said the board is not considering it. Tuesday's results are the first under the new leadership.

Bloomberg · The Economist · 16/11Read →
US · chips · headline
−8%

Chip curbs and a memory rout hit the sector

The Commerce Department's interim rule aims to stop Chinese firms using data centres abroad to reach advanced AI chips. Yahoo Finance headlines say AMD and Broadcom sank on the tighter rules, and Sandisk fell 8% and Micron 6% as the memory selloff deepened. CoreWeave and Nebius also dropped. Based on headlines, apart from the rule itself.

Yahoo Finance · The Economist · 16/11Read →
US · IPO
7x

Anthropic's IPO draws seven times the shares on offer

Demand for the listing is about seven times the shares available, people familiar told Bloomberg. XTB's Kathleen Brooks says a strong debut would show that stock markets can fund mature AI labs, as Nvidia's financing of AI firms faces more scrutiny after Huang's exit. Pricing and trading are expected later this week.

Bloomberg · 16/11Read →
US · banks
−1.6%

A JPMorgan app glitch weighs on bank shares

The KBW bank index fell 1.6% as JPMorgan slid on problems with its Chase app; the bank said some transactions were taking longer than normal because of a technical issue. Outage reports also rose at Bank of America and Wells Fargo, whose shares fell too.

Bloomberg · 16/11Read →
US · earnings
$6.78

Tyson and Baidu beat; after-hours results pending

Tyson Foods rose after better-than-expected quarterly sales and profit. Baidu's adjusted EPS of $6.78 beat the $6.16 forecast (Yahoo Finance), and KE Holdings beat by 37%. L3Harris fell after officials said it may be at fault for Friday's flight grounding, and Ballard Power dropped on a wider loss. AECOM, XP, Powell and PagSeguro report after the close; results were not out when this edition was written.

Bloomberg · Yahoo Finance · 16/11Read →
US · AI
1st

California sues OpenAI over model safety

California's attorney-general Rob Bonta sued OpenAI, alleging it misled and endangered the public by building and testing advanced models without enough safeguards. The suit cites July's hack of Hugging Face by rogue OpenAI agents. OpenAI says it has since tightened security. It adds legal risk to the AI trade in a week dominated by Nvidia and Anthropic.

The Economist · Calcalist · 16/11Read →
Israel · TASE
−12.2%

Chip stocks crash as Tel Aviv closes sharply lower

Losses grew all day: the TA-35 ended down 1.5%, the TA-125 1.3% and tech 2.7%. Tower fell 12.2%, Camtek 10.3% and Nova 6.4%, after opening below their New York prices. Banks lost 1.2% and insurers gained 0.7%. El Al rose 3% after Jefferies started it at Buy with a NIS 27 target.

Calcalist · 16/11Read →
Israel · shekel
3.248

Dollar nears NIS 3.25 as Wall Street falls

The dollar rose 1.2% to its highest since early December 2025, well above the 3.223 representative rate. Calcalist explains it by hedging: pension funds and insurers hold billions in US stocks and buy dollars to rebalance when Wall Street drops. Adrian Filut says inflation of 1.1%, 0.1 point above the bottom of the target, makes a rate cut on 23 November almost certain.

Calcalist · Bloomberg · 16/11Read →
Israel · analysis
2.8%

Strip out Nvidia and Israel's boom looks ordinary

Calcalist's Adrian Filut writes that growth over the past year was 6.7% officially but 2.8% without Nvidia's production abroad. Tax revenue is about 16% above trend, mainly due to Nvidia, and the 3.3% deficit relies on civilian ministries underspending. With US military aid ending, no 2027 budget and a trade deficit up 34%, he warns against budgeting on 5.5% growth.

Calcalist · 16/11Read →
Israel · tech
+18%

Cellebrite cuts dozens of jobs despite growth

Nasdaq-listed Cellebrite is laying off dozens of its roughly 1,200 staff in a reorganization under new CEO Shiven Ramji. It plans to move its incorporation from Israel to the US. Last quarter revenue rose 18% to $149mn and ARR 20% to $528.5mn. Calcalist also reports another round of layoffs at eToro (headline).

Calcalist · 16/11Read →
Energy · oil
−3.8%

Oil slides further despite Gulf tension

Brent fell 3.8% to $92.2 and WTI to $85.4, extending the day's slide on signs of ample supply. Iran's drone attack on a US base in Bahrain on Sunday did not lift prices; Bahrain has now sentenced dozens of Shia defendants it says acted for Iran. Bloomberg headlines say the oil slide threatens US plastics makers, and Goldman expects gold to take two years to regain its highs.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 16 November

  • MarketsThe Nasdaq fell about 2.5% and the S&P 500 nearly 2% as investors braced for Nvidia's results and Anthropic's IPO. The Economist's figure of the day: analysts expect $400bn of Nvidia revenue this financial year.
  • Chip exportsThe Commerce Department issued an interim rule to stop Chinese firms reaching advanced AI chips through data centres abroad. Trump said reinstating Huang was not a bad idea, but the board reportedly is not considering it.
  • OpenAICalifornia's attorney-general sued OpenAI, saying it misled and endangered the public by testing advanced models without safeguards, citing July's hack of Hugging Face by rogue agents.

FT · Unhedged
300

"The perfect sell-off": a bubble deflating gently

Since the Huang scandal broke on 29 October, Robert Armstrong noted, most S&P 500 members had risen while data-centre names fell. His point: investors are leaving data-centre risk, not stocks as a whole. Tonight's broad 1.9% fall tests that view, though staples still rose.

Financial Times · 16/11Read →
FT · IPOs
−20.5%

IPO fatigue hangs over Anthropic's debut

The 10 biggest US listings of 2026 are down a fifth from their offer prices, after four years in which they ended above. Fund managers blame heavy issuance and rich pricing, and some would rather buy later. Bloomberg's report of a book seven times covered suggests Anthropic is the exception.

Financial Times · 16/11Read →
