Good morning, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street had its best day in weeks on Wednesday: the Nasdaq 100 jumped 2.3% and the S&P 500 1.3% as CoreWeave soared 25% and the AI trade came back. Then, after the bell, Nvidia's board met in urgent session to weigh ousting Jensen Huang after a second relationship came to light, and the shares fell about 8.6% in late trading. Nasdaq futures are down 1% this morning and Asian chip stocks are lower. Tel Aviv closed 1% down on Wednesday as a weak First International report dragged the banks 3.6% lower.

The AI trade roars back on Wednesday

The Nasdaq 100 rose 2.3% and the S&P 500 1.3%, recouping nearly all of last week's post-midterm rout. CoreWeave's results, OpenAI's quarterly revenue reaching $10bn and reports that Anthropic's IPO is several times oversubscribed lifted tech. +24.7%

CoreWeave's best day in months

CoreWeave closed at $60.77, up 24.7%, after Tuesday's beat-and-raise and a $132.5bn order backlog. The stock had halved since the summer on doubts about its debt-funded AI build-out. Bloomberg headlines say it now plans to raise at least $4bn in a high-grade loan. Bitcoin miners that have turned into AI cloud providers also jumped, per Seeking Alpha (headline).

FT · Nvidia
$4.2tn

Nvidia's board must now decide Huang's fate

The FT reports the board held urgent talks after news of Huang's relationship with Kimberly Powell, an 18-year Nvidia veteran. Options range from asking him to step back during the probe to an immediate exit. His departure would hit far beyond Nvidia, since its investments and guarantees sit at the centre of AI financing. Elon Musk said hiring Huang "would be an honor."

Financial Times · 11/11Read →
FT · Lex
$1.4tn

What losing its CEO could cost Nvidia

Lex runs a rough sum: if Nvidia's GPU share fell from 72% to 50% and its gross margin from 73% to 65%, 2030 gross profit would be about $300bn lower. At today's multiple that is $1.4tn, or 30% of its pre-scandal value. A 10% fall so far implies the market saw about a one-in-three chance of the worst case. That was before Wednesday night's news.

Financial Times · 10/11, still driving marketsRead →
FT · Oil
10.6mn b/d

Saudi oil output rebounds close to prewar levels

Saudi Arabia told Opec it pumped 10.6mn barrels a day in October, its highest since the US-Israel war on Iran began. It shipped 7.9mn b/d of crude and products through both Yanbu on the Red Sea and its Gulf ports, per Kpler, despite Houthi strikes on airports and Aramco sites. The UAE, now outside Opec, produced over 4mn b/d.

Sources and method
