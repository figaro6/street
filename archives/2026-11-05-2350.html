Thursday 5 Nov 2026 · 23:50 Tel Aviv
Night edition · Wall Street close 23:00 · Tel Aviv close 17:30

Good night, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street closed in a rout: the Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4%, its worst day since March 2020, the S&P 500 4.4% and the Dow 3.4%, wiping out Wednesday's post-election rally on fears that a Democratic Congress will slow the AI build-out. The VIX jumped 70% to 28, Treasuries rallied and bitcoin dropped to $75.9k. Tel Aviv fell 3.7% with defence stocks down 7%, and the shekel slid to about 3.20 per dollar. Friday's US jobs report, due at 15:30 Tel Aviv time, is now a sideshow.

Edition focus

Wall Street's close, after-hours, Tel Aviv, tomorrow

How Wall Street closed

  • Nasdaq 100 −6.4%, S&P 500 −4.4%, Dow −3.4%: worst day for the Nasdaq 100 since March 2020 (Bloomberg).
  • Chip gauge −12%; Micron −17%, Qualcomm and Arm hit by weak outlooks, Tesla and SpaceX down about 10%.
  • VIX 28.2, up 70%; the Economist calls it the S&P's worst day since April 2025.
  • Treasuries rallied: 10-year 4.84%, 2-year 4.45%.

After-hours and earnings

  • Airbnb, Cloudflare and Akamai reported after the close; Bloomberg lists Akamai, Airbnb and Sweetgreen as movers, but their results were not read.
  • Monster Beverage slipped on margin pressure despite $2.56bn revenue, a $70m beat (Seeking Alpha).
  • Grindr lifted its outlook and expects to meet 2027 targets early (Bloomberg).
  • Futures are deeply negative: S&P −4.7%, Nasdaq −6.8%.

Tel Aviv and tomorrow

  • TA-35 −3.69% on NIS 19.4bn turnover, inflated by the index revision; defence −7.09% (Globes).
  • Shekel at a seven-month low; Bank of Israel rate 3.1670.
  • Friday: TASE closes 13:50; US jobs report 15:30.
  • Globes asks whether it is time to buy the dip; IBI's fund chief says multiples, not politics, make TASE unattractive.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 5 November

  • MarketsUS shares skidded again: the S&P 500 lost nearly 4.4%, its worst day since the April 2025 tariff shock, and the Nasdaq 100 fell 6.3%. Investors first took the Democratic win in stride, then worried it would slow America's AI build-out.
  • Iran / HormuzIran said two tankers and a tugboat were damaged in the Strait of Hormuz, with at least five incidents since Sunday, mostly from mines. Oil dipped on bets that Trump, chastened by the midterms, will not restart retaliatory strikes.
  • ParaguayPresident Santiago Peña resigned after depositors were frozen out of digital bank Ueno, which collapsed amid bookkeeping allegations. Opposition parties were preparing to impeach him.
  • Hong KongA court sentenced activist Joshua Wong to ten years under the national-security law, on top of the six he has already served.
  • FranceRiot police fired tear gas in Lyon as unions and students held nationwide protests and a public-sector strike over the cost of living. Up to 700,000 are expected outside Paris, five months before a presidential election.
  • Air travelAt least 21 European flights were disrupted by bomb threats, including a Wizz Air jet diverted to Vienna; no explosives were found in earlier cases and Britain called them malicious hoaxes.
  • Figure of the day$31bn: Saudi Aramco's adjusted net profit last quarter, showing how it is weathering the Iran war.

Micron: when a 3.8x forward 2028 P/E is too expensive
