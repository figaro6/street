Good morning, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street ended a brutal week with a tepid Friday rebound: the Nasdaq 100 gained 1.35% but the Dow fell 0.5%, after Thursday's tech rout that the FT says cost $3.2tn. The FT's Katie Martin argues the trigger was political: voters in the midterms backed Democrats and rejected AI data centres, so the debt-fuelled build-out now faces tougher permits. Strong payrolls keep a December Fed hike alive, while Trump is keeping Iran guessing and Israel's Gadi Eisenkot is expected to get the mandate to form a government this weekend. It is Saturday, so markets are closed until Monday.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · AI rout
$3.2tn

Tech rout, then a tepid rebound

The FT reports Thursday's selloff wiped $3.2tn off US stocks on AI fears, and Friday's recovery was modest. Katie Martin counts it as the third recent shakeout and says the same question keeps returning: is this the big one? Bloomberg's headline puts the week's damage at $5.7tn and says options volumes hit records. Treasuries have turned higher as investors seek a haven.

US · politics & AI
$600m

Midterms turn AI donors into a liability

The FT says Democrats won both chambers, some on anti-AI platforms, and promise hearings on tech billionaires from January. Republican groups took over $600m from tech donors, and activists now urge the party to distance itself from Musk, Huang and David Sacks. Texas elected a Democratic governor who wants a data-centre moratorium; SpaceX and Tesla together lost $420bn of value since the vote.

US · Nvidia
18 Nov

Congress summons Huang over China spy claims

Three House committee chairs asked Nvidia's chief executive to attend a closed briefing on 18 November, after a New York Times report that US intelligence thinks he was targeted by Chinese espionage. Nvidia denies wrongdoing and says its internal probe found no chip diversion. The FT home page adds, as a headline only, that US officials believe Huang had a relationship with an alleged spy.

Eisenkot expected to get the mandate

FT · AI markets
Third

Is the AI stock bubble finally bursting?

Katie Martin says tech was hit hard for the third time recently, with a modest stabilisation on Friday. She cites the BIS finding that nearly 29% of AI firms' deals are with other AI firms, and warns circular financing can become a firing squad. The trigger, she argues, was the midterm message against data centres. TS Lombard's worst case is a 30% fall in US stocks and up to $200bn of credit losses.

Financial Times · 6/11Read →
FT · politics & tech
$420bn

America turns on Trump's tech cheerleaders

Silicon Valley is rethinking its politics after its alliance with Trump cost Republicans dearly. Democrats vow hearings in January, a probe of Musk and even a pause on advanced AI. Brockman dropped a $25m pledge, and Palantir, Meta and OpenAI hired Democratic-linked lobbyists. Executives say they will hedge, since voters could turn on Democrats in two years.

Financial Times · 6/11Read →
FT · Nvidia
Nov 18

House panels summon Nvidia's Huang

Republican chairs want Huang at a closed briefing on 18 November over Chinese spying. The concerns centre on Megaspeed, a Singapore cloud firm that bought billions of dollars of Nvidia chips. Nvidia says customers get only ordinary business information, and its own inspection found no diversion of chips to China.

US tells China to meet its Busan commitments

Bessent and Greer told China's He Lifeng on a Friday call to implement the Busan truce fully and immediately, citing rare-earth exports and farm purchases. The truce was extended to January 2027. The warning comes about two weeks before an expected Trump-Xi meeting at the APEC summit. Based on the article text read in the previous edition; the story still leads the FT home page.
