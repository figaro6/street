Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

The AI unwind turned into a rout at the New York open. The Nasdaq 100 fell about 3.4% in the first half hour, close to a 20% drop from its October record that would mark a bear market, and the VIX jumped above 34. Core PCE inflation at 15:30 came in at 3.3%, which keeps a December Fed hike in play, yet investors still bought Treasuries: the 10-year yield fell to 4.84%, down for an eighth straight day. Washington also proposed new tariffs of up to 20% on 14 trading partners. In Tel Aviv the TA-35 lost 1.6% as chip and tech stocks fell, and the dollar rose above NIS 3.37, its highest since September 2025.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · markets
−4.7%

Nasdaq 100 slides toward a bear market

The index fell as much as 4.7% early on Wednesday, its second steep loss in a row, extending its drop from the early-October record to about 20%. Bloomberg's wrap describes investors unwinding leveraged risk and running to bonds before the Thanksgiving break. Goldman's head of asset allocation research said it is too early to buy: the market needs proof that AI spending earns real profits.

Bloomberg · 25/11Read →
US · inflation
3.3%

Core PCE firm, but bonds rally anyway

The Fed's preferred gauge, core PCE, rose 0.3% in October and 3.3% from a year earlier, while headline inflation was 3.8%, far above target. That keeps the Fed on course to raise rates on 9 December; Calcalist cites a 97% market probability. Even so, Treasury yields fell across the curve as the equity sell-off drove a dash for safety.

Bloomberg · Calcalist · 25/11Read →
US · trade
20%

Trump proposes new tariffs on 14 partners

The new levies replace the "liberation day" tariffs the Supreme Court struck down. China and Vietnam face up to 20%, five Asian exporters 18%, and the EU, Japan, Korea and Taiwan 15%; China's rate could double if Washington decides it broke the trade truce, which expires on 10 January. Brazil was hit with an extra 10% on aircraft, and Embraer fell almost 10% in São Paulo.

Financial Times · Bloomberg · 25/11Read →
US · chips & AI credit
−9%

Nvidia's Huang rally is fully reversed

Nvidia fell about 9% early, Micron 11% and Broadcom 7%, per the FT; Nvidia is down about 35% from its October peak, some $2trn of value. Huang apologised to staff and said he is in daily therapy. In credit, CDS spreads on Oracle and CoreWeave hit record highs, CoreWeave bonds fell to all-time lows, and the WSJ reports bond investors expect Anthropic's debt to be rated junk.

Tax Authority seeks billions more from Wiz founders

The Israel Tax Authority wants Wiz's four founders to pay about NIS 20bn more on their share of Google's $32bn takeover, covering cash proceeds, options and intellectual property. They have paid about NIS 8bn so far and offer perhaps half the demand; Globes' sources expect a deal at NIS 12–13bn. Without one, an assessment and a court fight could follow.

Refiners boom; insurers' profits shrink

Bazan earned more than NIS 1bn in the third quarter on a refining margin of $32.5 a barrel. Norstar sells shares at a deep discount

After a failed convertible bond issue, Chaim Katzman's Norstar, which controls G City, is raising NIS 80m at a 60% discount, with free warrants that cut the effective price to NIS 0.36 a share. The shares fell more than 50%, to a market value of about NIS 155m. Its Series 13 bonds yield about 20% with less than a year to maturity.

Judges urge a rethink of Netanyahu's cases

The three judges suggested the prosecution drop the bribery charge in Case 4000, reconsider Case 2000 and seek a plea deal in Case 1000, saying the public interest required it now that Netanyahu has testified. Separately, Gadi Eisenkot won Yisrael Beiteinu's backing in his coalition talks, but is still 13 seats short of a majority before his 6 December deadline.

Israel · shekel
3.38

Dollar climbs to a 14-month high

The dollar rose about 1% to NIS 3.378, its highest since September 2025, as global investors sold risk and US stocks, a big share of Israeli institutions' portfolios, fell. ING says an unwind of emerging-market currency trades is under way, and Bloomberg reports the global carry trade is losing its reliability. A weaker shekel is the reason the Bank of Israel gives for keeping rates on hold.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 25 November

  • TariffsAmerica proposed duties of up to 20% on goods from China, the EU, Japan, South Korea, India and others, to replace the tariffs the Supreme Court struck down. They follow trade-practice probes into 16 countries.
  • NvidiaJensen Huang apologised to staff for breaching the code of conduct and said he attends daily therapy, but denied he was reinstated under pressure from Donald Trump. He also rejected succession planning.
  • IsraelGadi Eisenkot unexpectedly won the backing of Yisrael Beiteinu's seven seats for a coalition that will probably depend on Arab parties. He is still 13 seats short of a majority.
  • HuaweiA New York jury convicted the Chinese firm on ten counts, including trade-secret theft and dodging Iran sanctions; it was acquitted of racketeering and denies wrongdoing.
  • AirlinesEasyJet's annual profit fell 96% to £24m as Middle East fighting raised jet-fuel costs and hurt demand. It will cut 1.3m winter seats before its £5.7bn buyout by Apollo.
  • BangladeshThe government offered garment workers a 36% rise in the minimum wage after violent protests shut factories. The sector makes up about 90% of exports.

The AI unwind accelerates on Wall Street

The FT reports the Nasdaq 100 down more than 4% as the AI trade unwinds, with Nvidia off about 9% after Monday's surge. A Russell Investments strategist says the mood is very skittish and expects closer scrutiny of the whole AI ecosystem. Anthropic slipped another 9% to just under $75.

FT · IPOs
$83

How Anthropic's bankers overplayed the IPO

The column argues the deal was pushed too far: priced above its $70–78 range, upsized 19%, with 315m insider shares free to sell on day one and the greenshoe used at once, leaving no buffer to support the stock. It closed below its $83 issue price on Tuesday. The bigger loser may be OpenAI, whose own IPO now looks harder.

Financial Times · 25/11Read →
FT · AI capex
3.2%

Is AI bigger than the railway boom?

A Columbia paper projects the US AI build-out at 3.2% of GDP a year for eight years, above the 1870–90 railroad boom's 2.2% a year. The columnist cautions that 2032 spending is a guess, eight years against 21 is not a fair comparison, and Britain's 1840s railway mania was bigger still. Booms leave surplus assets behind.

Trump drops business deals as bait for Russia

Kushner and Witkoff have told European officials that corporate tie-ups, such as US money for Russian oil and titanium projects or restarting gas sales to Europe, are no longer central to the US peace pitch, after the Kremlin showed little interest. Talks now focus on territory and security guarantees.
