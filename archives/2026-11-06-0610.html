Good morning, Tel Aviv.

The AI sell-off has gone global. After Wall Street's worst day since April 2025, Japan's Nikkei fell as much as 5.7% and South Korea's Kospi plunged 8.8%, triggering a circuit breaker, before regulators stepped in and US futures steadied: S&P futures are flat and Nasdaq futures up 0.9%. The chip index is down almost 16% this week. Brent is back at $100, gold has slipped, and bitcoin is stuck near $75.7k. Today's US jobs report (15:30 Tel Aviv) is expected to be weak, and Tel Aviv, after its 3.7% fall, opens a short Friday session into this mood.

Edition focus

Last night's close, Asia overnight, what to watch today

How Wall Street closed

  • S&P 500 −4.4%, Nasdaq 100 −6.4%, Dow −3.4%; the FT puts the loss at $3.2tn, the biggest in US stocks this century.
  • Chip index −12% on the day; Micron −17%, Tesla about −11%, Nvidia −9%.
  • VIX closed at 28.2, up 70%.
  • Treasuries rallied on the day; the 10-year is back at 4.84%.

After-hours and Asia

  • Nasdaq futures +0.9% after Asia's lows; Seoul waives a 140% margin-collateral rule and Taipei is expected to announce support this afternoon.
  • Doximity −11% despite a beat and raised guidance; SoundHound −7.5% on losses; Axon fell after Q3 on drone delays (Seeking Alpha).
  • Hedge fund losses mount: Walleye, Kirkoswald, Arini; Samlyn exits all stocks but financials.

  • Whether Democrats soften the data-centre moratorium talk.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

Asia · overnight
−8.8%

Asia's AI rout: Kospi triggers circuit breaker

Japan's Nikkei fell as much as 5.7%, its biggest drop since April 2025, and the Kospi slid 8.8% at the low before a circuit breaker. Samsung and SK Hynix fell more than 4%, and Taiwan's index fell 4.9%. Seoul will waive its 140% collateral rule on margin loans to curb forced selling, and Taiwan's regulator is expected to announce support this afternoon. Korea's market has lost about $1.7tn in the rout.

US · politics & AI
40%

Data-centre politics drive the sell-off

Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa wants an immediate moratorium on data-centre projects. Cleanview data cited by Bloomberg shows about 40% of pending US capacity is in states whose new governors promised to pause building. Microsoft chose not to extend a Texas contract with Nscale, and Oracle's $165bn New Mexico complex may slip again. A Lombard Odier CIO says some Democratic comments are already being dialled back.

US · desks
Wrong-footed

Wall Street's bullish election call backfires

Trading desks at JPMorgan, Citadel Securities and UBS had told clients for weeks that the rally would resume once the midterms were over. Thursday's sell-off reversed that call. Goldman says its equity desk was flooded with calls and buyers were "nowhere to be found". Walleye is returning cash after its worst month since 2020, and Samlyn has dumped everything except financials.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 6 November

  • MarketsThe S&P 500 lost nearly 4.4% on Thursday, its worst day since April 2025's tariff shock, and the Nasdaq 100 fell 6.3%. Investors first shrugged off the Democratic win, then feared a slowdown in America's AI build-out.
  • ParaguayPresident Santiago Peña resigned "for the stability of our country" after digital bank Ueno collapsed over book-keeping allegations and depositors were frozen out. He held shares in a related company until last year, and the opposition was preparing to impeach him.
  • Hong KongA court sentenced activist Joshua Wong to ten years under the national-security law, on top of the six he has already served. He had been due for release in two months.
  • FranceRiot police fired tear gas in Lyon as unions and students protested over living costs, with up to 700,000 expected to march outside Paris. It comes five months before a presidential election.
  • Iran / HormuzIran says two tankers and a tugboat were damaged in the strait, with at least five incidents since Sunday, mostly mines. Oil dipped on bets that a chastened Trump will not resume strikes.
  • Air travelAt least 21 European flights were disrupted by bomb threats on Thursday, after about seven earlier in the week. No explosives were found, and Britain called the threats malicious hoaxes.
  • Figure of the day42%: how much American rents have risen since 2019, with the Economist asking whether a "renters' recession" is under way. Separately, Blue Origin delayed its New Glenn launch to January.

Don't mistake an AI slowdown for an AI crash
