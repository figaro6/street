News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · AI rout
$5.7tn

Wall Street grapples with the AI unwind

Bloomberg says US equities lost $5.7tn from their 6 October peak by Thursday's close. Hedge funds and systematic strategies added to the selling, and retail traders sold at a pace rarely seen since the pandemic. BlackRock's Rick Rieder expects AI spending to proceed "at a more deliberate pace". Morgan Stanley sees leverage cuts nearing exhaustion. Bloomberg's $5.7tn is the loss since the peak; the FT's $3.2tn is Thursday alone.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 7 November

  • IsraelThe president gave Gadi Eisenkot first go at forming a government after a majority of parliament backed him over Binyamin Netanyahu. Neither bloc has an outright majority, so a deal will be hard. He has up to six weeks before another candidate can try.
  • PolandA gunman killed at least six people and wounded at least 15 in a central square in Kraków. Police shot the attacker, a Polish citizen, who was taken to hospital.
  • SenateTwo races are still uncalled. In Alaska, Mary Peltola leads Dan Sullivan by 0.3 points and has sued for access to rejected ballots; in Mississippi the Democrat leads by 2,574 votes with 97% counted.
  • New ZealandThe National Party won the most seats but no majority; Labour's Chris Hipkins has not conceded. The upstart Opportunity Party could be kingmaker. The FT reports National looks set to hold on.
  • TexasHouston's mayor told police to limit co-operation with immigration agents after Democrats prevailed in this week's Texas elections. Governor Greg Abbott had threatened to cut $110m in funding.
  • ParaguayPedro Alliana was sworn in as president after Santiago Peña was impeached and placed under house arrest. This follows the collapse of digital bank Ueno, which left over half the population without access to savings.
  • IranThe Economist's next item is headlined "Donald Trump keeps Iran guessing". We read the headline only; see the oil card for market context.

Sources and method

Market numbers are Bloomberg quote-page values read at 23:36 Tel Aviv on 7/11 (a Saturday): US, European and Israeli markets are closed, so all index, Treasury, FX, energy and gold values are Friday closes or settlements (values unchanged from the afternoon edition), while Bitcoin and Ether were read live. Changes are against Thursday's close. Some futures and yield quotes carry a 5:00 UTC timestamp on 6/11, which we treat as Friday's generic close. Gold: the XAU quote ($4,070.80, -0.42%) differs from the figure on Bloomberg's markets page ($4,074.90, +0.94%); we keep the XAU quote. USD/ILS: Bloomberg 3.1890 versus 3.1670 on Globes; we keep Bloomberg. Israeli Shahar yields were re-read from TASE (6/11 closes). The Bank of Israel rate (3.25%) and the Hang Seng level are carried from the afternoon edition; the Nikkei level was not fetched. Bloomberg's $5.7tn rout figure is the loss since the 6 October peak, while the FT's $3.2tn is Thursday alone. Bloomberg's Thursday Nasdaq 100 drop and Seeking Alpha's -5.5% differ slightly from the FT's "over 6%"; we quote each as reported. Sources differ on the size of the Binance crypto heist ($6bn in the latest Bloomberg headline, $2.5bn earlier). Anthropic's IPO valuation also differs by source (FT about $1.5tn). The Economist brief was read for its first item only. Calcalist, Bizportal and Yahoo Finance were not read, so TASE movers and next week's data and earnings calendar are missing.
