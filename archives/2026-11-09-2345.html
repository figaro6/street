Monday 9 Nov 2026 · 23:45 Tel Aviv
Night edition · Wall Street close 23:00 · TASE close 17:25

Good night, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street had its best day since April. The S&P 500 closed up 2.35% and the Nasdaq 100 3.4% as chipmakers jumped almost 7%, with AMD up 13%, even as Nvidia fell 4.3% over the spy scandal around Jensen Huang. Brent sank 5.5% to $92.95, a 10-week low, on hopes the Iran war will not restart, despite an attack on Saudi Arabia's key export pipeline. In Tel Aviv the TA-35 rose 1.8% to close above 4,000, and the shekel strengthened to 3.154 per dollar.

Edition focus

Night view: the close, after the bell, and Tuesday's CPI

Wall Street's close

  • Best day since April: S&P 500 +2.35%, Nasdaq 100 +3.4%, chips almost +7%.
  • Nvidia −4.3% was the red island; AMD +13%, Broadcom +8.8%, Micron +6.3%.
  • Energy was the only S&P sector down; airlines jumped on cheaper fuel.
  • Treasuries fell: 2-year +5 bp to 4.56% as money moved back to stocks.

After the bell

  • Take-Two: GTA 6 preorders "unprecedented", but FY27 revenue guide below consensus.
  • Trade Desk beat on EPS and revenue; Q4 revenue guide of at least $755m.
  • Rocket Lab beat on revenue, missed on EPS; Hims & Hers missed.
  • Futures hold the gains: S&P contract +2.3% at 23:25.

Tel Aviv's close & Tuesday

  • TA-35 +1.84% to 4,013.5; NextVision +9.3%, El Al +5.4%, Nova +7.4%.
  • Shekel at 3.154 per dollar on Bloomberg, its strongest in weeks.
  • Tuesday 15:30: US October CPI, with no 12-month figure published.
  • Also Tuesday: SoftBank results, China trade data, CoreWeave after 23:00.

US · Wall Street close
+3.4%

Dip buyers drive the best day since April

The Nasdaq 100 closed up 3.4% and the S&P 500 2.35%, with a chip gauge up almost 7% after last week's crowded-trade unwind. Cheaper energy eased inflation worries before Tuesday's CPI. Nationwide's Mark Hackett sees the resilience needed to turn last week's weakness into a buying opportunity. Edward Jones still favours stocks over bonds; UBS warns concentrated positioning remains a risk.

+13.3%

AMD soars as investors look beyond Nvidia

AMD closed at $508.58 after Oppenheimer upgraded it to outperform with a $600 target, forecasting 135% data-centre revenue growth in 2027 (Calcalist). Broadcom rose 8.8%, Micron 6.3%, Marvell about 9.5% and Western Digital 10.7%. The FT's Lex notes that investors already seem to be contemplating a less Nvidia-centric world.

US · Nvidia
−4.3%

Nvidia ends lower as the Huang probe goes on

Nvidia closed at $178.67, down 22.5% from a month ago according to Seeking Alpha, as its board investigates Jensen Huang's relationship with a suspected Chinese spy. Lex points out that Nvidia is nearly 7% of the S&P 500, Huang has about 60 direct reports and no obvious successor, and directors average 18 years on the board. It suggests naming a clear number two.

GTA 6 launches on 19 November; Sensor Tower counts over 7 million preorders. Trade Desk beat, with revenue of $736m and a Q4 guide of at least $755m. Rocket Lab beat on revenue but missed on EPS. Saudi Arabia's key export pipeline hit in attack

The East-West pipeline, Saudi Arabia's route to the Red Sea since the Hormuz closure, caught fire after an attack near Madinah on Monday. The fire was put out and the energy ministry says operations were not affected. Nobody claimed the attack; the Houthis had hit the Aramco tower in Riyadh a day earlier. Oil barely reacted.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 9 November

  • NvidiaNvidia's shares fell 4.3% as markets reacted to the spying scandal. The board is investigating reports of Huang's relationship with a suspected Chinese spy; he admitted an affair, and the firm says it has no sign that secrets were compromised.
  • US-ChinaChina extended its pause on higher tariffs and other measures against America by two months. It will wait until at least January before raising tariffs by 24%, charging port fees on US ships or ending some rare-earth export waivers.
  • RussiaNew today: Rosneft restricted petrol sales across Russia after Ukraine resumed long-range strikes on refineries on 30 October, once US efforts to halt attacks on energy sites failed. Ukraine recently hit Russia's second-largest refinery.
  • IranNew today: France blamed Iran for last month's cyber-attacks on refrigeration systems in 700 supermarkets, which spoiled food and caused about 20 fires. It issued arrest warrants for three people linked to a Revolutionary Guards cyber unit.
  • KKRKKR posted record quarterly profit: adjusted net income rose 29% to $1.65bn on higher fees and dividends, offsetting weaker insurance profits.
  • UkraineFigure of the day: Ukraine's fertility rate has fallen to 0.8-0.9 children per woman, from 1.2 before the war.

FT · Iran diplomacy
4 leaders

Gulf leaders press Trump not to restart the Iran war

The Saudi crown prince, the UAE president, Qatar's emir and Turkey's Erdoğan have all called Trump in recent days to urge more time for diplomacy. They worry about Netanyahu's lobbying for new strikes; a person familiar with Israel's position says there is no current push. The interim deal on the table would reopen Hormuz in return for lifting the US blockade of Iranian ports. Iran's enriched uranium stockpile remains a key obstacle.

The world is too reliant on Jensen Huang

Lex argues that Nvidia's weight in the S&P 500 and its role financing customers and data-centre builders make Huang's scandal a market-wide risk. It cites successful CEO-to-chair transitions at Morgan Stanley, Amazon and Apple, and suggests regulators could treat big tech like systemic banks, possibly on the new Congress's agenda in 2027.

FT · AI & power
$3.4bn

Anthropic buys its own gas-fired power

Anthropic paid $3.4bn in September for 1.7 GW of Caterpillar generators originally ordered by Texas supplier OnePWR, the largest known power-equipment purchase by an AI lab. It has about $900bn of computing commitments through 2045. The FT notes Blackstone warned investors that Anthropic might get a junk credit rating rather than investment grade.

FT · Pharma
6.4 vs 3.8

FDA orders a blindness warning on GLP-1 drugs

Lilly, Novo and other makers of GLP-1 drugs must add a warning about a rare optic nerve condition that can cause permanent vision loss. The FDA's meta-analysis found 6.4 cases per 100,000 patient-years on GLP-1s versus 3.8 on controls. Novo says the data do not show cause and effect. Lilly still rose 4.3%.
