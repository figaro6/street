Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street opened mixed on election day: the S&P 500 is up 0.2% at about 7,558 and the Nasdaq 0.6%, while the Dow is down 0.5% as Apple (−3.5%) and Palantir (−5%) fall on weaker margin and growth signals and chip stocks bounce after Monday's rout. Number of the day
$114bn

Apollo lent a record $114 billion in the third quarter, much of it to the AI boom

The FT reports that lending at the US private capital group jumped more than 50% from the second quarter, with deals including chip financing for Anthropic and a $500 billion financing effort for Nvidia. Apollo has lent over $350 billion in 12 months, volumes that rival big banks such as HSBC and Citigroup. It is funded by insurance premiums, and aims to earn 0.3% to 0.4% above high-rated bonds. The same day, the FT also reports that yields on the lowest-rated junk debt have climbed past 21% in Europe and 18% in the US. Credit is both financing the AI build-out and cracking at the edges.

US · credit
21%

Junk debt rout: CCC yields pass 21% in Europe

The FT reports a prolonged sell-off in the riskiest junk bonds and loans, driven by rising global rates, fears over AI disruption and high energy prices, plus worries about French public finances. Yields on CCC-rated borrowers have climbed to above 21% in Europe and 18% in the US, from about 16% and 15% earlier. Some of the most active credit investors face steep losses.

Hedge funds lost 3% in October's AI reversal

Marshall Wace's two main strategies fell 5.4% (Eureka) and 3.6% (Tops) last month, according to the FT. Across the industry, funds lost 3% in October on average, per PivotalPath, with equity strategies the worst hit. For most the year is still positive, but the tech sell-off has trimmed the gains.

Junk debt rout hurts credit investors

The FT describes a spreading sell-off at the bottom of the junk market, with CCC yields above 21% in Europe and 18% in the US. Rising rates and fears of AI disruption and expensive energy have made lenders doubt the weakest borrowers. This is the clearest sign of stress outside tech equities.
