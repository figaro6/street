Good morning, Tel Aviv.

Asia is bouncing back from last week's AI rout even as Nvidia faces a crisis at the top. Jensen Huang admitted an affair with a woman US officials suspect of spying for China, and Nvidia's board has opened an independent probe; the stock fell 3.7% in overnight trading on Robinhood. Number of the day
−3.7%

Nvidia's overnight drop after Huang's admission

In a Sunday memo to staff, Jensen Huang admitted an affair with a former executive of Megaspeed, a Singapore customer that US officials suspect worked for Chinese intelligence. Nvidia's board formed a committee of independent directors, with outside counsel, to investigate, though it says it has no reason to think secrets were leaked. Bloomberg says the shares fell 3.7% on Robinhood's overnight platform, and Nasdaq 100 futures briefly lost 1% before recovering. Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon calls it a reminder of big "key man" risk: Nvidia has no public succession plan.

US · Nvidia
−3.7%

Huang admits affair; Nvidia board opens probe

Huang told staff he had been unfaithful to his wife and backed an independent board inquiry into reports that he was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The board says it has no reason to believe secrets were compromised. Trump called him a "fine person" who should stay CEO. Bloomberg Intelligence warns the affair could weaken Nvidia's lobbying on chip-export rules as Democrats take over committees in January. Nvidia reports earnings later this month.

+4.9%

Asia rebounds as Taiwan steps in

MSCI's Asia-Pacific index rose 1.8% after Friday's 3.9% fall, its worst day since April 2025. Taiwan's Taiex ended a seven-day losing streak as regulators curbed short selling and readied a NT$500bn support fund; TSMC and MediaTek gained at least 4%. One strategist quoted by Bloomberg says investors hope Democrats will soften their campaign stance on AI and data centres. Japan and Taiwan both entered bear markets last week.

Israel · budget
NIS 100k

Tax Authority floats a tax on every departing flight

A Tax Authority position paper for the 2027 budget proposes taxing each aircraft leaving Israel by weight and distance, Globes reports. Rates would run from NIS 1,300 for a small plane on a domestic route to NIS 100,000 for a heavy jet on a long-haul flight. The tax chief also says NIS 8bn could be raised from the black economy. These are proposals, not law, and fiscal plans now depend on the next coalition.

FT · Markets
$3.2tn

Anatomy of the AI blow-up

The FT traces Thursday's $3.2tn rout to a chain reaction: Democrats' sweep raised fears of slower data-centre building, then retail investors sold and leveraged hedge funds were forced to cut positions. Vanda counted $388m of retail single-stock selling in two hours. JPMorgan says hedge funds lost 1.6%, their worst day since April 2025. Bulls call it a vote on data centres rather than AI; one macro CIO expects further falls.

Financial Times · 7/11Read →
FT · AI & energy
1.7 GW

Anthropic buys $3.4bn of gas generators

Anthropic took over commitments for 1.7 gigawatts of Caterpillar gas generators from Texas supplier OnePWR, the largest known equipment purchase by an AI lab. The move cuts out middlemen as turbine lead times stretch for years. It comes as Anthropic prepares a possible listing this month to fund about $895bn of computing commitments through 2045, though Blackstone warned it might get a junk credit rating.

Financial Times · 8/11Read →
FT · Opinion
4 tests

El-Erian: what markets want from Democrats

Mohamed El-Erian says the new Congress must restrain policies that rattled investors, such as attacks on Fed independence and erratic tariffs. It should regulate AI carefully rather than block data centres, work on fiscal repair and restore US leadership abroad. He notes many investors see the AI stumble as a chance for a rotation trade rather than a reason to sell.

FT · US-China
Window

China sees a chance to catch up on AI

Analysts in Beijing argue a slower US data-centre buildout could give China a rare opening to close the AI gap, using cheap power and open-source models. Others warn Democrats tend to favour tighter export controls, which could hurt Chinese firms' overseas data centres more than Trump's deal-making approach. HSBC says a US slowdown would hit Asian exports tied to data centres.
