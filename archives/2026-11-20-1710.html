Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

Anthropic has given back most of its debut pop: the stock is down about 8% at $89, barely above its $83 IPO price, after the banks exercised their greenshoe. But Wall Street is rotating rather than collapsing. The Dow is up 0.8% and every S&P sector except tech is higher, while Treasuries rally and the 10-year slips below 5%. Tel Aviv closed its short Friday down 1.5%, capping a 4.4% weekly loss for the TA-35, as a CBS report that Israel and the US are weighing a new strike on Iran and a shekel near 3.33 per dollar add to the strain.

Anthropic's price on day two, down 8% and near its IPO level

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · stocks
+0.8%

Wall Street rotates out of tech instead of selling everything

In the first half hour, every major S&P group except technology rose, and Bloomberg's wrap had the index up 0.2%, on course to end a six-day slide. The Dow led with a gain of about 0.8%, while the Nasdaq 100 fell 0.6–0.7%, far less than the 1.8% drop futures had signalled before the open. Money also went into bonds, a sign of de-risking rather than panic.

US · Anthropic
−8.2%

Anthropic nearly erases its IPO pop on day two

ANT fell as much as 13% before the open and traded near $89 after it, against an $83 offer price. The greenshoe was exercised, taking proceeds to about $119.5bn. Goldman analysts see positive free cash flow only by 2030; the company lost $567m at the operating level in the third quarter. Index buying could help: it may join the Nasdaq 100 as soon as December.

US · companies
20–30%

P&G to cut plant jobs; Apple builds its own AI agent

Procter & Gamble plans to shrink its manufacturing and logistics workforce by 20–30% by the end of the decade as price pressures mount. Apple is developing an autonomous AI agent, a direct challenge to Anthropic and OpenAI. Gap cut its full-year guidance as Banana Republic turned negative, and SK Hynix will become Kioxia's largest shareholder.

US · rates
4.98%

Treasuries rally as the stock rout lifts haven demand

The 10-year yield fell about 7 bp to just under 5%, and the 2-year about the same. The FT notes the old pattern of bonds rising when stocks fall seems to be back. The VIX touched 30 before the open, its highest since the start of the Iran war, double its September low. Fed minutes released Wednesday show officials debating further hikes.

Amazon close to buying Israeli AI firm Decart

Globes reports Amazon will pay more than $7bn in cash and stock, with five-year performance incentives, for Decart, founded by Unit 8200 graduates. Israel Railways faces an immediate liquidity risk

The Government Companies Authority warned of a real cash squeeze at the railway and demanded an efficiency plan. Wage costs rose 60% between 2019 and 2025 and staff 37%, while passenger numbers grew only 4%. The authority estimates about 1,170 more employees than output growth would justify.

Financial Times · key reads

FT · markets
−15%

Anthropic slides as the AI boom's psychology breaks

The FT says the listing was meant to be a clearing event, but worries over AI capex and debt-funded data centres persist; the Nasdaq 100 is more than 15% below its October peak. Some managers sold other tech stocks to buy Anthropic. One strategist noted that the biggest IPOs usually come at the top, not the bottom.

Financial Times · 20/11Read →
FT · chips & China
879,868

US approved Nvidia and AMD chip sales to Chinese groups

Files released by the House foreign affairs committee show requests to ship 3.4mn chips to firms such as Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu and BYD; the Commerce Department approved nearly 880,000. The committee chair says two buyers were smuggling fronts. Nvidia says the shipments were legal and that it has stopped sending H200s to China.

Financial Times · 20/11Read →
FT · opinion
$530bn

Can Nvidia avoid AIG's fate?

The columnist sees echoes of AIG before 2008: a pillar of a circular, half-hidden financing web, run by a dominant leader who has now left. Nvidia has given about $100bn in equity and over $530bn in off-balance-sheet guarantees and lease backstops. Its core business is far stronger, but the risk is what happens if it stops acting as AI's quasi-central bank.

Financial Times · 20/11Read →
FT · rates
<20%

Oil loses its grip on bond markets

The correlation between oil and government bond yields, about 65% in September, has fallen below 20%, per Cboe. WTI is down from $110 to under $90, but yields stay high as investors focus on entrenched inflation and hawkish central banks. Two-year gilt yields topped 5% for the first time in three years.
