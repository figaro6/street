Midday edition · built 09:20 before TASE opens: Monday's US close, Asia, US futures

Good morning, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street ended Monday split: the Dow rose 0.4% but the S&P 500 fell 0.5% to 7,541 and the Nasdaq 1.1%, as chip stocks sank (Micron −6%, AMD −7.5%) and the early gains vanished. Apple beat forecasts but warned that memory-chip costs will squeeze margins, and Anthropic's filing disclosed about $895 billion of AI spending commitments. Seoul fell 3.5% on chips this morning, US futures are slightly lower, and Brent bounced 1.8% to about $107 after Monday's 7.6% drop. In Tel Aviv the shekel is weakening past 3.15 per dollar, dual-listed stocks open with negative arbitrage of 2.5% to 6%, Teva reports around 14:00, and America votes today.

Anthropic's filing reveals AI spending commitments of about $895 billion

Ahead of what The Economist calls one of the largest IPOs ever, the filing shows Q3 revenue of almost $17 billion against $1.4 billion a year ago, but an operating loss of $567 million after a $974 million profit in the prior quarter, as computing costs jumped. Financial Times · key reads

FT · tech
45–46%

Apple warns "unprecedented" memory chip costs will hit margins

Apple forecast sales growth of 7% to 10% for the holiday quarter, ahead of Wall Street's 6%, but guided gross margin to 45% to 46% against 47% expected. CEO John Ternus called memory pricing "unprecedented" as data centres absorb supply, and the services outlook was also weaker. Shares slipped about 3% after hours. A signal that the AI build-out is raising costs well beyond the chip makers.

Financial Times · 3/11Read →
FT · AI & China
51%

Nvidia tells cloud partners to sell at least half their services outside China

Five people told the FT that Nvidia has verbally told data-centre clients in recent months that at least 51% of their services must go to non-Chinese customers, a rule that did not exist before. It further restricts Chinese tech groups, which had relied on overseas cloud deals to reach advanced US chips, and comes as Washington tightens access.

FT · opinion
Rates

Ruchir Sharma: AI mania can't survive higher interest rates

The Rockefeller International chair argues that analysts shrugging off surging bond yields is classic bubble logic. Record global debt, much of it on government books, means companies compete with governments for capital, and he warns of a rapid liquidity squeeze that traps firms between dearer borrowing and warier lenders. With the 10-year Treasury at 4.92% and the 30-year at 5.29%, the point is live. This is one writer's opinion.
