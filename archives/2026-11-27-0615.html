Good morning, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street reopens today for a short session, and the futures market has already voted: Nasdaq 100 futures are up about 2.4% from Wednesday's close and S&P 500 futures 1.6%, as buyers return to chip stocks. Asia followed, with Korea's Kospi up more than 3% as memory makers rebounded from Wednesday's 6% rout. Tel Aviv got there first: on Thursday the TA-125 rose 3% and the TA-90 almost 5%, its best day in two years, which Bizportal links to progress in coalition talks. The dollar is at about NIS 3.30, and the question for today's four-hour session is whether this is a real bottom or a thin-volume holiday bounce.

US · futures
+2.4%

Chip buyers return before a four-hour session

Nasdaq 100 futures rose through the holiday and are about 2.4% above Wednesday's close, with S&P 500 futures up 1.6%. Asia · memory chips
+3.5%

Kospi leads Asia's rebound as memory recovers

Korea's Kospi climbed back towards 4,900 after Wednesday's 6.2% plunge, led by Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, the stocks that took most of the selling. The Nikkei rose about 1.8% and the Hang Seng 0.8%, while Shanghai barely moved. AP notes that investors are still weighing planned US tariff increases on China, Japan and the EU.

Bizportal · AP via Yahoo · 27/11Read →
China · economy
+20.1%

AI demand lifts China's industrial profits

Profits at Chinese industrial firms rose 20.1% in October from a year earlier, the fastest pace since May. Computers, communications and electronics more than doubled their profits this year and now earn about a sixth of the total, thanks to demand for memory chips and optical fibre. Furniture, cars and steel fell, and retail sales rose just 0.4%.

Bizportal · 27/11Read →
US · economy
4.3%

GDP revised up; Trump calls first figure fake

The Commerce Department raised third-quarter growth to 4.3% annualised from a first estimate of 3.4%, citing software investment and consumer spending. Trump said the first figure, released five days before the midterms the Republicans lost, was deliberately wrong and demanded an inquiry, without evidence. Strong growth plus sticky inflation keeps a December Fed hike in play.

Bizportal · 26/11Read →
Chips · trade
$1.61tn

Chip exports overtake oil and gas

Deutsche Bank's Jim Reid calculates that world chip exports reached about $1.61trn over four quarters, against $1.53trn for oil and gas, and talks of a shift from the petrodollar to the "chip dollar". He expects faster growth but also more volatility. That came quickly: the Philadelphia chip index had its worst week in 25 years last week.

Israel · budget
NIS 58bn

Israel heads into 2027 without a budget

With Eisenkot's mandate ending in early December, Bizportal says a 2027 budget law is now almost impossible by January. Spending will run on a continuing budget of about NIS 58bn a month, linked to population growth rather than prices. Once a government is sworn in, a 145-day clock starts; if no budget passes, the Knesset dissolves.
