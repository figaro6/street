Wednesday 25 Nov 2026 · 23:50 Tel Aviv
Night edition · Wall Street close 23:00 · TASE close 17:25

Good night, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street ended a wild session well off its lows. The Nasdaq 100 had slid more than 4% and come close to a bear market, but dip buyers cut the loss to 0.6% at the close and most S&P 500 stocks rose. Nvidia still fell 3.9% after S&P Global Ratings warned it may cut its credit rating. Treasuries rallied for an eighth day, the longest run since 2024, even though third-quarter GDP was revised up to 4.3% and a December Fed hike is about 95% priced. In Tel Aviv the TA-35 closed 1.4% lower, led by chipmakers, and Norstar completed a deeply discounted share sale.

Key numbers

A wild day ends well off the lows

Number of the day
8

Days in a row of falling 10-year yields

The 10-year Treasury yield fell about 6 basis points to 4.87%, its eighth straight daily decline and the longest such run since 2024. That is striking because the macro news pointed the other way: GDP was revised up to 4.3%, core PCE inflation hit a three-year high of 3.3%, and traders still put a December rate hike at about 95%. Bonds are trading as a haven from the AI sell-off, not on the inflation outlook.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · markets
−0.6%

Dip buyers rescue the Nasdaq from a bear market

After briefly trading close to 20% below its October record, the Nasdaq 100 trimmed its loss to 0.6% by the close, and most S&P 500 members rose as money rotated out of tech. Bloomberg's wrap says the euphoria over Jensen Huang's return has faded, with AI spending and higher borrowing costs back in focus. One strategist called it a contradiction: markets are asked to fund a huge capex cycle while the Fed is set to make it dearer.

Bloomberg · 25/11Read →
US · bonds & the Fed
4.87%

Treasuries rally for an eighth straight day

The 10-year yield fell about 6 basis points, the longest losing streak since 2024, as investors sought safety before the holiday. The Fed's Beige Book showed moderate growth and some pickup in wage pressures, and headline PCE inflation rose to 3.8%. Markets still expect the Fed to raise rates on 9 December, so the bond rally is a haven trade rather than a bet on cuts.

Bloomberg · FT · 25/11Read →
US · chips & credit
−3.9%

S&P may cut Nvidia's rating over governance

S&P Global Ratings said it may lower Nvidia's credit rating by one notch, citing management and governance after its CEO's exit and swift reinstatement. The shares fell 3.9% to about $149. Bloomberg also reports that credit risk on CoreWeave and Nvidia hit fresh highs, and that CoreWeave's banks plan to sell a $4.5bn loan backed by GPUs used by Meta as soon as next week.

Bloomberg · 25/11Read →
US · companies
1m

SpaceX cleared for data centres in orbit; HP jumps

The FCC approved SpaceX's request to launch up to a million satellites for a network of orbiting AI data centres, a costly plan, The Economist notes. HP rose after forecasting profit likely above estimates, a sign it is coping with soaring memory-chip costs. Palo Alto Networks was sued by a former executive who says he was fired for resisting accounting fraud.

Israel · TASE close
−1.4%

Tel Aviv's sell-off continues, led by chips

The TA-35 closed 1.42% lower at 3,792 and the TA-125 1.21% lower. Tower Semiconductor fell 7.35%, the index's worst performer on the day's highest turnover, with Nova −5.7%, Camtek −3.8% and OPC Energy −5.7%; the big banks lost about 1.3%. El Al rose 2.6%, Strauss 1.9% on its efficiency plan, and insurers Migdal and Harel also gained.

Israel · real estate credit
NIS 90m

Norstar completes its deeply discounted raise

Chaim Katzman's Norstar raised about NIS 90m by selling 45m shares at NIS 2, with demand for more than 40m shares. Katzman bought about a quarter and will be diluted to roughly 30%, but keeps control. The stock fell 61% on the day to a value of about NIS 120m; the money is for 2027 bond and bank repayments. G City's NIS 245m dividend, most of it going to Norstar, angered its bondholders.

Calcalist · 25/11Read →
Israel · companies
150

Teva cuts generic R&D jobs in Kfar Saba

Teva will phase out generic-drug development at its Kfar Saba site, cutting 150 jobs by the end of 2027 as it refocuses on innovative drugs and biologics. It will still invest tens of millions of dollars in the plant's manufacturing. Strauss approved a plan to save about NIS 100m a year before tax by cutting management layers, from early 2027.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 25 November

  • TariffsThe Trump administration proposed duties of up to 20% on goods from China, the EU, Japan, South Korea, India and others, replacing levies the Supreme Court struck down.
  • Wall StreetUS stocks recovered from Tuesday's tech rout, with the Nasdaq 100 down 0.6% at the close. Nvidia lost about 4%, Anthropic stayed below its $83 IPO price, and safe-haven buying pushed Treasury yields lower.
  • SpaceXThe FCC approved SpaceX's plan to launch a million satellites for data centres in space. Elon Musk welcomed it; the cost is likely to be steep.
  • LukoilA consortium led by Todd Boehly, including America's International Development Finance Corporation, agreed to acquire the sanctioned Russian oil firm's international assets in a $10bn securities swap, pending Treasury approval.
  • BangladeshThe government offered garment workers a 36% minimum-wage rise to 17,000 taka a month after violent protests. Clothing is about 90% of the country's exports.

Financial Times · key reads

FT · US economy
4.3%

GDP upgrade keeps a December hike in play

Third-quarter growth was revised up to a 4.3% annual rate from 3.4%, driven by consumer spending, while core PCE inflation rose to a three-year high of 3.3%. Economists at ING and Capital Economics see a December hike as all but certain; traders price about 95%. But spending was flat in October, a hint that growth is fading into the holidays.

Financial Times · 25/11Read →
FT · credit
$87bn

The Ellisons' debt gamble on Hollywood

Buying Paramount and Warner Bros Discovery left the Ellisons' Skydance with about $87bn of debt, and some old Paramount bonds trade at 56 cents on the dollar. The FT finds Skydance and Oracle bonds make up nearly half of the 100 widest-spread investment-grade issues. Larry Ellison has pledged about $45bn of Oracle shares for loans, tying the AI build-out and Hollywood together.

Financial Times · 25/11Read →
FT View · funds
15%

The fund manager's dilemma over AI

The editorial asks whether managers who stayed underweight tech should now be praised, and argues the real question is concentration risk across a whole portfolio. TSMC alone is about 15% of the MSCI emerging-markets index and moves with Nvidia and Broadcom, so "EM" and "developed" buckets overlap more than investors assume. UK regulators are urging trustees not to judge managers on returns alone.
