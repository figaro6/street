Thursday 19 Nov 2026 · 17:20 Tel Aviv
Afternoon edition · Wall Street open 16:30 · US data 15:30 · Tel Aviv into the close

Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street opened with a rally as Anthropic's record IPO revived the AI trade: the Nasdaq 100 is up about 1.8% and the S&P 500 0.9%, on course to snap a five-day slide, with Nvidia up 4%. Anthropic itself is expected to start trading near $100 a share, some 20% above its $83 offer price. Tel Aviv is going the other way, with the TA-35 down 0.8% as banks, insurers and builders sell off. The shekel is at its weakest in more than a year, near 3.29 to the dollar, four days before the Bank of Israel decides on rates.

Where Anthropic is indicated to open, vs an $83 IPO price

Early buying interest points to a first trade around $100 a share, a gain of about 20%; Calcalist cites indications nearer 25%. That would lift the Claude maker well above the roughly $1.5tn fully diluted value set by the $83 price, which was itself above the $70–78 range. The listing is being treated as a test of appetite for AI after Nvidia's results and Jensen Huang's exit, and so far the market is passing it: the Nasdaq 100 is up almost 2% and the VIX down 7%.

The stories behind the numbers

US · the open
+1.8%

Anthropic's IPO revives the AI trade at the open

The Nasdaq 100 climbed about 2% in the first 15 minutes and the S&P 500 almost 1%, on track to end a five-day slide, its longest since June. Bloomberg ties the bounce to demand for Anthropic's shares after a rough month: the Nasdaq 100 had lost more than 4% in November through Wednesday, its worst November since 2008. State Street's Marija Veitmane says AI enthusiasm and earnings are intact.

ANT set to open about 20% above its $83 price

Early indications point to a first trade near $100 a share on Nasdaq under ANT; Calcalist cites a pop of about 25%. Anthropic raised $81.3bn in the IPO plus $26bn in a private placement, valuing it near $1.5tn fully diluted. Needham started coverage at Buy with a $150 target, putting it in the same bucket as Amazon, Alphabet and Microsoft. Bloomberg says most institutional buyers got no shares.

Initial claims rose by 1,000 to 203,000 in the week to 14 November, an unexpected increase but still a low level. Treasury yields edged higher after the data, with the 10-year up about 3 bp to 5.10% and the 2-year at 4.83%, as markets keep pricing a Fed hike in December.

Financial Times · key reads

FT · Middle East
80 floors

Strike hits Saudi wealth fund's Riyadh tower

Several middle floors of the Public Investment Fund's headquarters, Riyadh's tallest building, were damaged early on Thursday as Houthi rebels keep up near-daily missile and drone attacks. It is the second hit on the financial district this month after Aramco's office. The attacks have already closed the airport for a time and forced the kingdom to cancel its flagship investment conference.

Financial Times · 19/11Read →
FT · energy
$240bn

IEA: the world could manage without Hormuz

In its World Energy Outlook, the IEA says oil markets can be rebalanced without restoring flows through the strait, at a cost of about $40bn of new pipelines and $200bn of oilfield investment elsewhere. About a fifth of world oil and LNG passed through Hormuz before the war. It would take time, but import-dependent countries may build up domestic supply.

Financial Times · 19/11Read →
FT · gold
−33%

Gold fails as a haven in the tech sell-off

Gold has lost a third of its value since January's record of $5,595 and touched $3,742 on Wednesday, its lowest since September 2025. It fell about 10% while tech stocks slid from their October peak, behaving like a risk asset, says Ninety One's George Cheveley. Investors who bought it as a hedge have been let down.

Financial Times · 19/11Read →
FT · Israel politics
66 votes

Opposition takes the Knesset Speaker's chair

Matan Kahana of Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party was elected Speaker with 66 votes, including Arab-Israeli lawmakers, tightening the opposition's grip on parliament after the inconclusive 27 October election. Netanyahu's coalition holds 53 seats. The Speaker sets the agenda, which could help the opposition push legislation and scrutiny of the government.
