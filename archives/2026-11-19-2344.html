Good night, Tel Aviv.

Anthropic got its blockbuster debut, but the market did not get its rescue. The Claude maker jumped as much as 39% before closing 17% above its $83 IPO price, while the S&P 500 swung from a 0.9% gain at the open to a 2% loss, its longest losing run since April 2024, as AMD sank 8% on a short seller's report. After the bell Intuit fell 14% on a guidance cut. Tel Aviv closed down 1.2%, with builders, insurers and banks hit by fears that UK sanctions could spread, and the dollar ended above 3.31 shekels.

Number of the day
−2.0%

The S&P 500's fall on the day Anthropic listed

The index opened up almost 1% as Anthropic's shares were indicated sharply higher, then reversed to close at 7,175.83, down 2.0%. More than 460 of its members fell and every megacap ended lower. It is the longest losing streak since April 2024, and the FT says the index is down 5.3% since the end of October, on course for its worst month since March 2025. The message from traders: one hit IPO for the leading AI lab does not answer the doubts over AI spending, debt and high rates.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · the close
−2.0%

Anthropic's debut is no all-clear for AI stocks

The morning rally faded and the S&P 500 closed 2% lower, with the Dow on the edge of a technical correction and all megacaps down. Miller Tabak's Matt Maley said the IPO does nothing about AI profitability, heavy spending, high rates, credit stress or geopolitics. Others noted investors will back the leading lab, but are pickier about debt-heavy cloud and data-centre plays.

Bloomberg Markets Wrap · 19/11Read →
US · IPO
+17%

ANT hits $115, then settles at $97

Anthropic rose as much as 39% to $115.10 before closing at $97.17, a market value of about $1.3tn, the FT reports. The company raised $107bn including a placement led by Nvidia and Google, more than SpaceX's record. Dario Amodei, ringing the opening bell, said the firm would not bend to business or political pressure. It needs the cash for more than $900bn of computing deals.

Financial Times · Bloomberg · 19/11Read →
US · chips
−7.7%

AMD tumbles on a short seller's China claims

Hunterbrook Capital said it is short AMD after its media arm reported the chipmaker's growth depends on products facing tighter US export curbs and on a large Chinese supply chain. AMD said the claims misrepresent its business. The stock, still more than double this year, fell to $489, and Visdom's Mike Zigmont said it pulled the wider market into the red.

Bloomberg · FT · 19/11Read →
US · after hours
−14%

Intuit slides on a weaker revenue outlook

Intuit's first-quarter results met forecasts, but it cut its full-year revenue guidance on slow growth at Credit Karma, and the shares fell more than 14% after the close. Gap rose about 4% after hours on an earnings beat, though Bloomberg says it lowered its full-year outlook as Banana Republic turned negative. Webull fell despite beating estimates.

US · movers
−15%

Williams-Sonoma sinks; Palo Alto and Okta gain

Williams-Sonoma was the S&P 500's worst performer, down 15%, followed by Twilio and AMD. Palo Alto Networks held a 6.4% gain after its results and $4.5bn AI deal, and Okta rose 5.7%. Intel bucked the chip sell-off with a 1.9% rise while Broadcom lost 2.1%.

US · China chips
879k

US approved 879,000 AI chips for Chinese buyers

Documents released by the House Foreign Affairs Committee show the Commerce Department licensed sales of more than 879,000 AI chips to Chinese firms by May, including Alibaba, Tencent and companies the Pentagon lists as military-linked. Requests covered 3.4m chips. It adds to the political fight over export controls after the Huang affair.

Israel · TASE close
−1.16%

Builders, insurers and banks sink Tel Aviv

The TA-35 closed down 1.16% at 3,919 and the TA-125 1.15%. The construction index lost 3.3%, insurers 3.1% and banks 2.3%, with Mizrahi Tefahot down 3.1% and Teva 4.2%. Ackerstein, Azorim and Electra Real Estate fell 6–10%. Palo Alto rose 9.7% in Tel Aviv, catching up with Wednesday's Wall Street jump.

Israel · sanctions
8 firms

UK sanctions on builders stoke fears for banks

Britain sanctioned eight construction and architecture firms working beyond the Green Line, months earlier than expected. Globes says the UK government has not ruled out targeting Israeli banks that finance such building, which would threaten their access to global markets. Alony Hetz's Nathan Hetz warned this week that European banks are already shying away from Israeli deals.

Israel · shekel
3.31

Dollar pushes above 3.31 shekels

The Bank of Israel set the representative rate 0.64% higher at 3.282, and the dollar kept climbing into the evening, to 3.312 on Bloomberg. The euro reached 3.635. Weak local stocks, sanction fears and a firm dollar are working against the shekel four days before Monday's rate decision, which makes a cut harder to argue for.

Israel Railways warned of a liquidity crunch

The Government Companies Authority says Israel Railways faces an immediate liquidity risk and must draw up an efficiency plan. Wage costs rose 60% between 2019 and 2025, to NIS 1.66bn, and staff numbers 37%, while service barely improved and profitability declined.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 19 November

  • AnthropicShares closed 17% up on the first day, valuing the AI lab near $1.3trn after the biggest listing ever, but the Nasdaq still fell 2.2%, a sign of lingering caution about AI.
  • ShenzhenTrump, Putin and Xi held their first three-way talks: heavy on symbolism, light on breakthroughs. Putin brushed off fears of a new Yalta.
  • AMDThe chipmaker fell almost 8% on a short seller's claim it relies on blacklisted Chinese suppliers, which it denies. Commerce issued a rule this week to stop Chinese firms renting advanced chips in data centres abroad.
  • RiyadhSaudi Arabia says a Houthi missile hit the city's tallest tower, blowing out windows on several floors, less than two weeks after an attack on Aramco's offices.
  • WalmartThe retailer raised its sales and profit forecasts after US online sales grew 23%; quarterly revenue rose 5.2% to $188.8bn.
  • MyanmarThe ICJ found Myanmar guilty of genocide against the Rohingya, the first such verdict against a state by an international court. Reparations will follow.

−5.3%

"Resignation that the AI peak is behind us"

The FT's market report puts the S&P 500 down 5.3% since the end of October, on course for its worst month since March 2025. Nvidia, Arm and Broadcom fell 2–3% alongside AMD. Visdom's Mike Zigmont describes a mood of resignation about AI, a striking contrast with the queue to buy Anthropic.

FT · venture capital
$100bn+

Low-profile VCs set for a giant Anthropic payday

A small group of early backers stands to share well over $100bn. Angel investor Anjney Midha first backed the lab in 2021 at a value about 2,300 times smaller. Spark Capital's stake is worth about $11bn and Factorial Funds' just over $10bn, both from a 2023 round at a roughly $4bn valuation.

FT · Ukraine
36 nations

Putin invites Europe to Moscow, on his terms

After the Shenzhen summit, Putin invited European leaders to Moscow to discuss Ukraine without promising them a formal role in peace talks, signalling no softening of his demands. The 36-nation Coalition of the Willing, meeting in Berlin, pledged more money and arms for Kyiv.

Musk says he feels "foolish" over midterm donations

Elon Musk told the DealBook Summit he regrets spending more than $470mn backing Republicans in midterms where they lost the House, the Senate and several statehouses, and said he is not part of the Maga movement. It is a sharp turn for Trump's former "first buddy" and Doge chief.
