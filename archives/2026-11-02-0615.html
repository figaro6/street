Friday's closes, and what moved overnight

Number of the day
−3.3%

Brent's overnight drop, to about $109.70, on a report that Trump wants more time for Iran diplomacy

Axios reported that Trump told Netanyahu by phone he prefers diplomacy, a stance that worried the Israeli prime minister; Secretary of State Rubio said on Saturday no decision on new strikes had been made. That took out Friday's 2% gain and pulled WTI to about $100.70. The risk has not gone away: ships were struck near Hormuz and deep in the Gulf over the weekend, Iran's Supreme Leader ordered commanders to prepare for renewed fighting, and Rabobank calls the drop temporary. Goldman Sachs raised its Brent forecast to $105 for December and $88 on average for 2027. For Israel, the story cuts both ways: cheaper oil eases inflation, while a US–Israel split on Iran is a political risk.

Prediction markets price a Democratic sweep of Congress

Israel · economy
5%

Moody's raises Israel's growth forecasts

In a report issued after the Knesset election, Moody's now sees GDP growth of 5% in 2026 (from 3.7%) and 6% in 2027 (from 5%). The agency warns the jump mostly reflects a statistical change: the Central Bureau of Statistics now counts more of Israeli companies' overseas production, boosted by AI-linked high-tech exports. Domestic activity has not improved by as much.

  • Iran talksIranian negotiators held a third day of talks with Qatari mediators in Doha. Foreign minister Araghchi says the draft plan would reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days of a deal. Trump says he won't strike before Tuesday's midterms but might afterwards. Oil fell on the news.

"The credit market's warning is coming back to haunt AI stocks"

  • Credit default swap spreads for Meta, Broadcom and Nvidia have widened sharply, even while their shares held up or rose.
