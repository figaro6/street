Thursday 26 Nov 2026 · 06:15 Tel Aviv
Morning edition · US close 23:00 · Asia midday · Thanksgiving: Wall Street shut

Good morning, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street pulled back from the edge last night: the Nasdaq 100 briefly sat 20% below its peak, then ended just 0.6% lower as dip buyers arrived before the holiday. Asia picked up the mood this morning, with regional stocks up 1.3% and Nasdaq futures almost 1% higher. Treasuries closed out an eighth straight rally, the 10-year at 4.85%, and there is no cash bond trading today. US markets are shut for Thanksgiving, so the Tel Aviv exchange trades alone after yesterday's 1.4% fall, with El Al's NIS 1bn loyalty-club buyback in focus.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

Asia · overnight
+1.9%

Asia rallies as AI optimism returns, for now

Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose about 2%, Japan's Topix 0.7% and Australia's ASX 0.9%, while Nasdaq 100 futures climbed almost 1%. South Korea's central bank raised rates and lifted its 2027 growth forecast, and a hawkish Bank of Japan board member called for quick hikes; Japan's 10-year yield rose to 2.66%. UBS's Asia CIO told Bloomberg this is a time to manage risk, not to be brave.

US · bonds & the Fed
4.85%

Treasuries end an eight-day rally into the holiday

The 10-year yield fell 8 basis points in New York to 4.85% as investors sought safety from the AI sell-off, even though October's core PCE inflation rose 0.3% and headline inflation is 3.8%. A BMI economist says markets now price about one fewer Fed hike by next September than two weeks ago, as wider credit spreads and weaker stocks tighten conditions. There is no cash Treasury trading anywhere today.

Bloomberg · 26/11Read →
US · Nvidia
+1.3%

Pentagon weighed a wartime law to restore Huang

The Washington Post reports that Defence Department officials discussed using the Defense Production Act to pressure Nvidia's board into reinstating Jensen Huang before his return on Monday; a senior Pentagon official denies it. The shares fell 3.9% to $149.30 on Wednesday after S&P put Nvidia's AA rating on watch negative, then rose 1.3% after hours. The episode adds political risk to the governance worries.

US · trade
20%

New tariffs to replace the ones courts struck down

The US proposed duties on 14 partners: 20% for China and Vietnam, 18% for five Southeast Asian countries, 15% for the EU, Japan, Korea and Taiwan, and 10% for Singapore. Most match rates agreed in earlier trade deals; oil, gas and fertilisers are largely exempt. China's rate could double if Washington decides Beijing broke its truce, which expires on 10 January.

Israel · El Al
NIS 4.2bn

El Al buys back its loyalty club stake

El Al will pay Phoenix NIS 1.05bn for the 25% of its frequent-flyer club that it sold when it was in distress, valuing the club at NIS 4.2bn, more than Isracard's NIS 3.4bn market value. The club earned NIS 160m in nine months, with revenue up 61%, and has 3.8m members. Moving its Fly Card from ICC-Cal to Isracard should add more than NIS 100m a year in profit.

Israel · defence IPO
49%

Israel Aerospace IPO pencilled in for 2027

A senior official at the Government Companies Authority told Calcalist the IAI flotation will happen next year, in several tranches of up to 49%, and will be the largest IPO Tel Aviv has seen; reports put the value near NIS 100bn. She said the Defence Ministry owes defence firms about NIS 20bn, an unprecedented sum. Missile-engine maker Tomer raised NIS 600m of debt on TASE this week.

Israel · housing
12,000

Home investors start selling at a loss

Finance Ministry figures on about 12,000 investor home sales in 2025 show a substantial share of five-year holders lost money in real terms. Sellers are cutting asking prices to find buyers, and the high purchase tax on investors deepens the loss. It is a sign that the housing slowdown is reaching investors, not just developers.

Autonomous bondholders demand fresh capital

Holders of Autonomous's NIS 46m bond, now trading at a 35.7% yield, want controlling shareholder Tomer Avnon to inject capital, and some are weighing a demand for immediate repayment. The counter-drone and border-security company's value has fallen more than 85% to about NIS 22.5m. It had only $2.9m in cash at the end of June but says it can repay.

Financial Times · key reads

FT · Alphaville
$83

Anthropic's pushed-to-the-limit IPO hurts OpenAI

Craig Coben argues Anthropic and its banks pushed every lever: pricing above range, a 19% upsized deal, 315m insider shares free to sell on day one, and a full greenshoe that left no room to support the stock. Raising about $120bn, it now sits on a $250bn war chest, so trading below issue barely matters to management. The cost may fall on OpenAI, whose float now faces more sceptical buyers.

Financial Times · 25/11Read →
FT · AI boom
10%

AI build-out vs Britain's railway mania

Tim Harford weighs a study putting US AI capex at 3.2% of GDP a year to 2032 against the 1840s UK railway boom, which hit 3.8%. The warning signs rhyme: politically connected promoters and borrowing hidden in off-balance-sheet vehicles. To pay off, AI data centres need revenue near 10% of GDP; the railways expected the same and earned a quarter of it.

Financial Times · 25/11Read →
FT · Russia & Ukraine
Donbas

US drops business deals as a lure for Putin

Five officials told the FT that Kushner and Witkoff have shelved offers of US investment in Russian oil, titanium and pipeline gas, after Moscow showed little interest at the Abu Dhabi talks. The focus is now land-for-peace and security guarantees; Putin still wants all of Donbas. A US official called the report inaccurate. For markets, it lowers the odds of Russian energy returning to Europe soon.
