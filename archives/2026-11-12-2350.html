Good night, Tel Aviv.

Nvidia ended its first day without Jensen Huang down 4.3%, a third of its 13% plunge at the open, and the S&P 500 slipped only 0.4%. The bigger move was in bonds: Trump's "we don't need a deal with Iran" lifted Brent 3.3% to almost $96, and the 10-year Treasury yield jumped 13 bp to 5.13% as Fed officials talked up more hikes. After the bell Cisco raised its outlook and Cerebras jumped 14%. In Tel Aviv the TA-35 closed up 0.4%, but small caps fell 1.4% and insurers 2.4%.

Key numbers

Stocks shrug off Huang; bonds don't shrug off oil

Number of the day
+13 bp

The 10-year Treasury yield jumps to 5.13%

While Nvidia grabbed the headlines, the bond market had the bigger day. Trump's post that the US no longer needs a deal with Iran sent Brent up 3.3% to almost $96, and the 10-year yield rose 13 bp to 5.13%, its highest in these editions; the 2-year rose 15 bp to 4.89%. Fed officials piled on: San Francisco's Mary Daly said more hikes are likely needed, and Cleveland's Beth Hammack called for more restrictive rates. Bloomberg says traders now bet the Fed will step up the pace of its hikes. For Israel, the Oct 2035 Shahar now yields a full point less than Treasuries.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · the close
−4.3%

Nvidia claws back most of its opening plunge

Nvidia closed at $166, down 4.3%, after losing 13% at the open, as interim CEO Colette Kress, CFO for 13 years, takes the helm. Strategists quoted by Bloomberg argue the big AI buyers need the chips whatever the name on the door; Edward Jones says fundamentals are unchanged. eToro notes next week's earnings will now face an even brighter spotlight.

Bloomberg · 12/11Read →
US · Fed
5.13%

Oil and hawkish Fed talk push yields higher

The 10-year yield rose 13 bp and the 2-year 15 bp. San Francisco Fed president Mary Daly said further tightening will likely be needed because price pressure is spreading beyond tariff- and oil-hit sectors. Cleveland's Hammack also wants more restrictive rates, and Goolsbee said inflation is edging into a "tough spot" (headline). BofA's desk sees hedge funds rebuilding shorts in rate-sensitive stocks.

Bloomberg · 12/11Read →
US · after hours
$18.1bn

Cisco raises its outlook on AI networking

Cisco guided to about $18.1bn of sales in its January quarter, above the $17.8bn analysts expected, and raised its full-year revenue range to $74.8-75.8bn from as much as $73.4bn. Bloomberg calls it a sign the AI boom keeps fuelling demand for networking gear. The stock closed up 1.2% at $102 before the report.

Bloomberg · 12/11Read →
US · after hours
+14%

Cerebras soars; Applied Materials edges up

Cerebras forecast about $320mn of Q4 revenue against $271mn expected and raised its full-year outlook, sending the Nvidia challenger up more than 14% after hours despite a wider loss. Applied Materials rose 1.4% after hours as fiscal Q4 adjusted EPS of $4.16 and guidance topped forecasts; Seeking Alpha highlights Taiwan revenue up 59%. AMAT had fallen 3.6% in the session.

Bloomberg · Seeking Alpha · 12/11Read →
US · probe
−11%

DOJ probes Alice Huang over Super Micro smuggling

Manhattan prosecutors are examining whether entities linked to Alice Huang, the suspected spy at the centre of the Nvidia scandal, helped smuggle Super Micro servers to China, Bloomberg reports. Semafor says Nvidia itself is now entangled in DOJ probes involving Super Micro and Singapore's Megaspeed. Super Micro shares fell 11%.

Bloomberg · Seeking Alpha · 12/11Read →
US · chips
+5.5%

Broadcom and AMD rally as Nvidia's rivals

Investors bet Nvidia's rivals can win business during its leadership vacuum: Broadcom rose 5.5% and AMD gained early. The neoclouds stayed weak, with Nebius down about 17% on its outlook and CoreWeave down 7%, even as CoreWeave lines up a $4bn Meta-linked loan to cut funding costs.

Bloomberg · 12/11Read →
US · IPO
6x

Anthropic's record IPO draws six times demand

Anthropic's offering has attracted about six times as many orders as shares available, Bloomberg reports. The AI company and its co-founders seek up to $64.4bn in the IPO plus $26bn in a private placement, which together would beat SpaceX as the biggest listing ever. Separately, SpaceX and Tesla are said to be moving toward a merger as soon as December.

Bloomberg · Seeking Alpha · 12/11Read →
Energy · Iran
+3.3%

Brent climbs to almost $96 after Trump's Iran post

Trump wrote that the US doesn't need a deal with Iran because it already has "their strait", dimming hopes of a deal to end the war that has disrupted Gulf supplies. Brent rose steadily through the US session and settled 3.3% higher; WTI is near $89 and US natural gas up almost 5%. Israel's oil and gas index rose 2.1%.

Big caps up, small caps down in Tel Aviv

The TA-35 closed up 0.4% and the TA-125 0.2%, but the TA-90 slipped and the SME60 small-cap index fell 1.4%, Bizportal says. Banks rose 0.9% while insurers dropped 2.4%; real estate and tech fell too. Valorix jumped 11% on a drone launch, Top Gum rose 5.4% as Ofra Strauss joins its board, and Nextvision gained 3.8%.

Israel · taxes
31%

Treasury floats tax ideas for after the election

A Finance Ministry and Tax Authority panel is compiling options for the next budget: widening the 31% bracket for high earners, fewer child tax credits for parents, VAT on foreign streaming and apps, and a tax on every flight leaving Ben Gurion. None is approved and no minister backs them yet; the aim is a ready package for the government formed after elections, expected in early 2027.

AFI's profit rises, yet it trades below book

AFI Properties' Q3 attributable profit rose about 15% to NIS 512mn, but the stock ended near NIS 185, roughly 10% below equity per share. Teva's CEO, interviewed by Bizportal with shares at decade highs, said innovative drugs now drive most of its profit and growth. Mimun Yashir's quarterly profit fell 26% (headline).

Financial Times · key reads

All three cards are based on the article text.

FT · Lex
$5.8tn

"Jensen jeopardy" reaches every corner of AI

Lex argues Huang's fall leaves a chasm at the company, the industry and in geopolitics. Nvidia peaked at a $5.8tn valuation in October and had committed $124bn to equity investments in AI companies by July, making it the sector's financier as well as its chip supplier. That web of stakes is what makes the leadership gap so disruptive.

Financial Times · 12/11Read →
FT · Washington
Jan

Huang's Washington allies turn to scrutiny

The FT says Huang, who lobbied hard against AI regulation and chip export bans, has lost credibility in Washington. He faces an FBI probe and bipartisan calls to brief lawmakers. China hawks are gathering support for tighter export rules, including on remote access to chips in South-east Asian data centres such as Megaspeed's. Democrats take Congress, and subpoena power, in January.

Financial Times · 12/11Read →
FT · Europe
Dublin

Europe rejects Putin's security overhaul demand

European leaders meeting Zelensky in Dublin rejected Russia's push to redraw the continent's security architecture as part of Ukraine peace talks. Zelensky warned that Washington and Moscow are already discussing it; Witkoff and Kushner met Putin on Thursday after talks in Abu Dhabi, where Russia refused to meet Europeans.
