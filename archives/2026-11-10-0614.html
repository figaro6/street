SoftBank rebounds hard before its results

SoftBank jumped to ¥3,869 in Tokyo on the global chip rally, hours before half-year results. The FT notes the shares had fallen about 60% since June, as had its biggest holding, Arm. Analysts estimate its loan-to-value ratio has risen to 18-22% from 13%, still below the 25% target. The $50bn SB Energy listing may be delayed, and SoftBank has about $65bn committed to OpenAI. Today's report will show how much the AI selloff has hurt.

Edition focus

Morning view: last night's close, Asia, and CPI day

How Wall Street closed

  • Best day in about seven months: S&P 500 +2.35%, Nasdaq 100 +3.4%.
  • Chips almost +7%; Nvidia −4.3% was the exception.
  • Energy the only losing sector as oil hit a 10-week low.
  • The Dow is back above its pre-midterm level.

After hours & Asia overnight

  • Take-Two about +4% after hours despite a soft guide; Rocket Lab about −7%.
  • Kospi +3.8%, Nikkei about +2%; SoftBank +13.8%.
  • China posts a $105bn October trade surplus (The Economist).
  • US futures flat; Nasdaq 100 contract −0.4%.

What to watch today

  • 15:30: US October CPI, with no 12-month rates published.
  • SoftBank half-year results; Anthropic may start IPO marketing.
  • US-Russia-Ukraine talks resume in Abu Dhabi.
  • After 23:00: CoreWeave results, with its bonds under stress.

Asia · overnight
+3.8%

Asia heads for its best two days in three months

MSCI's Asia-Pacific gauge rose 1.6%, led by Korea's Kospi (+3.8%) as chipmakers bounced back; Japan's Topix gained 1.7% and SoftBank 13.8%. Pepperstone's Dilin Wu says cheaper oil gave markets breathing room, but the questions about AI spending and returns have not gone away. Hong Kong and Shanghai barely moved.

US · futures
0.0%

Futures pause after the best session since April

S&P 500 futures are flat at 7,574 and Nasdaq 100 contracts are down 0.4% after Monday's dip-buying surge. Last week's rout was driven by fears that the new Democratic Congress will slow the AI build-out. The FT reports hedge funds cut leverage at the fastest pace since Covid during that selloff, which helps explain the violent rebound.

October CPI tests the Treasury rally

The 10-year yield slipped 2 bp to 4.86% in Asia before today's inflation report, which is expected to show smaller increases than September. The BLS will not publish 12-month headline or core rates this time. Cheaper oil helps the inflation outlook, but Fed officials sounded hawkish last week after strong October jobs data (165,000).

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 10 November

  • IranTrump said the Iran war is effectively over and new US strikes are highly unlikely, citing positive momentum after he met Qatar's prime minister. Iran's top security official rejected the claim.
  • UkraineUS, Russian and Ukrainian delegations meet in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday, the first talks in nine months. Hopes are low: Putin still wants all of the Donbas, and envoys Kushner and Witkoff face possible congressional scrutiny over alleged Russian business ties.
  • China tradeChina's October trade surplus was $105bn, putting this year on track to beat last year's record of almost $1.2trn. Cheaper oil cut its import bill and AI-related exports are booming.
  • US-ChinaChina extended its pause on higher tariffs and other measures against America by two months, so it will wait until at least January before raising tariffs by 24% or ending some rare-earth export waivers.
  • WashingtonRepublican congressman Thomas Massie reintroduced impeachment articles against war secretary Pete Hegseth, aiming to force a vote this week. They accuse him of starting the Iran war without authorisation.
  • RussiaRosneft restricted petrol sales across Russia after Ukraine resumed long-range strikes on refineries on 30 October; Ukraine recently hit Russia's second-largest refinery.

The tech selloff exposes SoftBank's AI bet

Before today's results, the FT says SoftBank is especially vulnerable because almost all its holdings sit in one AI ecosystem. A delay to Anthropic's or OpenAI's listings would hurt, and its credit default swaps have widened sharply after a record $11bn junk bond in September. Son is still pursuing a deal of almost $30bn for Allied Universal.

Exxon signed a memorandum of understanding with Venezuela and PDVSA to study upstream projects, months after its CEO called the country uninvestable. It says any investment needs stable rules and protections. Chevron plans to double output there in a $7bn plan, and Eni and GE have also signed deals since Maduro's capture.

Berkshire buys back a record amount of stock

Greg Abel spent a record $9.6bn on buybacks in the third quarter, even as operating income fell 5.8% on a weak Geico. Cash is still $357bn. Investors see Berkshire as a safe haven in the tech rout, and one CIO expects it to buy assets if the AI infrastructure trade implodes.
