Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

The chip selloff picked up again at the US open: a key semiconductor gauge fell 5% and the Nasdaq 100 lost about 1.5%, a day before Nvidia reports and days before Anthropic's listing. Tel Aviv followed, with Tower, Camtek and Nova down 5–9% and the TA-35 off 1.1%. The dollar rose above NIS 3.24, its highest in almost a year, as local institutions bought dollars to rebalance hedges, even though inflation has fallen to 1.1% and growth hit 8% in the third quarter.

Key numbers

Chips drag both Wall Street and Tel Aviv; the shekel slides

Number of the day
3.24

The dollar hits a near one-year high against the shekel

The dollar climbed above NIS 3.24 this afternoon, up about 0.9% on the day and its strongest since early December 2025. The Bank of Israel's representative rate was set at 3.223, against 3.199 on Friday. Calcalist explains the move by hedging: Israeli pension funds and insurers hold huge US stock portfolios, and when Wall Street falls they buy dollars to rebalance their currency hedges. That outweighed Sunday's surprise 0.1% drop in prices, which took inflation to 1.1%. With the rate decision a week away, Calcalist's Adrian Filut says a cut is now almost certain, while Bank Hapoalim's London investors still saw a hold.

The stories behind the numbers

US · open
−5%

Chipmakers get hammered again at the open

The tech selloff carried into a new week: a closely watched chip index fell 5% and the Nasdaq 100 1.6% in the first minutes, and the S&P 500 headed for a third losing day. Investors are positioning for Nvidia's results on Tuesday and Anthropic's debut later in the week. Europe held up, with the Stoxx 600 up 0.2%.

−2.5%

Washington tightens remote access to AI chips

The Commerce Department issued an interim rule limiting remote access to advanced AI chips and servers from certain countries, after the alleged espionage case linked to Nvidia. The shares fell about 2.5% early, Calcalist reports. Tuesday's results are the first under interim CEO Colette Kress, while Bessent repeats that the board should look outside the company.

The New York Fed's Empire State manufacturing index fell to 7.8 in November from 25.5 in October, according to Yahoo Finance's calendar. The index is still above zero, which signals growth, but the pace slowed a lot. Treasuries barely reacted; Tuesday's October retail sales are the week's bigger test for the Fed.

Wall Street's fall pushes the dollar above NIS 3.24

Israel · economy
+8%

Israel's economy grows 8% in Q3, helped by Nvidia

GDP grew 1.9% in the third quarter, an annualized 8%, the Central Bureau of Statistics said. Excluding production abroad by Israeli-registered companies, mainly Nvidia, growth was a still-solid 4%. Business output and exports drove the gain, while government spending fell as war costs eased.

Israel · banks
NIS 1.2bn

Hapoalim holds profit steady and keeps paying out

Bank Hapoalim earned NIS 2.4bn in the third quarter, 14% less than a year earlier but flat excluding a one-off gain; return on equity was 14%. It will pay out half the profit, NIS 1.2bn, and approved a new NIS 1bn buyback. The special bank tax and lower rates weighed. Separately, Isracard plans to cut 320–370 jobs after a 43% fall in core profit.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 16 November

  • NvidiaBessent insisted Nvidia must look outside the company for a new boss, as a Musk-backed campaign to bring back Jensen Huang gathers pace. Analysts expect about $400bn of revenue this financial year.

  • SpaceA ULA rocket carrying 40 Amazon internet satellites blew up after launch, a setback in Amazon's race with Starlink, while SpaceX returned Starship's second stage from orbit.

FT · Opinion
80%

Ruchir Sharma: the whole world is long America

Some 80% of global equity flows this year went into US stocks, double the long-term average, Sharma writes, driven by retail investors at home and abroad. Foreigners hold $25tn of US shares, more than a quarter of non-US GDP. By his estimate, a 25% fall in US stocks would cut US consumer spending by about 2% and by up to 1% in Europe, Japan, Canada and Korea.

Financial Times · 16/11Read →
FT · Unhedged
300

"The perfect sell-off": a bubble deflating gently

Since the Huang scandal broke on 29 October, the S&P 500 is down less than 2% and 300 of its 500 members are up, Robert Armstrong notes. Data-centre names are falling, but Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Visa and J&J are not. In his view, investors are leaving data-centre risk, not stocks in general. The newsletter also warns that fast-tracking Anthropic into the Nasdaq 100 forces index funds to buy before the price settles.

Financial Times · 16/11Read →
FT · IPOs
−20.5%

IPO fatigue hangs over Anthropic's debut

In each of the previous four years, the 10 biggest Wall Street IPOs ended the year above their offer prices, even in 2022. This year they are down a fifth. Fund managers blame a flood of equity and tech debt issuance and rich offer prices. Some say they prefer to wait and buy later, cheaper. Demand for Anthropic is still strong, and bankers have considered raising the price.
