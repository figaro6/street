Wednesday 25 Nov 2026 · 06:11 Tel Aviv
Morning edition · US close 23:00 · after-hours earnings · Asia overnight

Good morning, Tel Aviv.

Monday's Huang relief rally is gone. After Tuesday's 2% drop in the Nasdaq 100 and a 7.9% slide in chip stocks, Asia's AI barometers cracked overnight: Taiwan fell as much as 8.6% and Korea 6.4%, with TSMC, Samsung and SK Hynix down about 9% at their lows. US futures point to a second losing day for tech, while Treasuries rally and the 10-year yield slips to 4.90% ahead of PCE inflation at 15:30, the last big US release before Thanksgiving. In Tel Aviv, the exchange plans to widen its flagship index to 50 stocks, and the Bank of Israel governor says the weak shekel is why rates stay on hold.

The chip rout spreads to Asia

Number of the day
−40%

Chip stocks, from their June peak

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 7.9% on Tuesday, taking its loss since June to about 40%, bear-market territory for the engine of this year's rally. Overnight the selling moved to the AI supply chain in Asia: Taiwan's Taiex dropped as much as 8.6% and Korea's Kospi 6.4%, as Tuesday's Huang-driven gains were sold. Strategists quoted by Bloomberg called it a valuation reset and a positioning unwind at once; investors doubt the pace of AI spending can be funded.

The stories behind the numbers

Asia · AI trade
−8.6%

Huang's boost fades fast in Taiwan and Korea

Tuesday's Asian rally on Huang's reinstatement reversed sharply. The Taiex fell as much as 8.6% and TSMC briefly more than 9%; Korea's Kospi slid up to 6.4%, with Samsung and SK Hynix both down over 9%. MSCI's Asia-Pacific gauge lost 2.3%, putting world stocks on course for an eighth fall in ten sessions.

US · markets
−0.9%

Futures point to a second down day for tech

Nasdaq 100 futures fell about 0.9% and S&P 500 contracts 0.5%, after the cash index rallied from the edge of a bear market on Monday and then fell 2% on Tuesday. Investors moved into Treasuries, cutting the 10-year yield 3 bp to 4.90%. One strategist summed it up: the market is "turning on Nvidia's dime".

US · after hours
−40%

Ambarella collapses on a weak outlook

The chip designer guided fourth-quarter revenue to $70–75m, blaming customers pushing out orders as memory costs rise, and the shares fell more than 40% after hours on top of a 20% drop in the session. Autodesk raised the midpoint of its fiscal 2027 billings outlook to $8.625bn. Dell guided fourth-quarter revenue to $56.25bn and edged higher with HP.

US · inflation
3.8%

PCE test before Thanksgiving

Economists expect October's PCE price index at 3.8% a year, with core at 3.4%, when it comes out at 15:30 Tel Aviv. Revised third-quarter growth is seen slightly above 3.4% annualised. The Economist notes headline consumer prices have risen at a nearly 6% annualised pace over three months, and markets already price a December Fed hike.

The Economist · Bloomberg · 25/11Read →
US · trade
16

New tariffs on China and the EU are imminent

Washington may announce higher tariffs on goods from China, the EU and more than a dozen other economies as soon as late Tuesday, Bloomberg reported. Overall rates would not go above levels agreed in existing trade deals. HSBC's Asia economist thinks the move is mostly priced in, and China has extended some rare-earth exports to America until 27 January.

Israel · TASE
TA-50

Tel Aviv to widen its flagship indices

The TA-35 will become the TA-50, the TA-90 the TA-100 and the TA-125 the TA-150 from the May 2027 rebalancing, the first overhaul in a decade. Single-stock weight caps fall to 6% in the main index and 4% in the TA-150. Likely winners include YH Dimri, Shufersal, Fattal, Energix, Kenon, Shikun & Binui, Amot and Electra, as index funds buy in.

Globes · Calcalist · 24/11Read →
Israel · Bank of Israel
−7.6%

Yaron: the weak shekel keeps rates on hold

Inflation is down to 1.1%, near the bottom of the target, but Governor Amir Yaron told Globes the shekel's 7.6% fall against the dollar since the previous decision, and likely rate hikes abroad, will push prices up. He expects inflation to return to the middle of the range in 2027. Wider rate gaps with the rest of the world would add to the pressure.

Holders of the defence-tech company's NIS 46m bond, due in one payment in 2029, want controlling shareholder Tomer Avnon to inject equity; some favour demanding immediate repayment. Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 25 November

  • US inflationThe economy is strong, but prices are the problem: headline inflation has run near 6% annualised over three months and turkeys cost 9% more than a year ago. Today's PCE is expected at 3.8%, core 3.4%, with a December hike priced in.

Financial Times · key reads

FT · deals
$3.5trn

The five directors negotiating SpaceX–Tesla

Two special committees, three Tesla and two SpaceX directors, all with long ties to Musk, are negotiating an all-stock merger and aim to finish before year-end. Morgan Stanley and Goldman advise SpaceX, Evercore advises Tesla. The exchange ratio is the crux, watched closely by retail investors who own about 20% of Tesla, and lawsuits are the main risk.

Financial Times · 24/11Read →
FT · private credit
$512bn

The tiny ratings firm behind the private-credit boom

Egan-Jones rated more than 3,200 private deals in 2025 with about 25 analysts, and its founder calls it the "Tesla" of rating agencies. US insurers held $512bn of privately rated bonds at the end of 2025. A Chicago Fed study found such bonds about three notches riskier than publicly rated peers, and Blue Owl, Carlyle and Apollo exclude its ratings.

Financial Times · 24/11Read →
FT · regulation
$150k

Is the SEC cutting red tape or safeguards?

The SEC wants to cut pay disclosure from five executives to two and raise the threshold for reporting perks from $10,000 to $150,000. It also plans to drop mandatory quarterly reports and ease rules for smaller listed firms. The columnist argues that, days after Nvidia's governance crisis, weakening investor oversight sends the wrong message.
