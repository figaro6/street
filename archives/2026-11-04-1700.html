Wednesday 4 Nov 2026 · 17:00 Tel Aviv
Afternoon edition · Wall Street open 16:30, TASE close 17:25, Europe midday

Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street opened higher on Wednesday after Democrats won both the House and the Senate: the S&P 500 gained about 1.6% and the Dow 2.2% in the first half hour, the S&P's best day in three months. Treasury yields fell, the dollar weakened, gold topped $4,000 and oil slipped. AMD's strong forecast helped chips, and Amazon jumped 7%, while Tesla and SpaceX fell on Texas's new Democratic governor. In Tel Aviv the TA-35 was down about 2.2% shortly before the close, dragged by financials, with Amdocs plunging in New York on a T-Mobile dispute. The shekel recovered to about 3.157 per dollar.

Number of the day
+1.6%

S&P 500 jumps after Democrats sweep Congress, best day in three months

Stocks reversed early losses overnight and rallied once it became clear Democrats had taken both chambers. Strategists quoted by Bloomberg see divided government as good for markets: gridlock limits new policy, and political uncertainty is gone. The Treasury also revised its debt-sales guidance, which may open the way to smaller long-bond issuance in 2027, and yields fell across the curve. The Nasdaq 100 gained less than the Dow, as investors weigh tougher scrutiny of AI and data-centre building. In Tel Aviv the mood was the opposite: the TA-35 fell about 2.2% before the close.

Edition focus

Wall Street's open, Tel Aviv into the close, Europe at midday

Wall Street's opening moves

  • After early losses, stocks rallied: S&P 500 +1.6%, Dow +2.2%, small caps +2% (Bloomberg).
  • Amazon +7% and Microsoft +4.5% led; Tesla and SpaceX fell about 5% after Democrats won the Texas governor's race (Calcalist).
  • AMD forecast fourth-quarter revenue near $17bn, above estimates; Super Micro lifted its outlook.
  • Yesterday's pre-open data: private payrolls beat forecasts, the September trade deficit narrowed.

  • TA-35 −2.2%, TA-125 −2.1% at 16:43, with the close at 17:25.

Europe and what to watch

  • Stoxx 600 +1.8% at midday; European banks bounced, defence stocks slid (Bloomberg headlines).

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 4 November

  • MidtermsDemocrats took the Senate and the House. James Talarico won a Senate seat in Texas, the first Democratic win there since 1988, and Kansas elected a Democratic senator for the first time in almost a century. Gerrymandering by Republicans backfired in Texas. The party can now subpoena members of the administration.
  • TrumpThe president said "I wasn't on the ballot" and told the New York Times he would accept the results, perhaps a sign of how comprehensively Republicans were routed.
  • MarketsThe dollar weakened while the Swiss franc and yen rose. Asian AI stocks slid overnight on fears of more scrutiny, and Tesla and SpaceX fell about 4%, but US futures and European shares recovered by morning.
  • Israel aidStill in play: Trump said America will stop the roughly $4bn of annual military aid at year-end; the existing agreement runs to 2028. Netanyahu recently lost his parliamentary majority.
  • BinanceHackers who stole $6.3bn used a fake job interview and software hidden in an advert to breach two engineers' computers, in a multi-stage operation since June. The FBI blames North Korea-linked hackers, and it may be the largest financial theft ever.
  • AI chipsAMD reassured investors about the AI build-out with $13bn of third-quarter revenue and a forecast of $17bn for the fourth quarter, doubling in 2027.
  • UkraineRussia struck two Ukrainian power stations, near Kyiv and in Zhytomyr, in its campaign against energy infrastructure as temperatures fall; Ukraine hit Russian logistics and data centres.
