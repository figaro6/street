Thursday 26 Nov 2026 · 17:05 Tel Aviv
Afternoon edition · TASE late session · Europe afternoon · Wall Street shut for Thanksgiving

Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

With Wall Street closed for Thanksgiving, Tel Aviv had the stage to itself and used it: the TA-125 jumped about 3%, led by construction, cleantech and tech stocks, reversing Wednesday's sell-off. The shekel rebounded just as hard, with the dollar falling below NIS 3.30 from 3.39 yesterday, although holiday trading was thin. Europe's Stoxx 600 rose 1.7% as the AI trade came back and the new US tariffs on Europe looked milder than feared. US futures are up about 0.7%, but the real test of the rebound comes on Friday's shortened session.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

Israel · TASE
+7.8%

Construction and cleantech lead a broad rally

The rally widened through the afternoon: the TA-35 rose 2.6% and the TA-125 about 3%, with construction up 7.8%, cleantech 6.4% and tech 4.3%. It follows Wednesday's sell-off and tracks the global AI rebound. Nofar Energy jumped 17% after reporting deals to bring partners into its projects.

Calcalist · 26/11Read →
Israel · shekel
3.30

The shekel's sharp rebound, on thin volume

The dollar fell below NIS 3.30 from 3.39 on Wednesday, when Globes reported the shekel at a 14-month low, and the euro dropped to under NIS 3.63 from 3.70. Calcalist notes that the Bank of Israel limits foreign-exchange activity on US holidays and global FX moves are small, so the size of the swing partly reflects thin trading. A stronger shekel and rising stocks usually go together in Tel Aviv.

Calcalist · Globes · 26/11Read →
Israel · telecoms M&A
NIS 500m

Cellcom in exclusive talks to buy Icon

Cellcom has exclusivity to negotiate buying all of Icon Group, owner of iDigital, for about NIS 500m against a market value of NIS 424m, through a tender offer that would delist it. Only a day earlier Icon reported a binding offer from Benda Magnetic for up to 20% of its shares. The competition authority is expected to focus on the overlap between iDigital and Cellcom's Dynamica.

Calcalist · 26/11Read →
Israel · real estate
NIS 3bn

Dunitz Elad agrees to buy Dan Real Estate

Dan's shareholders will get about a third of Dunitz Elad's shares, warrants worth around NIS 30m and NIS 30–50m in cash; the combined group is valued at about NIS 3bn. The deal lands as developers report weaker sales: Dimri sold only 125 flats in the third quarter, 37% fewer than a year earlier. Real estate stocks rose 5.6% today.

Calcalist · 26/11Read →
Israel · earnings
−21%

Shufersal's profit falls; new discount format

Israel's largest supermarket chain earned NIS 132m in the third quarter, 21% less than a year earlier, as revenue slipped 4.6% to NIS 3.55bn. It blamed disruption to its online service after a delivery overhaul and a 21% rise in Israelis travelling abroad. It will launch a "Hyper Neto" format in 20 branches, starting in Kiryat Motzkin.

Calcalist · 26/11Read →
Israel · energy
NIS 84m

Nofar says it can grow without the market

Ofer Yannay's Nofar Energy swung to a NIS 84m third-quarter profit as revenue from power generation more than tripled. It plans to fund growth by bringing in partners and selling assets rather than raising equity or debt on TASE. It will also buy back NIS 200m of its own bonds to swap expensive traded debt for cheaper private loans.

Calcalist · 26/11Read →
Israel · politics
2020

Judges urge dropping some Netanyahu charges

The three judges hearing the caretaker prime minister's corruption trial, under way since 2020, told the attorney-general that a bribery conviction was unlikely and urged prosecutors to drop some charges. Globes reports the proposal covers two of the three cases. Netanyahu said the cases were collapsing; the move comes as Gadi Eisenkot tries to form a government before his mandate ends on 6 December.

The Economist · Globes · 26/11Read →
US · holiday trading
+0.7%

Futures rise, but the rebound needs Friday

S&P 500 futures are up 0.7% and Nasdaq 100 futures 0.6%, extending Wednesday's late recovery from the edge of a bear market. State Street argues sentiment cracked while fundamentals did not; Panmure Liberum's Joachim Klement calls the dip buying a mystery and sees Friday's session as the real test. A likely Fed hike next month remains a headwind for AI stocks.

Bloomberg · 26/11Read →
Europe · stocks & ECB
+1.7%

Europe rallies; de Cos to replace Lagarde

The Stoxx 600 rose 1.7%, led by miners and tech, and banks gained on hopes that US tariffs on Europe will be lower than first expected: Deutsche Bank, SocGen and Barclays rose about 3%. Spain's Pablo Hernández de Cos will become ECB president when Lagarde leaves in February. Bloomberg reports that ECB officials saw a case for more tightening as the inflation outlook worsened.

Financial Times · key reads

FT · chips & China
$104bn

Nvidia halts server shipments to south-east Asia

Partners such as Dell and Supermicro have told buyers in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia that Nvidia will not let their orders ship, the FT reports, as it works out how to comply with new US export rules aimed at Chinese firms renting AI compute. The region matters: Nvidia booked $104bn of revenue through its Singapore unit in 2025, up 72%. It is unclear whether the halt is temporary.

Financial Times · 26/11Read →
FT · Opinion
40 yrs

Huang's return is a bad sign for US capitalism

Governance veteran Nell Minow argues that Huang's reinstatement shows how shareholder rights are being eroded in America: the ability to file proposals, to sue management and to see executive pay is being chipped away by governments, regulators and exchanges. For investors, her point is that weaker oversight should mean a higher risk premium. It echoes S&P's concern about board independence.

Financial Times · 26/11Read →
FT · Markets
≈ 0%

Software is spared as chips take the hit

Software stocks, dumped earlier this year on fears AI would kill their business models, have held up in November's rout, which has fallen mainly on semiconductors. Both S&P 500 sub-indices are now slightly negative for the year. Deutsche Bank's Max Uleer says sentiment on chips now looks like sentiment on software six to nine months ago.
