Good night, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street had its best day since April 2025 after Nvidia's board brought Jensen Huang back as chief executive. Nvidia closed 31.5% higher, its biggest gain since 2003; the Nasdaq 100 rose 6.9% and the S&P 500 4.7%, wiping out Friday's rout. Tel Aviv turned around late to close 0.5% higher, led by chip stocks, and the dollar slid 1.5% to 3.31 shekels. The question for tomorrow: whether a rebound built on one man's return can survive a Fed that still plans to hike in December.

The best day in 19 months

Number of the day
+31.5%

Nvidia's close after Huang's return, its best day since 2003

Nvidia ended at $165.05 after its board reinstated Huang following meetings and input from senior government officials; Trump said the move posed no security risk. The gain pulled the chip index up 14% and the whole market with it. Even so, the FT notes the stock is still about $500bn below its value on 7 November, when the scandal first broke, and senators from both parties have criticised the decision.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · stocks
+4.7%

Stocks have their best day since April 2025

The S&P 500 snapped a seven-day losing streak and the Nasdaq 100, which was near a bear market last week, gained 6.9%. Strategists quoted by Bloomberg said Huang's return removes a key uncertainty over the AI trade; one called the sell-off a crisis of confidence rather than a growth shock. The S&P is still about 7% below its October record.

Bloomberg · 23/11Read →
US · Nvidia
Backlash

Washington cheers Huang's return, Congress pushes back

Trump took credit for the reversal, calling Huang a national treasure. Republican senators including John Cornyn questioned the board, and a Democratic senator accused Trump of trading security for a high stock market. Bloomberg reports the board acted without consulting management (headline), and the US plans to blacklist Megaspeed, the Singapore firm at the centre of the spying scandal.

The Economist · Bloomberg · 23/11Read →
US · strategy
2018

Citadel Securities warns of a late-2018-style trap

Its strategists say a rising dollar, sinking cyclicals and a hawkish Fed echo late 2018, when the S&P fell almost 20% in three months. Unlike then, core inflation runs above 3%, so investors should not expect weak stocks to bring rate relief. The risk they flag is a Fed tightening into a slowing AI investment cycle.

Bloomberg · 23/11Read →
US · strategy
Most favourable

Wells Fargo upgrades US large caps after the rout

The Wells Fargo Investment Institute raised big US stocks to its top rating and advised moving money out of medium-term bonds. It called the sell-off a test of confidence, not an earnings reset, and sees 7,000 on the S&P as a level where upside outweighs downside. It favours industrials, materials and communication services, and advises buying gradually.

Bloomberg · 23/11Read →
US · earnings
−6%

HPE beats and raises, but slips after a 17% day

Hewlett Packard Enterprise reported fourth-quarter revenue of $14.5bn, up 50% on a year earlier, and adjusted EPS of $1.43, above its own guidance. It raised its fiscal 2027 outlook. After gaining 17% in the session on the AI hardware rally, the stock fell about 6% after hours. Agilent rose 7.6% after its results.

Yahoo Finance · company release · 23/11Read →

−8.9%

CrowdStrike falls, Zscaler soars on their $44bn deal

Investors marked down the buyer and marked up the target on the all-stock takeover, one of the largest in cyber-security: Zscaler rose 17.7%. Amazon gained 3.5% after confirming its Decart deal. Short-leveraged funds betting against Nvidia lost up to 92% (headline).

Tel Aviv erases its losses late, led by chips

The TA-35 closed at 3,880.45 after spending much of the day in the red. Tower Semiconductor rose 8.6%, Camtek 5.4% and Nova 4.8%; the tech index gained 1.4% and banks 0.9%. NextVision fell 3.6%, Elbit 1.9% and Harel 2.8% after weak quarterly results. Equity turnover was NIS 5.1bn.

Israel · shekel
3.31

The shekel's best day in weeks

The dollar fell 1.5% to about 3.31 shekels and the euro 2.1%, after a Bank of Israel rate hold and the Wall Street rally. The representative rate, set at midday, was still 3.343. As Calcalist explained this afternoon, rising US stocks push Israeli institutions over their foreign-currency targets, so they sell dollars.

Israel · politics
6 Dec

Eisenkot asks the Treasury to price coalition demands

Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar party, asked the budget director to assign an official to the coalition talks to cost the emerging agreements. His deadline to present a government is 6 December. Calcalist says this matters because of Haredi demands for funding and draft exemptions, and Arab parties' demands for billions for their communities.

Decart chose Amazon over Anthropic

Globes reports that Decart's founders had earlier turned down an all-share offer from Anthropic, based on its pre-IPO valuation, before talks collapsed. The Amazon deal should close in January, pending approvals; the founders will stay five years. Globes weighs which offer would have paid more, now that Anthropic's shares have fallen to near their IPO price.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 23 November

  • NvidiaShares jumped 31% on Huang's reinstatement, lifting chip stocks worldwide. Lawmakers from both parties criticised the comeback, and Huang is due before Congress next week to answer questions about the spying scandal.
  • RussiaGerman media, citing Western intelligence, reported that Russia could launch a nuclear-armed anti-satellite weapon within weeks. Germany's chancellor called the reports shocking; Russia denies it.
  • CyberCrowdStrike's all-stock deal values Zscaler at $43.9bn, following other large deals such as Alphabet's $32bn purchase of Wiz, amid fears after recent outages.

  • BritainBuilding data centres pushed UK factory output to its highest since August 2024, but the flash composite PMI dipped to 52.3.

Financial Times · key reads

FT · opinion
−$500bn

Huang is back because he left Nvidia no choice

The columnist argues Huang returned not because he was the best candidate but because he never built an independent board, a chair or a succession plan, leaving all lines of authority running to him. Even after today's surge, Nvidia is worth roughly $500bn less than before the scandal broke on 7 November.

Financial Times · 23/11Read →
FT · IPOs
$12.2bn

Did Anthropic's bankers exercise the greenshoe too early?

A former Bank of America capital-markets chief asks whether underwriters erred by exercising the over-allotment option after one day. It raised $12.2bn more for Anthropic, but left no short position to support the price. On Friday the stock came within five cents of its $83 offer price; it closed today at $87.64.

Financial Times · 23/11Read →
FT · asset management
FCA

Fund managers say they were fired for avoiding Big Tech

Britain's regulator has received complaints from managers dropped by pension trustees for underweighting US tech and missing its returns. The FCA plans to remind trustees that managers should not be judged on returns alone. It is a sign of how concentrated portfolios have become, just as AI stocks turn volatile.

Financial Times · 23/11Read →
FT · Middle East
Hormuz

The Gulf looks normal, but the war is leaving scars

Daily life in Dubai, Kuwait and Doha looks ordinary, but bankers and business people say trade systems are strained by the US-Iran stand-off over the Strait of Hormuz, which the region relies on for oil exports and food. They worry the conflict will hold back the Gulf's diversification plans and its image as a haven of stability.
