Afternoon edition · Wall Street's open, TASE into the close, Europe's afternoon, 17:15

Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street opened higher at 16:30 and has held its gains: the S&P 500 is up 0.6% and the Nasdaq 1.0% as Brent fell 6.6% to about $106 and WTI to $97 on hopes of a US–Iran deal. The FT, however, reports that Netanyahu is pressing Trump to resume strikes on Iran after Tuesday's midterms, so the oil drop rests on talks, not supply. Tel Aviv is the odd one out: the TA-35 is down about 1% at the close as Tower Semiconductor, which beat forecasts and guided Q4 revenue to $600 million, fell 14% and Check Point dropped about 10% on weak results. ISM manufacturing came in at 55.5, above the 54.6 forecast.

US · data
55.5

ISM manufacturing beats forecasts at 55.5

The index rose from 54.5 in September and beat the 54.6 consensus, a tenth straight month of growth. Four of the five sub-indexes, including new orders and production, grew faster, while employment growth slowed. Strong factory data fits a Fed that is still debating how many more hikes are needed, as Bloomberg's headline video suggests.

US · M&A
$115bn

Marvell buys Lumentum in one of tech's biggest-ever mergers

Marvell will pay $1,132 a share in stock, a 23.9% premium to Lumentum's 28 October close, and expects to close in the first half of 2028. The FT calls it the largest deal ever between two listed tech companies and a bet on optical links for AI data centers. Lumentum fell about 4% and Marvell 1.5% before the open, a sign investors still worry about AI spending.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 2 November

  • West BankIsrael's army withdrew from Hebron, ending the operation it began after gunmen killed two Israeli soldiers in late October. Homes of the gunmen were demolished and more than 3,000 Palestinians were displaced. The army says counterterrorism activity will continue across the West Bank.
  • Tech dealMarvell agreed an all-stock, $115bn purchase of Lumentum, one of the biggest tech mergers ever. Investors, nervous about an AI bubble, sold both shares in premarket trading.
  • CryptoBinance users pulled a net $3.6bn after the exchange reopened, about 2% of its holdings. The $6.3bn theft is believed to be North Korean; withdrawals are limited and service restarts in phases.
  • MidtermsHaley Stevens, who lost Michigan's Democratic primary, endorsed Republican Mike Rogers for the Senate. The Economist's model gives Democrats a 94% chance of the House and 53% of the Senate.
  • CyberEmergency-alert apps in Russia and Ukraine were reportedly hacked, with a false "nuclear alert" in Ukraine. Separately, supermarket freezers overheated in a suspected cyberattack in Belgium and Sweden.
