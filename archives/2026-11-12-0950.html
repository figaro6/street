Nvidia slides after hours as the board weighs ousting Huang

Nvidia closed Wednesday up 2.9% at $173.73, swept up in the chip rally. After the bell the Wall Street Journal reported that the board was preparing to remove Jensen Huang after several affairs, one of them with a woman reportedly suspected of spying for China. The New York Times also reported a relationship with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's head of healthcare. The shares fell to about $159.95 in extended trading, and Bloomberg says the company is working with an outside firm to find possible successors. The Economist cites speculation that CFO Colette Kress could step in as interim CEO.

The stories behind the numbers

US · the close
+2.3%

CoreWeave and OpenAI revive the AI trade

The Nasdaq 100 rose 2.3% and the S&P 500 1.3% on Wednesday, with chip stocks leading. CoreWeave's upbeat outlook drove the move, helped by reports that OpenAI's quarterly revenue had reached $10bn and that Anthropic's IPO was oversubscribed several times over. Last week tech had sold off on fears that the Democrats' sweep of Congress would bring tougher oversight of AI.

Nvidia's board prepares to remove Jensen Huang

The Wall Street Journal reports that the board is ready to oust its 63-year-old founder after several affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset. The New York Times reported a separate relationship with an Nvidia vice-president. Nvidia has no published succession plan. Bloomberg says it has hired an outside firm to identify candidates, and Semafor names CFO Colette Kress as a possible interim chief (SA headline).

Israel · Teva
$6-7bn

Teva raises its 2030 target for innovative drugs

At its R&D Day, Teva raised its 2030 revenue goal for branded innovative medicines from about $5bn to $6-7bn. That compares with $3.77-3.92bn expected this year, about a fifth of sales. CEO Richard Francis named duvakitug, a phase-3 drug for inflammatory bowel disease, as a possible next flagship, and said Teva will not sell its generics arm. The shares rose 2% in New York.

FT · Nvidia
$4.2tn

Nvidia's board to decide its founder's fate

The board held urgent talks on Wednesday night after news of Huang's relationship with Kimberly Powell, an 18-year Nvidia veteran and its vice-president for healthcare. That came days after he admitted an affair with an executive at an Nvidia client whom US officials believe is a Chinese intelligence asset. The FT notes that an affair with a subordinate takes the scandal at the $4.2tn company to a new level.

Financial Times · 11-12/11Read →
FT · Lex
$1.4tn

Lex: what losing Huang could cost Nvidia

Bank of America expects $930bn of Nvidia gross profit in 2030, assuming a 72% GPU market share and a 73% margin. If share falls to 50% and the margin to 65%, Lex's rough maths cuts that by $300bn, or about $1.4tn of market value: 30% of the pre-scandal worth. By Tuesday the shares had fallen only about a third as much. Past CEO exits over workplace relationships, at McDonald's and Intel, cost those shares less than 3%.

Financial Times · 11/11Read →
FT · Anthropic
$1.5tn+

Anthropic's bankers weigh a higher IPO price

Heavy demand has bankers signalling a price above the $70-78 range filed on Tuesday, which could lift the valuation to $1.5tn or more and the total raised above $100bn. Backers say Anthropic is focused on raising as much cash as possible for computing power, rather than on maximising its valuation. The listing is due next week.

Financial Times · 11/11Read →
FT · oil
10.6mn

Saudi oil output is back near prewar levels

Riyadh told OPEC it pumped 10.6mn barrels a day in October, its most since the US and Israel went to war with Iran at the end of February. That is above the average of the two months before the war. Iran's control of the Strait of Hormuz had collapsed Gulf shipments and forced producers with little storage to cut output. More Gulf supply helps explain why Brent is stuck in the low $90s.
