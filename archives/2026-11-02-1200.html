Midday edition · TASE morning session, Europe open and US futures at 12:05

Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

Oil is still the driver: Brent is down 4.5% at about $108 and WTI has dropped below $100, on reports that Trump wants to give Iran diplomacy more time and that Iran's Doha talks drafted a plan to reopen Hormuz. US futures are up (S&P 500 +0.5%, Nasdaq 100 +0.9%) after Friday's tech rout, though ING and others call the bounce modest. In Tel Aviv the TA-35 is roughly flat after Friday's 2% fall, with Perion up about 31% on a $420 million takeover bid from India's Affle and Tower Semiconductor reporting today. The shekel has strengthened to 3.13 per dollar.

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 2 November

  • Iran talksIranian negotiators held a third day of talks with Qatari mediators in Doha. Foreign minister Araghchi says the draft plan would reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days of a deal. Trump says he won't strike before Tuesday's midterms but might afterwards. Oil fell on the news.

Junk-debt rout turns into a "bloodbath" for credit investors

Yields on CCC-rated debt, the riskiest tier, have jumped above 21% in Europe and 18% in the US, from about 16% and 15% at the start of the year. Rising global rates are amplifying fears about how indebted companies cope with AI disruption and high energy prices, and France's fiscal worries add to the pressure in Europe.
