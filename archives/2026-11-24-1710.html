Tuesday 24 Nov 2026 · 17:10 Tel Aviv
Afternoon edition · Wall Street open 16:30 · TASE into the 17:25 close

Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

Monday's Nvidia euphoria lasted one session. Wall Street opened lower, with the Nasdaq 100 down about 1.5% and Nvidia off around 6%, after reports that Washington is preparing new tariffs on China, the EU and dozens of other economies. Anthropic slipped below its $83 IPO price for the first time. In Tel Aviv, Tower and the other chip stocks gave back gains and the shekel weakened to about 3.33 per dollar. Bonds are still the calm corner, with the 10-year Treasury at 4.91%.

$83

Anthropic's IPO price, now broken

Anthropic fell as much as 8.4% to $80.25 in early New York trading, below the $83 at which it listed last Thursday, the FT reports. News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · trade
Tariffs

Washington prepares new tariffs on China, the EU and others

The Wall Street Journal reports that the US is getting ready to raise tariffs on China and dozens of other countries, aimed at foreign industrial subsidies. Bloomberg's Markets Wrap headline blamed returning trade fears for the slide; Calcalist and Bizportal said it was a reopening of the tariff war. Details, rates and timing were not in the short report we could read.

US · Nvidia
143 bp

Nvidia's credit risk sits near a record

Nvidia's five-year credit default swaps stood near 142.7 bp early on Tuesday, just under last week's one-year high and more than double the one-year average of about 62 bp. The stock fell around 6% after the open, a small part of Monday's 31% jump, and is still about 35% below its October record. Michael Burry has reportedly cut his Nvidia puts by 75%.

Seeking Alpha · FT · 24/11Read →
US · earnings
−12%

HPE slumps on an AI-server margin warning

Hewlett Packard Enterprise fell 12% before the open after its finance chief warned that rising sales of AI servers could squeeze margins in the second half of the fiscal year. The headline guidance had beaten average estimates last night. Bloomberg's most-active list showed the stock down about 14%.

US · earnings
−10%

Dell drops 10% ahead of tonight's results

Dell traded near $300 in early dealing, down about $35, before its third-quarter report after the close (after 23:00 Tel Aviv). HPE's margin warning on AI servers hit the read-across. Workday, Autodesk and HP also report tonight.

Seeking Alpha · 24/11Read →
US · macro
GRAB

BofA tells clients not to ignore hard-landing risk

Bank of America argues that recession risk is heavily underpriced heading into 2027. Its hedge is "GRAB" assets: government bonds, real estate, AAA-rated stocks and bullion. The falling Treasury yields this week fit that view.

Seeking Alpha · 24/11Read →
Israel · TASE
−7.6%

Tel Aviv's chip stocks give back Monday's surge

Tower was the most traded stock, falling about 7.6% on roughly NIS 200m of turnover, a day after an 18% jump in New York. Camtek and Nova also fell more than 5%, and El Al dropped about 6% on a fare war on New York routes. Raga fell 18% after its gross margin shrank, and Beit Shemesh fell 11% as growth slowed. Plasson rose 14% after a strong quarter.

Bizportal · Globes · 24/11Read →
Israel · indices
TA-50

TASE plans to widen its flagship indices

In its first overhaul in a decade, the exchange proposes turning the TA-35 into the TA-50, the TA-90 into the TA-100 and the TA-125 into the TA-150. Stocks near the cut-off, such as YH Dimri and Shufersal, would benefit most from index-fund buying. Small caps jumped on the news, with the SME60 up 3.8%, and TASE's own shares rose about 3.5%.

Globes · Bizportal · 24/11Read →
Israel · Bank of Israel
−7.6%

Yaron: the shekel's slide keeps rates on hold

Inflation has fallen to 1.1%, near the bottom of the 1–3% target, yet the Bank of Israel held at 3.25% for a second time. Governor Amir Yaron told Globes that the shekel has lost 7.6% against the dollar since the previous decision, which could reignite price pressures, and that global rate hikes and uncertainty argued for caution after five cuts.

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 24 November

  • NvidiaHuang's reinstatement, for which Trump took credit, sent the shares up 31% and added nearly $1trn of value, before they fell in pre-market trading on Tuesday.

Financial Times · key reads

FT · markets
Squeeze

Monday "looked very much like a short squeeze"

The FT's markets team says Monday's leap was driven by funds covering huge short positions, much of it automated. Man Group's Dan Taylor warns that some of the biggest rallies happen in bear markets, and Cavendish notes the market's new dependence on one CEO may itself raise AI concerns. Asian brokers expect the rebound to be limited.

Financial Times · 24/11Read →
FT · Unhedged
$1trn

The Jensen put

Unhedged notes that Asian authorities were rushing out emergency support, from state buying to short-selling curbs, as the sell-off threatened to spread. Reinstating Huang did the job faster, making him a "$1tn man". The newsletter is more worried about the signal it sends to executives in sectors vital to national security.

Financial Times · 24/11Read →
FT · M&A
$3.5tn

The five directors negotiating SpaceX–Tesla

Two SpaceX and three Tesla directors with long Musk ties are running special committees on an all-stock merger, advised by Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs and Evercore. The exchange ratio is the crux, and Tesla needs more than 50% of all possible votes. China, national security and antitrust are the hurdles; insiders say a deal could still come before year-end.

Financial Times · 24/11Read →
FT · credit
$512bn

The tiny ratings agency behind private credit

Egan-Jones rated more than 3,200 private deals in 2025 with about 25 analysts, helping US insurers build $512bn of privately rated bonds. Chicago Fed economists found such bonds are about three notches riskier than publicly rated peers, and Blue Owl, Carlyle and Apollo exclude its ratings. Its founder calls the critics Luddites and credits technology.
