Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

Dip buyers are back. Half an hour into the session the S&P 500 is up 1.4% and the Nasdaq 100 2.5%, with chipmakers up 3.5%, even as Nvidia slides 5.6% over the spy scandal around Jensen Huang. Cheaper oil helps: Brent has fallen 4.4% to $94 after Trump said new strikes on Iran are "highly unlikely". The shekel is at 3.159 per dollar, its strongest in weeks, and the TA-125 is up 1.3% into the close, led by NextVision and insurers.

Number of the day
$94.02

Brent's 4.4% drop takes pressure off inflation

Brent fell $4.30 from Friday's settlement, extending last week's 16% slide, its worst since 2020. Trump told CNN a new wave of strikes on Iran is "highly unlikely" if talks on its nuclear programme and the Strait of Hormuz progress, though Rubio played down hopes of fast progress. Cheaper energy eased inflation worries on the eve of US CPI: the 10-year Treasury yield is 4.85%, gilts and Bunds rallied, and the shekel gained almost 1%. Edition focus

Afternoon view: Wall Street's open, rates and Tel Aviv's close

Wall Street's first minutes

  • Broad rebound: S&P 500 +1.4%, Nasdaq 100 +2.5%, chip gauge +3.5%.
  • Nvidia −5.6% is the outlier; AMD +5.7% after an Oppenheimer upgrade.
  • Alphabet +3.8% as Google widens its AI chip line-up against Nvidia.
  • JPMorgan sees signs of capitulation after the biggest momentum unwind in ~40 years.

US data & rates

  • No major US data at 15:30 today; September wholesale inventories were revised up a notch.
  • Tuesday 15:30: October CPI, the last before the December Fed meeting.
  • 2-year yield +3 bp to 4.54%; 10-year 4.85% as oil falls.
  • UBS (via Bizportal) expects two Fed hikes and favours short, high-quality bonds.

Tel Aviv into the close & Europe

  • TA-125 +1.3%; insurers +3.6%, defence stocks +2.4%, banks lag.
  • NextVision ~+10% on results; Palo Alto −4% and Tower −5% on arbitrage.
  • Turnover passed NIS 3.1bn an hour before the close.
  • Stoxx 600 +1.0%; gilts rally 5 bp; Merz threatens the EU budget over Ukraine aid.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · Wall Street open
+2.5%

Dip buyers return after the AI rout

The Nasdaq 100 is up 2.5% and the S&P 500 1.4% half an hour into trading, with the VIX down 13% to 22. JPMorgan's strategists say last week's momentum unwind was the biggest in about 40 years and see signs of investor capitulation. E*Trade calls it a reshuffle within the AI trade rather than an exit. UBS suggests investors who are overweight tech use rallies to rebalance.

US · Nvidia
−5.6%

Nvidia slides as the spy story deepens

Nvidia fell as much as 7% early on after its board opened a probe into Huang's affair. The FT reports that Alice Huang, the woman US officials suspect of spying for China, pitched a south-east Asia data-centre deal to Chinese investors in 2025, saying she had "no problem securing GPUs". She cited Alibaba as a client and did not disclose her ties to Huang. China's foreign ministry calls the spy claims groundless.

+5.7%

AMD, Alphabet and Tesla lead the bounce

AMD is up 5.7% after Oppenheimer upgraded it to outperform following what it called another bullish earnings call. Alphabet gains 3.8% as Google rolls out more versions of its TPU AI chips to challenge Nvidia. Tesla is up 5.1%; Musk says it will return many Cybertruck deposits. McDonald's will favour Uber over DoorDash for app delivery in 14 countries.

US · IPO
$1.1-1.3tn

Anthropic can start marketing its IPO on Tuesday

The 15-day waiting period after Anthropic's public filing ends, so it can begin roadshow meetings as early as Tuesday and list on Nasdaq the week of 16 November. Potential investors have discussed a $1.1-1.3tn valuation, well below last month's talk. Retail buyers may get up to 20% of the shares. Bloomberg says slower quarterly revenue growth and data-centre politics are the main worries.

US · Fed & CPI
+0.3%

Tuesday's CPI is unlikely to stop a December hike

RBC expects October core CPI to rise 0.3% on the month, citing accelerating goods prices from clothes to toys; there will be no annual rate because of data gaps from last year's shutdown. Citi says the report should not change expectations of a December Fed hike. The 2-year yield is up 3 bp to 4.54%. Bizportal reports UBS now expects two hikes.

3.159

Dollar slides against the shekel

The dollar fell 0.9% to 3.159 shekels on Bloomberg, and Calcalist reports 3.16, as the dollar weakens worldwide and risk appetite returns. The euro is down 0.4% at 3.537. The shekel hit a seven-month low last week, when the dollar topped 3.22 during the tech rout. Cheaper oil and rising Israeli stocks both support the currency.

Israel · budget
3.3%

Israel's deficit widens on defence spending

The 12-month deficit rose to 3.3% of GDP in October, or NIS 74bn, from 3.2% in September, the Accountant General reported. October alone showed a NIS 18.3bn gap. Revenue is up 10% so far this year against 4.2% growth in spending, but defence spending climbed again; Bizportal puts the jump near 10%. This comes as the 2027 budget awaits the next coalition.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 9 November

  • NvidiaNvidia's shares fell as markets reacted to the spying scandal. The board is probing reports that Huang had a relationship with a suspected Chinese spy; he admitted an affair, but the firm says it has no sign secrets were compromised.
  • IranTrump says a new wave of strikes on Iran is "highly unlikely" if talks on its nuclear programme and Hormuz progress. Many had expected attacks to resume after midterms in which the war hurt Republicans. He also admitted his immigration crackdown was "too tough".
  • US-ChinaChina extended its suspension of higher tariffs and other measures against America by two months. It will wait until at least January before raising tariffs on US goods by 24%, charging port fees on US ships or ending some rare-earth export waivers.
  • KKRKKR posted record quarterly profit: adjusted net income rose 29% to $1.65bn on higher fees and dividends from its holdings, offsetting weaker insurance profits.
  • Middle EastJordan and Syria will jointly investigate a Jordanian airstrike that killed civilians in Syria last month, a sign of warming ties since Assad's fall.
  • UkraineFigure of the day: Ukraine's fertility rate has fallen to 0.8-0.9 children per woman, from 1.2 before the war.

FT · Nvidia scoop
$3bn

The woman in the Nvidia scandal sold GPU access

Alice Huang and a partner who sits on a Chinese state bank's board pitched a data-centre project worth several hundred million dollars, stressing easy access to Nvidia chips. Megaspeed, the Singapore firm she was linked to, saw its balance sheet grow from $33m to $3bn in 2024, partly on an Alibaba leasing deal and loan. Megaspeed is under investigation over alleged chip smuggling, which it denies.

FT · Japan & AI
−60%

The AI selloff exposes SoftBank's all-in bet

SoftBank shares, and its Arm stake, are down about 60% since June ahead of Tuesday's results. The planned $50bn listing of SB Energy, its US data-centre power arm, may be delayed after Democrats' gains in Texas politics. Analysts put its loan-to-value ratio at 18-22%, up from 13%, and its credit default swaps have widened. A weak Anthropic IPO could also cloud OpenAI's listing, its biggest bet.

FT · Week ahead
$126bn

CPI, China's trade surplus and UK growth

Beyond US CPI on Tuesday, economists expect China's October trade surplus near $126bn, with exports up 38%, putting it on track to beat last year's record $1.2tn. The PBoC is leaning against further yuan gains. UK third-quarter GDP on Thursday is seen up 0.7%, even after the Bank of England raised rates to 4%.

FT · Europe
€70bn

Merz threatens EU budget over Ukraine aid

In a letter to António Costa, Germany's chancellor says he will block the EU's 2028-34 budget unless every member state helps close Ukraine's €70bn funding gap for 2027. Hungary, Slovakia, Bulgaria and the Czech Republic have signalled they will not contribute. Berlin also wants future Ukraine funding decided by qualified majority. Leaders meet in Dublin on Thursday and Friday.
