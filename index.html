Number of the day
$68.9k

Bitcoin breaks below $70,000

Bitcoin fell through $70,000 overnight to its lowest since 20 August, and by 06:00 Tel Aviv it was near $68,900, down 3.6% in a day; Ether lost 3.9%. It slid even as Asian stocks rose, which tells you what is driving it: higher bond yields make assets that pay nothing less attractive. Bloomberg adds two other weights: US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost more than $2.5bn to outflows between 30 October and 11 November, and the Democratic sweep in Congress raises the odds of tougher crypto rules. Bitcoin is now down about 21% this year.

Asia · overnight
+0.7%

Asian tech rebounds as investors look past Huang

The MSCI Asia Pacific index rose 0.7%, led by tech-heavy Taiwan and South Korea, as traders refocused on the AI outlook rather than Nvidia's boardroom. A KCM Trade analyst told Bloomberg the confirmed leadership change gives markets some certainty to work with. Japan's Topix rose 0.8%, while Hong Kong and Australia fell.

Bonds · Fed
2.87%

The Treasury rout spreads to Japan and Australia

Japan's 10-year yield rose 7 bp to 2.87% and Australia's 8 bp to 5.12%, catching up with Thursday's sharp US selloff as traders bet on faster Fed hikes. New York Fed president John Williams said inflation is still high and policymakers must act further. Treasuries were steady in Asia; TD Securities says the macro picture, not tech, is driving bonds now.

160

The yen's slide past 160 revives intervention talk

The yen weakened beyond 160 per dollar on Thursday for the first time since September, and traders are watching for Japanese authorities to step in. It was little changed near 159.9 in Asia. A Bank of Nassau economist told Bloomberg markets get jittery whenever the pair nears that level.

US · IPO

Anthropic's IPO: 6x demand, a lower valuation

Anthropic is offering 825mn shares at $70–78 and will trade on Nasdaq as ANT next week. With a $26bn private placement it could raise about $90bn, at a diluted value near $1.4tn, well below the $2tn first floated, Bizportal reports citing Bloomberg. The FT says Nvidia plans to put $10bn into the placement.

Israel · tech
18%

Huang's exit unnerves Nvidia's Israeli staff

Operations managed from Israel account for about 18% of Nvidia's revenue, Globes says, citing its second-quarter report. Huang built the Yokneam and Tel Aviv centres into a core part of the company after the 2019 Mellanox deal, and employees now worry whether new leadership will keep that commitment. Some had seen Nvidia's presence as a shield against sanctions on Israel.

Tel Aviv new-home sales collapse in Q3

New-home sales in Tel Aviv fell 60% from the second quarter, lifting the city's unsold supply by 5%, the Central Bureau of Statistics says. Nationally about 21,800 homes were sold, down 6%, and the quarter looks weak even after adjusting for holidays. For the first time, Haifa led the country in deal volume.

FT · Middle East
440kg

Trump shelves Vance's Iran peace push

Trump's post ends, for now, the vice-president's effort to reach an interim deal through Iran's top security official. Under a mediated proposal, Iran would reopen Hormuz and the US would lift its naval blockade and allow oil exports. The sticking points are the order of steps and Iran's 440kg of highly enriched uranium, which Washington wants shipped abroad.

Financial Times · 12/11Read →
FT · AI finance
$124bn

Huang was the AI industry's backer of last resort

The FT maps how Huang personally drove Nvidia's dealmaking: $124bn of equity commitments by July, $36bn of pledges to buy cloud capacity, and up to $105bn of guarantees behind OpenAI's Ohio campus. Bernstein halved its price target, asking what happens to those backstops. One neocloud executive expects Nvidia's dealmaking to largely pause until the uproar settles.

Financial Times · 12/11Read →
FT · Energy
271k b/d

Russian diesel exports double, but none reach the US

After Trump lifted fuel sanctions on Moscow and leaned on Kyiv to pause refinery strikes, Russian diesel exports doubled in October, per Kpler, going mainly to Brazil and Turkey. Not one cargo has reached the US. With Ukrainian attacks resumed, Russia extended its diesel export ban to end-January, and analysts expect lacklustre flows ahead.
