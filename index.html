Key numbers

Israel's inflation sinks as chips weigh on Wall Street

Number of the day
1.1%

Israel's inflation falls to its lowest level in years

Consumer prices fell 0.1% in October, the Central Bureau of Statistics said on Sunday evening, when economists had expected a rise of 0.2% to 0.4%. Annual inflation dropped to 1.1%, near the bottom of the Bank of Israel's 1–3% target. Fresh fruit, transport and housing services fell, while clothing jumped 4.1%. Home prices slipped for a second month and are 1.5% lower than a year ago. Investors in London told Bank Hapoalim they still see almost no chance of a rate cut at the 23 November decision, so the surprise mainly strengthens the case for cuts in 2027.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · stocks
−1.2%

Chipmakers sink Friday, but the week ends up

The Nasdaq 100 lost 1.2% and the S&P 500 0.4% on Friday, while the Dow edged up 0.1%. AMD fell 8.8%, Nebius 9.9%, Marvell 4.7% and CoreWeave 6.1%. Nvidia slipped 2%, its sixth straight weekly loss, the longest run since 2012. Even so, the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq broke four-week losing streaks, and the global MSCI index rose 2.5% for the week.

US · Nvidia
Tuesday

Nvidia heads into results with its CEO question open

Nvidia reports on Tuesday after the close, its first quarter under interim chief Colette Kress. Treasury Secretary Bessent said on Sunday the company needs a "dramatic shake-up" with an outside CEO, while Huang's allies lobby for his return. Bloomberg also reports Nvidia appealed to Taiwanese officials after prosecutors there accused an employee of helping smuggle AI chips into China. The shares were steady overnight.

Bloomberg · FT · 15–16/11Read →
US · IPO
$1.4tn

Anthropic's listing becomes the AI trade's next test

Anthropic is set for the biggest IPO ever, at a valuation of about $1.4tn, the first listing of a frontier AI lab. The FT expects $83bn to $90bn to be raised, almost all in new shares, to pay for computing. Bizportal estimates that Sound Ventures, co-founded by Israeli-born Guy Oseary, holds about 0.5%, worth around $7bn at the top of the range.

Bloomberg · FT · Bizportal · 15–16/11Read →
US · rates
+28 bp

Bonds stop cushioning the stock market

The 10-year yield is steady near 5.15% after rising 28 basis points last week, the most since April 2025, and the dollar index is at its strongest since May 2025. Kansas City Fed president Jeff Schmid said inflation is running too hot and the Fed has more work to do. Retail sales and the minutes of the Fed's last meeting come this week; a December hike is fully priced.

Bloomberg · Calcalist · 16/11Read →
Asia · overnight
−1.3%

Asian tech slides; China and Japan data disappoint

MSCI's Asia gauge lost 1.3% and its tech subindex more than 4%, with Taiwan and South Korea down at least 2.7%. China's consumption, output and investment all undershot forecasts in October, and Japan's economy shrank for the first time in a year. In Hong Kong, New World Development plunged as much as 47% on a deeply discounted $3.2bn rights issue. Morgan Stanley still moved to a "maximum" overweight on global stocks.

Bloomberg · FT · 16/11Read →
Israel · politics
NIS 3.00

Foreign funds see a stronger shekel under Eisenkot

Hedge funds and foreign banks told Bank Hapoalim in London that the dollar could fall to about NIS 3 if Gadi Eisenkot forms a stable government; one trader even mentioned below 2.85. One economist put the odds at 50–50 against new elections. Foreign holdings are very low, so a deal could bring a sharp inflow, and the Bank of Israel might resume buying dollars below NIS 3.

Globes · 15/11Read →
Israel · housing
−1.5%

Home prices fall again as inflation drops

The home price index fell 0.2% in the August–September period and 1.5% over a year; new-home prices dropped 0.7%. It came with the surprise 0.1% fall in October consumer prices. Calcalist reports listed developers now hold a record of about 25,000 unsold flats, which keeps pressure on prices and on builders' shares.

Teva's CEO sold shares a day before his 2030 plan

Richard Francis sold 250,000 Teva shares at about $42 on 10 November, the day before he presented the plan to make Teva a biopharma group with up to $7bn from innovative drugs. The sale was under a trading plan set in June; he has sold about $42mn over the past year and keeps roughly 928,000 shares. Other executives also sold in recent days.

FT · Nvidia
"War room"

Huang considers a comeback as allies lobby the board

Huang has discussed returning as CEO with confidants, while an ally is building a Silicon Valley lobbying effort that would seek White House support. Musk and Epic's Tim Sweeney back a public "Bring Jensen Back" letter; investors Brad Gerstner and Gavin Baker urged the board to keep all options open. Against that: Bessent's call for change, a board probe, and Arm's Rene Haas reportedly approached as a successor.

FT · AI trade
$4.2tn

The AI "picks and shovels" trade swings into reverse

Industrial, utility and mining stocks tied to data-centre building have lost $4.2tn since June, nearly half the $9.4tn they gained in the prior year, an FT analysis finds. Caterpillar is 44% off its high and Oklo, Corning and Comfort Systems have roughly halved. Bulls point to record backlogs, such as Caterpillar's $78bn; sceptics say double ordering inflates them.

Financial Times · 15/11Read →
FT · Washington
NDAA

Congress moves to tighten AI chip exports

Both parties are backing bills that curb sales of advanced chips abroad, block Chinese remote access through cloud services, and let Congress review licences already granted. Senior House Republicans want Huang, Nvidia's general counsel and a Commerce official to testify. The measures may ride on the must-pass defence bill in the lame-duck session, a risk for every US chipmaker that sells into Asia.

Financial Times · 15/11Read →
FT · The FT View
$900bn

Anthropic's IPO is a test of AI, and a cash call

The editorial board says the listing will be a defining moment for AI, but stresses that Anthropic is listing because it needs the money: it expects to spend about $900bn on computing and infrastructure in the years ahead. Public markets give investors more open control over that spending. Global equity issuance had already passed $1tn by September, the most in five years.
