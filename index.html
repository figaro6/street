Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street opened slightly lower, with the S&P 500 down 0.2% and Home Depot dragging on the Dow, as traders wait for two events that land at the same moment tonight: Nvidia's results and the close of Anthropic's order book, both around 23:00 Tel Aviv. October retail sales rose only 0.2%, but the control group jumped 0.6%, nudging the 2-year yield up 2 bp. Tel Aviv is rebounding from Monday's rout: the TA-35 is up 0.4%, led by Elbit Systems, up 7% on a record order book, and Leumi's NIS 3bn quarter. Europe is the weak spot, with the Stoxx 600 down 1.1%.

Key numbers

Number of the day
$90.4bn

What Anthropic's IPO would raise, as its order book closes tonight

Anthropic and its seven co-founders are marketing 825 million shares at $70–78; with a $26bn private placement, the deal would raise $90.4bn, and bankers are said to be guiding to the top of the range or above (Bloomberg). Banks stop taking orders at 16:00 New York, which is 23:00 Tel Aviv, just as Nvidia reports. Amazon and Google will own roughly 15% and 14% of the company afterwards. The FT's look at the prospectus shows why buyers care about tonight's Nvidia numbers: more than $900bn in compute commitments over two decades.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · open
−0.2%

Stocks edge lower in the countdown to Nvidia

The S&P 500 slipped 0.2% in early trade while the Nasdaq 100 held roughly flat. Nvidia touched its lowest since July 2025 before steadying; the focus tonight is its long-term outlook after Jensen Huang's ouster, not the quarter. The company has set up a board committee to oversee national security and export controls after the chip-smuggling scandal.

US · data
+0.6%

Retail sales slow, but core spending accelerates

October retail sales rose 0.2% after a gasoline-driven 0.8% in September, as car and furniture sales fell. The control group, which feeds into GDP, rose a stronger-than-expected 0.6%, the most since August. Bloomberg Economics says the weakness was concentrated in cars and does not signal a broad pullback; restaurant sales rose just 0.2%, the weakest since March.

Anthropic guides to the top of its range or above

Anthropic is telling investors it expects to price at the high end of $70–78 a share or above, for a raise of about $90.4bn including a private placement. Order books close at 23:00 Tel Aviv tonight. After the deal Amazon will hold nearly 15% and Google about 14%, and traders are already arbitraging pricing gaps in pre-IPO contracts.

Israel · defence
$33bn

Elbit jumps 7% on a record order book

Elbit Systems' backlog hit a record $33bn as third-quarter revenue grew 17.2% to $2.25bn. Adjusted net profit rose 31% to $210mn and EPS of $4.36 beat forecasts by a wide margin, with the operating margin above 10% again. Calcalist adds that the state will compensate Elbit for payment delays since the war began. The shares are up 7% in Tel Aviv and 8% on Nasdaq.

Leumi earns NIS 3bn; Mizrahi falls again

Bank Leumi's third-quarter profit rose about 11% to NIS 3bn, or NIS 3.3bn excluding the special bank levy, for a 16.9% return on equity; the stock rose 3%. Mizrahi-Tefahot posted a third straight drop in net profit. Discount named Mercantile CEO Barak Nardi to head its banking division and lead the merger of the two banks.

Israel · politics
6 weeks

Eisenkot's camp moves to take the Knesset speakership

Lawmakers vote today on a new speaker, and Eisenkot's candidate Matan Kahana appears to have majority support against Likud's Ofir Katz. The post would let the opposition prioritise bills, including rolling back parts of the judicial overhaul. Eisenkot has up to six weeks to build a coalition, but the ultra-Orthodox draft issue is blocking him and Netanyahu stays in charge meanwhile.

Israel · budget
$31bn

End of US military aid leaves a $31bn hole

Finance Ministry officials told the Knesset that the defence establishment has already committed $31bn in procurement meant to be paid from the next US aid deal. Trump said on election day that the $3.8bn-a-year package will end this year, two years early. Officials admitted they had no warning and that no staff work preceded the announcement.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 17 November

  • MarketsThe Nasdaq lost 2.7% and the S&P 500 1.9% on Monday over doubts about the AI boom, with Nvidia down more than 4% and Asian chipmakers hit too. Anthropic hopes the jitters will not derail its IPO this week.
  • Deutsche BankShares fell nearly 6% after leaked files pointed to past business with Russian oligarchs up to the Ukraine war and almost $600mn of suspicious payments linked to Jeffrey Epstein. The bank says its controls are stronger now.

  • Trump in AsiaTrump landed in Manila for a Quad meeting with Australia, India and Japan, before heading to China to meet Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin.
  • UkraineRussian ballistic missiles forced Kyiv's north and south bridges over the Dnieper to close overnight, after Ukraine's largest drone attack of the war struck the bridge to Crimea, a key supply route.
  • BritainA report by Alan Milburn on youth unemployment proposes cutting health benefits for under-25s and lowering taxes on employers who hire young people. About 13% of Britons aged 16–24 are not in work, education or training.

Financial Times · key reads

FT · AI credit
$800mn

Banks start offloading AI data-centre loans

ING and Société Générale have put up to $800mn of data-centre debt up for sale, including loans tied to Oracle's Stargate projects. Most trade above 90 cents, but New Mexico's Project Jupiter, hit by power delays, is expected in the mid-80s. Some lenders have dropped out of a $63bn Anthropic debt package, and the BoE and ECB are reviewing banks' exposure.

Financial Times · 17/11Read →
FT · Anthropic
$900bn

The data-centre bet behind Anthropic's IPO

Anthropic has signed more than $900bn in compute commitments, much of it for its own TPU-filled data centres built by former crypto miners such as Hut 8. Compute cost $9.9bn last quarter, half of it rented capacity. The FT estimates its leased chips at about $7bn per gigawatt a year against about $30bn for SpaceX cloud capacity, but the commitments stand whatever demand does.

Financial Times · 17/11Read →
FT · Markets Insight
19.8x

JPMorgan Private Bank: time to buy hyperscalers

Grace Peters argues the sell-off was rational but staying bearish may not be: capex growth is slowing, the midterm backlash makes existing data centres more valuable, and cloud backlogs are up over 140% in a year. JPMorgan sees hyperscaler free cash flow bottoming in 2027 and turning positive in 2028. The group trades at 19.8 times forward earnings, a small premium to the S&P 500.
