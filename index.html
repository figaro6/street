Good morning, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street had its best day in weeks on Wednesday: CoreWeave's results lifted the AI trade, the Nasdaq 100 jumped 2.3% and the S&P 500 rose 1.3%. Today: US jobless claims at 15:30, the Wall Street open at 16:30 and Cisco after the close.

Number of the day
−9%

Nvidia sinks after hours as the board weighs Huang's exit

Nvidia closed Wednesday up 2.9% at $173.73, then fell almost 9% in after-hours trading (as much as 10%, per Bloomberg) after the Wall Street Journal reported the board is preparing to oust Jensen Huang. The company has hired an outside firm to find possible successors, and the FT reports the board held urgent talks after news of a relationship with a senior Nvidia executive, days after his affair with a suspected Chinese spy emerged. For a $4.2tn company at the centre of AI financing, the risk reaches well beyond one stock: Nasdaq futures are down 1% and Taiwan fell 2.6%.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · Nvidia
~−9%

Nvidia's board moves toward replacing Huang

The Wall Street Journal reported the board is preparing to oust Jensen Huang after several extramarital affairs, including one with a woman suspected of spying for China. Bloomberg says Nvidia has hired an outside firm to identify replacements; The Economist reports speculation that CFO Colette Kress could step in as interim chief. Bloomberg's strategists warn that Nvidia's central role in financing the AI sector makes a sudden change risky well beyond the stock.

US · the close
+2.3%

Tech stocks win back last week's rout

The Nasdaq 100 rose 2.3% on Wednesday, recouping nearly all of last week's 4.9% post-midterm drop; the S&P 500 gained 1.3% and the VIX fell 10%. CoreWeave's results, OpenAI's $10bn quarterly revenue and strong demand for Anthropic's IPO revived the AI trade, and Bizportal says the chip index jumped 5.7%. The gains came despite hike bets after the hot October CPI.

US · CoreWeave
+24.7%

CoreWeave soars, then goes looking for $4bn

CoreWeave closed at $60.77 after its beat-and-raise, its best day in months; the FT notes its shares had halved since the summer as investors doubted its debt-funded AI bet. Bloomberg and Seeking Alpha now report it plans to raise at least $4bn in an investment-grade loan (headline). Bigger borrowing is exactly what worried investors, so watch the reaction.

AMD and Broadcom gain from Nvidia's turmoil

AMD rose 7.3% to $552.90, Broadcom 4.0% and Dell 11.8% on Wednesday, with further gains for rivals after the bell, per The Economist. Israel · banks
1.6x

Israeli banks trade at a 60% premium to book

Bizportal notes the big banks now trade at 1.5-1.7 times equity, against around 1 times in the past. Analysts expect the five largest to report combined Q3 profit of about NIS 8.2-8.3bn, below NIS 8.5bn in Q2 and NIS 8.7bn a year earlier. That premium holds only while returns on equity stay well above the cost of capital; First International's was 13.8%.

$6-7bn

Teva lifts its 2030 target for new drugs

At its R&D Day, Teva raised its 2030 revenue goal for innovative medicines from about $5bn to $6-7bn, against $3.77-3.92bn expected this year. CEO Richard Francis told Bizportal that duvakitug, a bowel-disease antibody in phase 3, could be the next flagship after Austedo, and that Teva will not sell its generics arm. The shares rose 2% in New York.

FT · Nvidia
$4.2tn

Nvidia's board to decide Huang's fate

The board met urgently on Wednesday night after reports of Huang's relationship with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice-president of healthcare and an 18-year veteran. It deepens a scandal that began when the FT reported his links to an executive at a client, whom US officials see as a Chinese intelligence asset. A separate FT piece says Huang has fallen out of favour in Trump's Washington.

Financial Times · 12/11Read →
FT · Nvidia investors
1 in 7

What losing a superstar CEO can cost

About one in seven departing Russell 3000 CEOs in 2025 were forced out, and when McDonald's and Intel fired bosses over relationships, shares fell less than 3%. Superstar founders are different: Tesla fell 15% when the SEC sought Musk's removal, and Altman's 2023 ouster briefly cut about $50bn from Microsoft. The piece weighs how much of Nvidia's value is really Huang.

Financial Times · 11/11Read →
FT · Anthropic
$1.5tn+

Anthropic's bankers prepare to raise the price

Heavy demand means bankers expect to price above the $70-78 range, lifting the valuation target from $1.4tn to $1.5tn or more, according to five people. The raise could then exceed $100bn. Backers say Anthropic now cares more about raising as much cash as possible for computing power than about the headline valuation.

Financial Times · 11/11Read →
FT · oil
10.6mn

Saudi oil output is back near prewar levels

Saudi Arabia told Opec it pumped 10.6mn barrels a day in October, its highest since the US-Israel war with Iran began in late February. The kingdom has kept exports moving despite attacks on shipping and energy sites, after Iran's grip on Hormuz collapsed Gulf shipments. Rising supply helps explain why Brent stays in the low $90s.

Sources and method

This edition belongs to the 16:45 afternoon slot but ran early, at about 09:40-09:55 Tel Aviv on Thursday 12/11, a few minutes after the late-running morning edition and before Wall Street's open and TASE's first trades; there was no edition on Wednesday 11/11. So it shows Wednesday's US and Tel Aviv closes, live US futures, Asia and currencies, not the opening moves or 15:30 data the afternoon slot normally covers. Market numbers are Bloomberg quote-page values read at 09:40; US index values are Wednesday's closes and Treasury changes are against Tuesday, because the bond market was shut on Wednesday. Bloomberg's quote pages put Brent at $93.35 (+0.6%) and WTI at $86.93; its Markets Wrap, timed about 45 minutes earlier, had Brent near $92.60 (−0.1%) and WTI at $86.38. We show the quote-page values; the gap may reflect a contract roll or timing. The Taiwan index move is from the wrap (no quote read). Nvidia's after-hours fall is from The Economist (almost 9%) and Bloomberg (as much as 10%); Bloomberg's quote page still shows the regular-session close. Israeli index closes are from Bloomberg; sector moves and arbitrage gaps from Bizportal (Hebrew). Shahar yields and prices are from TASE's security pages, with yields as of 11/11; price changes are computed against Tuesday's prices from the previous edition. Seeking Alpha ratings for the two Nvidia articles were not shown on the page, so they are described as bullish from the authors' text. Calcalist and Yahoo Finance were not read for this edition. Cards tagged headline come from headlines only. Seeking Alpha cards summarize authors' opinion bullets. Summaries are written for this page and link to the original articles. Nothing here is investment advice.
