Afternoon edition · Wall Street open 16:30 · Tel Aviv close · US jobs 15:30

Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street is bouncing after Thursday's rout. A strong US jobs report (+165,000, unemployment 4.1%) pushed Treasury yields up and lifted bets on a December Fed hike to about 70%, yet chip stocks are roaring back: Nasdaq 100 +2.1% and the chip gauge +6% in the first hour. The Dow is slightly down, Europe's Stoxx 600 fell 1.8%, and the VIX has eased to 25. In Tel Aviv, the TA-35 clawed back 3.1% in a short Friday session and the shekel recovered to 3.18 per dollar. Anthropic's IPO buyers are now eyeing a $1.2 trillion valuation.

Edition focus

Wall Street's open, the jobs data, Tel Aviv's close

Wall Street's first hour

  • Nasdaq 100 +2.1%, S&P 500 +0.8%, Dow −0.2%; the gap between tech and the rest is wide.
  • Chip gauge +6%; Nvidia +6.3%, Nebius +10%, CoreWeave +6.4% (Bloomberg most-active list).
  • VIX down to 25 from 28, still elevated.
  • Airbnb raised guidance for a third time; Tesla will cut Cybertruck output.

The 15:30 data

  • Payrolls +165k, prior two months revised up; unemployment 4.1%.
  • Dec hike odds about 70%; at least 40 bp more tightening priced by Sept 2027.
  • Strategists: "Main Street is doing just fine" (Lombard Odier); financial conditions still decide the Fed (Capital Economics).
  • Canada's jobless rate rose to 6.7%.

Tel Aviv & Europe

  • TA-35 +3.09% and TA-125 +2.81% in the short session, recouping most of Thursday's slump; real estate +3.1%.
  • Shekel regained ground: USD/ILS 3.184 from 3.204.
  • Stoxx 600 −1.8% as Europe lagged the US bounce; AI-linked credit looks resilient (Bloomberg).
  • Taiwan deploys its stabilisation fund and limits short-selling.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · jobs
165,000

Hot jobs report cements a December hike

Nonfarm payrolls beat all forecasts after upward revisions, and unemployment slipped to 4.1%. Strategists say it shows AI is not the whole economy. Capital Economics doubts the equity wobble is big enough to stop a December hike, while Edward Jones warns one strong report should not be extrapolated given tighter credit conditions.

US · rates
+7 bp

Treasuries stumble as hike bets build

The 2-year yield jumped about 7 bp to 4.53%, and the 10-year rose to 4.87%, trimming part of Thursday's haven rally. The market now sees roughly 70% odds of a December increase. Germany's 10-year rose 2 bp to 3.26% and Britain's 4 bp to 5.17%. We saw the headline and the wrap, not the full Treasury story.

Bloomberg · 6/11Read →
US · chips
+6%

Chip stocks roar back after Thursday's rout

The chip gauge is up 6% and Nvidia 6.3%, while neocloud names such as Nebius and CoreWeave jumped. Bloomberg headlines say Microsoft is souring on neoclouds over data-centre delays and costs. The rebound is sharp but follows a record $2.3tn one-day wipe-out in the Nasdaq 100, so volatility is likely to stay high.

US · IPO
$1.2tn

Anthropic IPO valuation talk slips after the rout

Buyers are discussing $1.2–1.3 trillion, down from $1.4–1.5 trillion and as high as $2 trillion in early October, ahead of formal marketing next week. HSBC's strategist suggests about $1 trillion would be calmer. Delay is possible but a less appealing option. Cerebras and Figma, which upsized their IPOs, now trade well below issue prices.

Data-centre backlash spreads to Europe

More than 100 protesters broke into a Microsoft data-centre construction site in Amsterdam, part of coordinated actions planned across Europe through the weekend. In the US, Democrats vow hearings on AI executives from January. Taiwan has imposed a short-selling limit and deployed a stabilisation fund after its AI rout (headline).

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 6 November

  • MarketsThe S&P 500 lost nearly 4.4% on Thursday, its worst day since April 2025's "Liberation Day" tariffs, and the Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4%, on fears that the Democratic midterm win threatens the AI boom. Asia suffered big losses on Friday and Europe slid more modestly.
  • Iran / HormuzTrump says he will soon decide whether to "go further" against Iran and warned America may "destroy the rest of the country". Iran says two tankers and a tugboat were damaged in Hormuz, at least five vessels since Sunday, mostly from mines.
  • Japan / RussiaJapan protested after Russia seized the local assets of Japan Tobacco International, partly state-owned. Tension has escalated in recent weeks, including the arrest of a Japanese journalist on espionage charges.
  • FranceHundreds of thousands protested again against the cost of living and public services, mostly peacefully. Real private-sector wages fell about 1% year on year in Q3, and the government promises answers next week.
  • ParaguayPresident Santiago Peña resigned after the digital bank Ueno collapsed over book-keeping allegations and depositors were frozen out. Opposition parties were preparing to impeach him.

FT · markets
3rd

Katie Martin: is the AI bubble finally bursting?

The FT columnist calls this the third tech shakeout in a few weeks and says the question itself is telling. She lists stress points: heavy debt, doubtful revenue and circular financing, noting the BIS says nearly 29% of AI firms' deals are with other AI firms. US politics may be the trigger, as voters backed curbs on data centres. CoreWeave is down 70% from May's peak. A swift end to the Iran war is markets' best footing.

FT · politics & tech
$600mn

America turns on Trump's tech cheerleaders

Republican groups took over $600mn from tech donors, and Democrats who swept Congress now vow to bring the "AI billionaire class" to heel. From January they plan hearings with executives under oath and probes of Elon Musk; some want a pause on advanced AI. Texas's governor-elect backs a data-centre moratorium. SpaceX and Tesla have lost a combined $420bn of value since the election.
