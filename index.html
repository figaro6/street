Afternoon edition · Wall Street's first 30 minutes · TASE into the close

Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

Nvidia's relief rally lasted less than half an hour. The chipmaker gave back an early 3% gain after the open and the S&P 500 hovered near flat, while the Dow fell 0.9%. The spark was Fed Governor Christopher Waller, who now wants a half-point hike in December. That sent gold down 2%, lifted the dollar index to a 19-month high and pushed the shekel to its weakest in nearly a year, near NIS 3.28. Tel Aviv turned lower into the close, led by builders and banks, and tonight Anthropic prices what could be the biggest IPO ever.

Key numbers

Waller's hawkish turn hits gold and the shekel

Number of the day
50 bp

The December hike Fed Governor Waller now says is probably needed

A week ago Waller said he would back a double-size move if October core inflation rose 0.3% or more; it did, and now he presents a half-point hike as the likely path, followed by reversing all three 2025 cuts. That would take the Fed funds range from 3.75–4% to 4.25–4.5%. New York Fed President John Williams pushed back, saying nothing dramatic is needed, and markets still price a quarter point. The fallout was immediate: gold −2%, the dollar index at a 19-month high and the shekel near NIS 3.28, with Israel's own rate heading the other way.

US · the open
−1.5%

Nvidia's post-earnings rally fizzles within minutes

Nvidia erased an almost 3% early gain and turned lower, and the S&P 500 wavered toward a fifth straight decline. Waller calls for a half-point hike; Williams disagrees

The Fed governor said inflation is stickier than he expected and spreading, so December probably needs a double move and the 2025 cuts should be reversed. A quarter point could do, he said, if the Fed signalled January too, but Chair Kevin Warsh dislikes forward guidance. New York Fed President Williams sees no need for drama. Anthropic set to price above range tonight

Anthropic is guiding investors to a price above its $70–78 range, with the book about seven times covered. Israel · shekel
3.276

Shekel hits its weakest level in nearly a year

The dollar is up about 5% against the shekel since late October and 16% from May's low near 2.82. Part is global: the dollar index is at its highest since March 2025. The rest is local: US rates are now above Israel's, October inflation fell to 1.1%, and markets expect the Bank of Israel may cut again on Monday. A trade deficit nearing $50bn a year adds to demand for dollars.

Israel · GDP
+8%

GDP jumps 8%, but half is production abroad

The first Q3 estimate shows 1.9% growth on the quarter, 8% annualised, but only 4% once output by Israeli-registered firms made abroad, led by Nvidia, is stripped out. Exports rose 51.5% annualised and private consumption 6.4%, while defence spending fell 8.4%. Nvidia Israel's revenue alone reached about $21bn in the quarter, 18% of the group total.

−3.6%

Builders and banks drag Tel Aviv lower

Aura's sales slowed and its net profit fell 41%, with 81% of flats sold through discount schemes; the construction index dropped 3.6%. Discount fell 6.5% after a 20% profit drop to NIS 910mn, hit by merger costs. The five big banks' combined Q3 profit fell 6% to NIS 8.1bn. Mega Or rose on a second data-centre deal with Nebius worth NIS 550mn a year.

ZIM soars on freight rates; Global-e slides 7%

ZIM's Q3 revenue rose 53% to $2.71bn and net profit jumped to $830mn as the average rate per container climbed 55%; it raised guidance for the second time and declared a $243mn dividend. Global-e fell about 7% despite 42% revenue growth: the guidance raise was at the low end and gross margin slipped to 44.8%.

Wall Street sees earnings growth halving next year

Analysts expect global earnings growth to slow from 33.5% this year to about 16% in 2027 as the AI capex boom cools. Goldman sees US profit growth falling from 36% to 11%, with depreciation and higher interest costs biting. Most banks still expect the S&P 500 to rise: Barclays targets 8,700 by end-2027.

FT · ECB
$4tn

ECB sees subprime parallels in the AI borrowing binge

The ECB's stability review says big tech has more than $4tn of off-balance-sheet commitments, well above its reported liabilities, and that circular chip-financing deals inflate both sides' revenues. Even if AI succeeds, it warns of overcapacity and credit losses. It also flagged French debt, with the spread over Germany at euro-crisis highs.

Low-profile VCs set for a $100bn Anthropic windfall

Small early backers such as Spark Capital and Factorial Funds could each hold $10bn or more at a $1.4tn valuation, reshuffling Silicon Valley's league table. Amazon and Google will own close to 30% after the IPO. FTX's old stake, sold in bankruptcy for $500mn, would now be worth about $35bn.
