Good morning, Tel Aviv.

Anthropic sold 980 million shares at $83 last night, an $81bn IPO that, with a $26bn private placement, makes it the biggest fundraising in market history at about $107bn. It starts trading on Nasdaq today as ANT, valued near $1.5tn, and US futures are climbing ahead of the debut: Nasdaq 100 contracts are up 1.1% and the S&P 500 0.6%. Asia is less sure, with Taiwan down 2.9% and Hong Kong 1.9%. At home, Calcalist reports the Treasury may defer some NIS 20bn of payments to defence companies rather than reopen the 2026 budget.

$107bn

What Anthropic raised, beating SpaceX's record

Anthropic said late on Wednesday it sold 980 million shares at $83, far more than the 825 million it had planned and above its $70–78 range: an $81bn IPO. Add the $26bn private placement with Nvidia, Microsoft and Google and the total is about $107bn, against SpaceX's $86bn. A greenshoe could add another $12bn. The FT says the deal values the Claude maker at about $1.5tn fully diluted and leaves it with more than $200bn of cash for computing power. Trading starts today on Nasdaq under ANT.

Financial Times (George Steer, George Hammond), published ~00:00 Tel Aviv; Bloomberg headline gives $107.3bn. Last night's edition estimated ~$94bn from the earlier share count; the upsized deal is larger.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · IPO
$1.5tn

Anthropic upsizes and prices its IPO at $83

Demand let Anthropic lift both price and size: 980 million shares versus 825 million planned, for $81bn. It joins the club of US companies worth over $1tn, though investors had hoped for $2tn in the summer. It made a $1.3bn operating loss in nine months and plans to fund future growth with debt, seeking an investment-grade rating.

US · the close
−2.9%

Nvidia's beat fades; Nasdaq still ends higher

Global stocks rallied on Nvidia's results early and then ran out of steam. The S&P 500 fell for a fifth day and the Dow lost 1.1%, while AMD rose 3.9% and CoreWeave fell 4.5%. Janus Henderson's Richard Clode called Nvidia's valuation "depressed"; Pictet warned marginal AI firms may lose their biggest backer without Huang.

Israel · budget
NIS 22bn

Treasury may owe defence firms instead of reopening budget

The defence establishment wants NIS 183bn for 2026 against the NIS 161bn agreed. Rather than ask the Knesset to raise the budget to NIS 720bn, the Treasury is weighing a delay of about NIS 20bn of payments to Elbit, IAI and Rafael. The accountant-general would show it in public debt but not in the cash deficit, a gap of about 1% of GDP. Calcalist says ratings firms may prefer the statistics bureau's measure, which counts it.

Israel · banks
−6%

Big five banks' quarterly profit slips to NIS 8.1bn

Leumi, Hapoalim, Mizrahi Tefahot, Discount and First International earned NIS 8.1bn in the third quarter, 6% less than a year earlier. Lower rates, the excess-profits tax and slower inflation on CPI-linked loans all weighed. Leumi was the only one to grow, up 11% to NIS 3bn helped by a one-off tax gain; Discount, mid-merger, fared worst.

Israel · tech
$21bn

Nvidia Israel's quarterly revenue jumps 156%

Interim CEO Colette Kress said Israel accounted for 18% of Nvidia's revenue again last quarter, which Globes puts at about $21bn from networking chips and servers. That is up 25% on the quarter and 156% on the year, making Nvidia Israel the country's biggest revenue generator, ahead of Teva, Check Point and Elbit.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 19 November

  • AnthropicThe AI lab priced its shares at $83, above the range, before Thursday's debut. The Nasdaq rose 0.4% while Nvidia fell almost 3% despite strong results, a sign traders remain jumpy.

FT · venture capital
$100bn+

Little-known VCs set for a giant Anthropic windfall

A handful of early backers stand to make well over $100bn combined. Angel Anjney Midha's stake is worth about 2,300 times his 2021 entry price, while Spark Capital and Factorial Funds would each hold roughly $10–11bn, on the FT's sums. Thousands of employees become multimillionaires, and lock-up expiries will be worth watching.

Financial Times · 18/11Read →
FT · credit
12–13%

JPMorgan offers deep discount on hung software debt

Banks stuck with financing for Qualtrics's $6.75bn Press Ganey deal are marketing about $2bn of junk bonds at 80 cents with an 8% coupon, a 12–13% yield. Software debt has been shunned since March on fears AI will upend business models. Proofpoint's bonds drew $7bn of orders, so demand exists at the right price.

Financial Times · 18/11Read →
FT · geopolitics
"Yalta 2.0"

Xi hosts Trump and Putin in Shenzhen today

Xi will cap a week of diplomacy with a trilateral meeting on the APEC sidelines, expected to cover nuclear arms control and the Iran and Ukraine wars. The FT says the symbolism matters more than any outcome. Xi's opening speech, calling for open rather than fragmented co-operation, read as a jab at Trump's tariffs.
