Key numbers

Softer PPI, but tech and bonds still sag

Number of the day
6.5%

Wholesale inflation at a four-year high

US producer prices rose 0.3% in October and 6.5% from a year earlier, the fastest pace since 2022, but slightly under forecasts. After Tuesday's hot 0.4% consumer-price reading, that small miss was enough to cool bets on a half-point Fed hike in December; a quarter point is fully priced. JPMorgan Asset Management's Priya Misra told Bloomberg there is "no urgency to go to 50". The relief is limited: the 2-year yield dipped, but the 10-year still rose to about 5.16%, capping its biggest weekly jump in 18 months, the FT says.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · stocks
−0.9%

Tech drags the open despite rates relief

The Nasdaq 100 fell 0.9% in early trade while the Dow held flat, as the Microsoft outage and the Nvidia turmoil kept tech under pressure. Nvidia is close to dropping below $4tn in market value after losing more than half a trillion this week. A Callan Family Office strategist expects the other 493 S&P companies to grow earnings faster than the Magnificent Seven this quarter and next.

US · Fed
25 bp

PPI miss takes the edge off half-point hike bets

A December quarter-point hike is fully priced, and a second rise in January is now mostly priced too, against odds of about 10% a week ago, the FT reports. Fed governor Waller had floated a half-point move; Boston Fed's Collins said on Friday the inflation fight outweighs housing weakness (headline). A JPMorgan portfolio manager warned a 50 bp hike would create a "messaging problem".

Bloomberg · FT · 13/11Read →
US · tech
Day 2

Microsoft's outage drags into a second day

One of the longest outages in Microsoft's history is still hitting Teams, Copilot and other 365 services, though Outlook email is coming back and queues are near normal. Bloomberg flags software and cloud stocks as in focus; Microsoft is down about 0.6%. Samsung's reported talks to buy data-centre cooler Modine keep infrastructure names in view.

+14%

Cerebras and CoreWeave jump as Nvidia lags

Cerebras rose about 14% after a strong sales outlook in its bid to challenge Nvidia, and CoreWeave gained 9% as solid results calmed worries about its debt; its bonds also rallied. AMD fell 5% after Thursday's surge. Trump suggested Apple's Tim Cook should replace Huang as Nvidia CEO (Bloomberg headline); Apple is up 1.4%.

Existing home sales sink to a 16-year low

The National Association of Realtors said previously owned home sales fell to their lowest level in 16 years, putting homebuilders and real-estate stocks in focus. With mortgage rates tracking a 10-year yield above 5%, relief looks distant. Based on Bloomberg's markets story; the full NAR release was not read.

Energy · IEA
2mn b/d

Oil market swings back into surplus

Supply beat demand by almost 2mn barrels a day in October, the IEA says, the first surplus since the US-Iran war began, as Gulf shipments rebounded. Stocks rose 1.7mn b/d, the first build since June. Brent is still up 1.1% to near $97 after Trump's no-deal post on Iran, and diesel stays tight because refining capacity is short.

Tel Aviv ends the week lower, led by clean-tech

The TA-35 lost 0.9% and the TA-125 1.0% in Friday's short session, after Thursday's jump in US yields. Clean-tech fell 2.3%, with Nofar Energy −5.6%, Phinergy −4.6% and Doral −4.5%; Azrieli dropped 3.6%. Palo Alto Networks rose 4.5%, the best of the leading names. Israel Corp fell 3%.

Israel · economy
~40%

Nvidia's turmoil reaches Israel's GDP

Bank of Israel forecasts 5.6% growth this year, and about 2.2 points of it come from "production abroad": chips designed here, made by overseas contractors and sold directly to foreign customers. That is close to 40% of growth, and about a third of next year's forecast 7.5%. Without it, growth would be 3.4% this year, Bizportal calculates.

Israel · tech
NIS 6.7bn

Nvidia's Israeli staff lose billions in stock value

Nvidia's roughly 6,200 employees in Israel may have lost about NIS 6.7bn in unvested stock value since the October peak, Bizportal estimates from the company's equity-pay data. That is roughly NIS 1.1mn per employee on average, or NIS 4.6bn on a more cautious assumption. The share is down about 35% from its peak near $243.

Israel · defence
$1mn

Romania returns to Rafael on cheap interceptors

Romania has reopened talks with Rafael to revive local production of the SkyCeptor interceptor, a Patriot-launched version of David's Sling's Stunner, after four years without a single missile built. Each costs about $1mn against $4–5mn for a standard Patriot round, which matters as NATO stocks run low.

Financial Times · key reads

All four cards are based on the article text.

FT · Rates
+30 bp

Treasuries head for their worst week since "liberation day"

The 10-year yield is up 0.3 points this week to 5.16%, the biggest weekly rise since the April 2025 tariff shock, and the 2-year is up 0.36 points to 4.88%. Fund managers had piled back into bonds after September's selloff; Tuesday's CPI forced them out. AllianceBernstein's trading head says those positions were washed out in two or three days.

Financial Times · 13/11Read →
FT · Energy
$95

The IEA's surplus doesn't end the shortage

Dated Brent is near $95, down from $136 in early October, as Gulf exports recover and demand weakens: the IEA sees consumption falling 2.7mn b/d this year. But most extra supply is crude, not fuel, so jet fuel and diesel remain tight. The agency expects surpluses through next year, and warns stockpiles will take months to rebuild.

Financial Times · 13/11Read →
FT · Washington
Lutnick

The Nvidia scandal reaches the Commerce Secretary

Republican and Democratic lawmakers want Howard Lutnick to explain how chips reached a company tied to the alleged Chinese spy. The FT reported earlier that Commerce delayed adding Megaspeed to the entity list. Senator John Cornyn told the paper "heads should roll"; tighter export enforcement is a risk for all US chipmakers.

Financial Times · 13/11Read →
FT · Opinion
Founder mode

Huang's fall indicts Big Tech's founder mode

The columnist argues tight founder control served tech well in its early years but becomes dangerous once AI sits at the heart of the economy and national security. Huang had no super-voting shares, yet dominated Nvidia through a compliant board. The piece sees a real need for a counterweight, and little sign one is coming.
