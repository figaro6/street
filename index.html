News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · AI unwind
$3.2tn

How Wall Street's AI bet blew up

The FT says a stack of leveraged tech positions unwound on Thursday after the midterms, with hedge funds cutting exposure at the fastest rate since Covid. Retail investors sold single stocks too, and Walleye said it would shut part of its equities business. The paper also quotes strategists who think the market overreacted and that construction already under way will continue.

FT · 6-7/11Read →
US · Berkshire
$9.6bn

Berkshire pours $9.6bn into buybacks

Under Greg Abel, Berkshire bought back $9.6bn of its own shares in the third quarter while selling a net $426mn of stocks. Its cash pile edged down 2.2% to $357bn and operating income fell 5.8% to $12.7bn. The shares are up 15% this year against about 8% for the S&P 500, per the FT.

FT · 7/11Read →
US · White House
Miller

Trump picks Miller as chief of staff

Days after the midterm defeat, Trump replaced Susie Wiles with her deputy Stephen Miller, architect of the immigration crackdown. The Economist says Trump wants a more aggressive White House and that Wiles was seen as a stabilising force. The FT's Big Read argues the defeat heralds a US retreat abroad.

Economist · FT · 7/11Read →
US · Fed & rates
165k

Strong jobs keep a December hike in play

October payrolls rose 165,000 against 60,000 in September, led by professional services. The Economist says this makes a Fed rate rise next month more likely; markets priced roughly 70% on Friday (carried from the earlier wrap). The 10-year ended at 4.86% and the 2-year at 4.51%. A Bloomberg headline says Wall Street sees the US hitting its $41.1tn debt cap in February.

Sources and method

Market numbers are Bloomberg quote-page values read about 16:50 Tel Aviv on 7/11 (a Saturday): US, European and Israeli markets are closed, so all index, Treasury, FX, energy and gold values are Friday closes or settlements, while Bitcoin and Ether were read at 16:53. Changes are against Thursday's close. Some futures and yield quotes carry a 5:00 UTC timestamp on 6/11, which we treat as Friday's generic close. Gold: the XAU quote ($4,070.80, -0.42%) differs from the figure on Bloomberg's markets page ($4,074.90, +0.94%); we keep the XAU quote. The Israeli Shahar bond data (TASE, 6/11 closes) and the TA-35 level were not re-fetched, since markets were shut. The December-hike probability (~70%) and the Bank of Israel rate are carried from earlier editions. The Nikkei level was not fetched. Bloomberg's $5.7tn rout headline is unexplained; we use the FT's $3.2tn for Thursday. Sources differ on Anthropic's IPO valuation (FT about $1.5tn; earlier reports $1.2-1.3tn from Bloomberg). The Economist brief was read for its first item only; the Iran and Israel items come from the morning edition.
