Good night, Tel Aviv.

Anthropic has reportedly priced the biggest IPO ever at $83 a share, above its $70–78 range, and starts trading on Nasdaq tomorrow. It did so on a shaky day: Nvidia fell 2.9% despite strong results, the Dow lost 1.1% and the S&P 500 slipped for a fifth straight session, while the Nasdaq still closed up 0.4%. Fed minutes showed nearly all officials expect to raise rates in the months ahead, gold fell more than 3% and the shekel ended near NIS 3.28. After the bell, Palo Alto Networks jumped on raised guidance and a $4.5bn AI deal.

Key numbers

A record IPO on a nervous day

Number of the day
$83

Anthropic's reported IPO price, above its $70–78 range

Bloomberg reports the Claude maker priced at $83 a share after heavy demand, and says the offering was enlarged. On the filed share counts plus the $26bn private placement with investors including Nvidia and AMD, that is roughly $94bn raised before any upsize, a record that beats SpaceX. Seeking Alpha says the price values the company above the $1.4tn it sought; Calcalist puts it at $1.5tn. The stock is due to trade on Nasdaq on Thursday as ANT, the first listing of a frontier AI lab.

Bloomberg Markets Wrap (updated 23:27 Tel Aviv) and Bloomberg's 22:39 report on the $80–85 range; Seeking Alpha and Calcalist (23:12). The ~$94bn is our arithmetic: 825m shares × $83 + $26bn.

The stories behind the numbers

US · the close
5 days

Nvidia drags the S&P to a fifth straight loss

Nvidia's post-earnings bounce faded into a 2.9% fall, pulling the S&P 500 down 0.2% to its lowest close since July and the Dow down 1.1%. The Nasdaq still rose 0.4%, which Seeking Alpha's Daniel Jones called an odd split. Bloomberg says investors want proof the AI trade can last; cheaper oil helped bonds.

US · IPO
$83

Anthropic prices above range, trades Thursday

Anthropic reportedly priced at $83 a share, above the $70–78 range, after first weighing $80–85, and the deal was enlarged. With the $26bn private placement it is the biggest IPO on record. Goldman, which leads the deal with Morgan Stanley, has forecast $685bn of revenue by 2030. Leveraged ETFs on the stock are already being prepared.

Bloomberg · Seeking Alpha · 18/11Read →
US · Nvidia
$2.4bn

Nvidia stops China chip sales; Arm's CEO sounded out

Interim CEO Colette Kress said Nvidia no longer ships licensed Hopper chips to China, after $2.4bn of such sales in Q3, and guidance assumes none. The board has set up a national-security committee. A recruiter has approached Arm's Rene Haas about the CEO job, Bloomberg reports. Kress told staff to move forward and offered pay rises.

US · Fed
Hikes

Fed minutes: nearly all see rate rises ahead

Minutes of the October meeting show nearly all officials think rates will likely need to rise in the coming months to bring inflation back to 2%. The Fed also flagged AI as a source of both inflation and financial instability. Governors Barr and Daly joined Waller in backing hikes, though at a measured pace; markets still price a quarter point in December.

Palo Alto raises guidance and buys an AI start-up

Palo Alto Networks lifted its full-year outlook with first-quarter results after the close and agreed an all-stock $4.5bn deal for AI model platform Applied Compute. The shares rose about 8% after hours. Also late: Microsoft says customers pick Copilot over Claude and ChatGPT on cost, and Meta paused its new share class amid an investor lawsuit.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 18 November

  • Iran–KuwaitIran sent drones at a Kuwaiti air base hosting US forces after American jets hit an Iranian missile site; Bahrain says it intercepted Iranian attacks. These are the first strikes in months, raising fears of escalation.
  • GazaIsraeli tanks crossed the ceasefire's "yellow line" into Hamas-held territory for the first time since last year's truce, in what the army calls a limited operation. Two Hamas commanders were killed in air strikes elsewhere.
  • ShenzhenTrump, Xi and Putin shook hands ahead of Thursday's APEC summit, where they are expected to discuss Ukraine, Iran, trade and AI. Putin called Western powers "former hegemons".
  • NvidiaBoard chair Harvey Jones ruled out bringing back Jensen Huang. Revenue rose 103% and profit hit $65bn, but the company has lost about $250bn of value since Huang quit.
  • UK inflationConsumer prices rose 3.6% in October from 3.5%, mostly on war-driven energy costs, which may push the Bank of England to raise rates again.
  • VenezuelaUS defence secretary Pete Hegseth made a surprise visit and met acting president Delcy Rodríguez; the two pledged closer defence ties without details.

Financial Times · key reads

FT · tech
NSA

US outages blamed on a rush to fix AI-found bugs

After a week of failures at Microsoft, JPMorgan, the FAA and even the NSA, Senator Mark Warner says there is no sign of a cyber attack. The likelier cause is organisations hurrying to patch flaws that AI tools now find at speed, which can destabilise critical systems. It is a new operational risk for banks and tech firms.

Financial Times · 18/11Read →
FT · energy
390kt

Russia imports record petrol as drones hit refineries

Russia brought in a record 390,000 tonnes of petrol by sea in October, Kpler data show, a striking reversal for a fuel exporter. Ukrainian strikes knocked 40–45% of refining capacity offline at the summer peak; supplies now come from Belarus, India, Turkey and others. Analysts say sustained strikes could reshape Russia's economy.

Financial Times · 18/11Read →
FT · US politics
Texas

Texas business frets over a Democratic governor

Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa, the first Democrat in three decades, ran on curbing data-centre growth, freezing power rates and ending corporate tax breaks. Dallas dealmakers worry she will reshape the new business courts that lured firms from Delaware. Data-centre developers say they will pause spending until the rules are clear.

Financial Times · 18/11Read →
FT · markets
36% → 11%

Wall Street sees earnings growth halving next year

Analysts expect global earnings growth to slow from 33.5% this year to about 16% in 2027 as AI spending cools. Goldman sees US profit growth falling from 36% to 11% as depreciation and interest costs bite. Most banks still expect the S&P 500 to rise, and today's Nvidia sell-off shows how sensitive markets are to that story.
