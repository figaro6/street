Good morning, Tel Aviv.

Asia picked up where Wall Street left off: the region's shares had their best day since July, with Taiwan up 9% and Samsung and SK Hynix more than 8% higher after Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia. US futures are flat, though, and bonds tell a calmer story, with the 10-year Treasury yield down to 4.91% and oil steady near $89 on signs of US-Iran diplomacy. Bitcoin slipped to about $60,200. Today brings US consumer confidence and earnings from Dell and Workday, and the question of whether a rally built on one man's return can last a second day.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

Asia · stocks
+3.7%

Asia has its best day since July

Chipmakers led the region after Huang's return restored faith in the AI trade. Taiwan's market rose 9%, while Samsung and SK Hynix gained more than 8% and Nvidia suppliers in Japan advanced. Hong Kong was the exception, slipping 0.4%. US futures swung between small gains and losses.

Bloomberg · 24/11Read →
US · bonds
4.91%

The bond market is not buying the euphoria

Treasury yields fell across the curve overnight as oil slipped, and copper weakened too. A Bloomberg strategist argues that falling yields and copper point to a slowing global economy, which argues against a full revival of AI optimism. Saxo's chief strategist put it simply: stocks are celebrating, bonds see a harder outlook.

Bloomberg · 24/11Read →
US · Nvidia
−63%

The rebound wrecked bets against Nvidia

A fund designed to return twice the inverse of Nvidia's daily move lost 63% on Monday. Meanwhile, the US blacklisted a Singapore unit of Alibaba and several companies tied to Megaspeed, the firm at the centre of the spying scandal that briefly cost Huang his job. A Tema ETFs strategist expects markets to come back to earth within days.

Bloomberg · 24/11Read →
US · earnings
$14–14.6bn

HPE's forecast falls short of the boldest hopes

Hewlett Packard Enterprise guided to revenue of $14bn to $14.6bn this quarter and adjusted EPS of $1.30 to $1.40, well above the average estimates of $12.8bn and $1.08. But some analysts had pencilled in more, and after a 17% jump during Monday's session the shares fell in extended trading.

Bloomberg · 23/11Read →
Energy · oil
$89.03

Oil holds its losses on US-Iran diplomacy

Brent steadied near $89 after a slide of more than 3% on Monday. Vice-President JD Vance met Qatar's prime minister to discuss talks with Iran, Bloomberg reports, citing Axios; Qatar has been a mediator in efforts to end the conflict, now nearly nine months old. Any sign of progress keeps pressure on prices.

Senate probes Vance over a Venezuela oil contract

Democratic senator Maggie Hassan wrote to Vance about his role in awarding a lucrative Venezuelan oil deal to Vitol. Hassan is set to chair the Senate's homeland security committee, which would give the probe more weight.

Financial Times · key reads

FT · Nvidia
War room

How Huang's allies lobbied Trump to bring him back

A group of Silicon Valley allies pulled in Trump and Vance, arguing that Huang was vital to the AI industry and so to the stock market. Trump spoke to the board himself; interim chief Colette Kress was kept out of the talks. Nvidia still faces FBI, SEC and congressional probes, and is worth about a fifth less than a month ago.

Financial Times · 23/11Read →
FT · politics
$15mn

Trump's Nvidia stake gained up to $15mn on Monday

Ethics filings suggest the president may have held up to 370,000 Nvidia shares at the end of September. He told CNBC he had persuaded the board and "made the deal". The White House did not comment; expect the conflict-of-interest question to feature in next week's congressional hearing.

Financial Times · 23/11Read →
FT · opinion
~$700bn

The problem with the circular AI economy

The columnist notes that Nvidia's vendor financing has grown 1,600% in three years to almost $700bn, citing MacroStrategy research. Cisco had just $1.3bn of such exposure before the dotcom bust, and its loans turned into a feedback loop of falling orders. The worry is that Nvidia also holds equity stakes in its customers, adding to the fragility.

Financial Times · 23/11Read →
FT · markets
−50–70%

What happens when bubbles burst

Absolute Strategy Research's co-founder says AI stocks tick every box of past bubbles: extreme moves, circular demand and funding, retail inflows and excessive capex. History suggests bubble stocks fall 50–70% over the following two years, and some of that has already happened in chips. He weighs whether strong earnings make this another buying opportunity.
