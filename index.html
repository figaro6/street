The stories behind the numbers

US · Nvidia
Huang

US memo links Nvidia chief to alleged spy

The FT reports that a US government memo says Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with Alice Huang, an executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, a customer of Nvidia. US intelligence believes she was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security. The memo went to top officials shortly before Trump's Beijing summit with Xi. The White House and CIA declined to comment. Nvidia shares still rose 2.8% on Friday.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 8 November

  • IsraelPresident Herzog gave Gadi Eisenkot first go at forming a government after a majority of parliament backed him over Netanyahu. Neither bloc won an outright majority last month, so a deal will be hard. He has up to six weeks before another candidate, probably Netanyahu, can try.
  • PolandA gunman killed at least six people and wounded at least 15 in central Kraków after driving into a square in military-style clothing. Police shot him; the interior ministry says he is a Polish citizen.
  • New ZealandThe National Party of PM Christopher Luxon won the most seats but no majority, and Labour's Chris Hipkins has not conceded. The upstart Opportunity Party could be kingmaker.
  • US SenateTwo races are still uncalled. In Alaska, Mary Peltola leads Dan Sullivan by 0.3 points and sued for rejected-ballot records; in Mississippi the Democrat leads by 2,574 votes with 97% counted.
  • TexasHouston's mayor told police to limit co-operation with immigration agents after Democrats won in Texas this week. Governor Abbott had threatened to cut $110m in funding.
  • HaitiOfficials say gang leader Mathias Saintil has been killed. A security campaign led by 2,000 Salvadoran troops targets gangs that control 90% of Port-au-Prince.
  • ParaguayPedro Alliana was sworn in as president after Santiago Peña was impeached and placed under house arrest, following the collapse of digital bank Ueno, which left over half the population without access to savings.
