Morning edition · US close Fri 23:00 · Asia Monday morning · futures 05:50

Good morning, Tel Aviv.

Friday was the worst day of the AI sell-off so far: the Nasdaq 100 lost 5.6% and the S&P 500 3.7%, its seventh straight fall, which pushed it into a correction. Nvidia sank 14% on a report that the SEC is examining its accounting, chipmakers lost 11% and the VIX hit its highest level since April 2025. This morning buyers are back: S&P futures are up 2.2% and Nasdaq futures 2.9%, Taiwan bounced and Asian governments rolled out support measures. In Tel Aviv, the Bank of Israel decides on rates today with the shekel at 3.36 per dollar, its weakest in about a year.

Number of the day
−5.6%

The Nasdaq 100's Friday fall, capping its worst week in six years

The tech index wiped out its gains for the year and ended within a whisker of a bear market, 20% below its peak. The trigger was Nvidia: a report that the SEC is investigating its accounting sent the stock down 14% and a chip index down 11%. Anthropic closed at $88.91, just above its $83 IPO price. Bloomberg's strategists note that after 12 earlier weekly drops of 10% or more, only twice did another losing week follow, in 2000 and 2008; futures are up about 3% this morning.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · stocks
−3.7%

AI euphoria turns to fear as the S&P enters a correction

The morning rotation into defensives did not last: by the close the S&P had fallen 3.7%, its longest losing streak since 2020, and the Dow 1.9%. Bloomberg ties the rout to stubborn inflation, possible Fed hikes and a flood of new paper to absorb. Anthropic led $125bn of US share sales last week, the most in over a decade.

Bloomberg · 20/11Read →
US · Nvidia
−14%

Nvidia sinks on an SEC accounting probe report

Losses deepened on Friday afternoon after a report that the SEC is looking into Nvidia's accounting; a chip index fell 11%. Over the weekend Trump again urged the board to rehire Jensen Huang, and Treasury secretary Bessent stopped short of opposing his return. Senators want Lutnick to explain how chips reached China.

Bloomberg · FT · 20–22/11Read →
US · futures
+2.9%

Futures bounce as buyers return after the wipeout

Nasdaq 100 futures rose 2.9% and S&P futures 2.2% in Asian trading, as analysts called tech oversold after seven down days. Goldman's trading desk sees the panic as a chance to add risk, but warns positioning is still too high for a true capitulation. Bloomberg's strategists expect defensives to keep outperforming.

Bloomberg Markets Wrap · 23/11Read →
US · rates & credit
4.94%

Treasuries rally as SpaceX pays over 8% to borrow

The 10-year yield fell about 10 bp on Friday, the fifth straight day of gains for bonds, as money fled stocks; Treasury futures are slipping this morning. In credit, SpaceX sold $32bn of debt at yields above 8%, and junk yields hit a 19-month high. Goldman expects $2.8trn of corporate bond sales next year.

Bloomberg · 20–23/11Read →
Asia · markets
−3.9%

Asia steps in to stop the rout

Seoul will ease daily buyback limits for three months and prepare a market stabilisation fund last used in 2008; the Kospi still fell about 4%. Taiwan widened short-selling curbs and said it has been buying shares, and its index rose 2.1%. China cut margin requirements. Asia-Pacific stocks are about 19% below their June peak.

Bloomberg · 23/11Read →
Energy & metals
$92.34

Gold rebounds, copper breaks $12,000, oil edges lower

Gold rose about 2% to $3,600 after its worst week in eight months, as traders scaled back bets on near-term Fed hikes. Copper fell below $12,000 a ton in London on doubts about AI-driven demand. Brent slipped slightly as strong exports outweighed Middle East risk, while European gas surged on a cold snap (headline).

Israel · TASE
8.55%

Chip stocks lose a third of their TASE weight since June

A Calcalist analysis finds chipmakers, mainly Tower, Nova and Camtek, made up 12.4% of the TA-125 at the end of June but only 8.55% now, erasing almost three years of gains in weight. Insurers have moved up the rankings instead. TASE reopens today after a 4.4% weekly loss for the TA-35.

Calcalist · 23/11Read →
Israel · rates
3.357

Bank of Israel decides with the shekel at a one-year low

The last rate decision of 2026 comes today, with the policy rate at 3.25%. The dollar rose 1.4% on Friday to about 3.36 shekels, and Globes notes the shekel is at its weakest in nearly a year. A falling shekel, a stock rout and Iran risk all argue against a cut; growth of 8% in the third quarter removes some urgency.

Bloomberg · Globes · 20–23/11Read →
Israel · tech deals
$7.5bn

Amazon close to buying Israeli AI start-up Decart

The FT says talks value Decart at about $7.5bn and a deal could come as soon as early this week, though it could still fall apart. Founded in 2023 by three Israeli engineers, Decart makes software that lets AI models run more efficiently, helping AWS compete with Nvidia. It was valued at $4bn in May.

Financial Times · 22/11Read →
Israel · defence

Greece weighs another €1bn of Israeli defence deals

After buying three air-defence systems for €3.1bn three months ago, Greece is negotiating electronic-warfare systems, long-range missiles and an expanded Elbit-run fighter training centre, citing Kathimerini. A good sign for Elbit and IAI order books at a time of strain with some European governments.

Calcalist asked four senior Israeli investment chiefs about Friday's crash. Meitav says it has started buying, Analyst sees opportunities in chips despite its big exposure, while Altshuler Shaham sees a flight to safe assets. All agree the market no longer trusts AI earnings forecasts.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 23 November

  • ClimateThe UN's COP31 talks floundered after the EU and a few others rejected a draft that failed to restate the pledge to transition away from fossil fuels. It is unclear whether talks will resume.
  • BalticUkraine struck a Russian cargo ship reportedly linked to Russia's armed forces; it caught fire and drifted into Finnish waters. Finland's prime minister warned the war is spilling into its neighbourhood.
  • UkraineRussia hit a power station in Zaporizhzhia and a port in Odessa overnight, straining the city's power supply. At least 14 people were trapped in a hit apartment block near Kyiv.
  • West BankIsraeli settlers attacked several Palestinian villages near Ramallah. Opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot called it Jewish terrorism and blamed ultra-nationalist ministers.
  • AviationDelta briefly halted flights to Atlanta after a weight-and-balance system failed, a week after an air-traffic-control glitch grounded thousands of US flights.
  • MexicoMexico captured cartel boss "Chapo Isidro", one of the FBI's ten most-wanted, with US intelligence help, triggering violence in Sinaloa.
  • Figure750,000: Spain's housing shortfall per the Bank of Spain, ahead of this week's election, a referendum on Pedro Sánchez.

Bessent softens on Huang's return to Nvidia

A week after demanding a shake-up and outside leadership, the Treasury secretary said Huang, who resigned, is now "on the outside" and declined to oppose a comeback. Trump wants him back. The affair with an alleged Chinese spy has exposed rifts in the administration, with senators blaming Lutnick's commerce department.

FT · IPOs
$88.91

Did Anthropic's bankers exercise the greenshoe too fast?

A former Bank of America capital-markets chief argues the overallotment option exists to steady the stock after a listing, not to raise extra cash. By exercising it within a day, the banks gave up their ability to buy shares if the price fell, just before ANT came within five cents of its $83 offer price.

Financial Times · 22/11Read →
FT · opinion
4.78%

Investors must unlearn the lessons of the past 20 years

The writer argues today's yields are not high; post-crisis rates were freakishly low because of bond buying. A study going back to 1311 puts the long-run average real rate at 4.78%. With inflation sticky and debt rising, high volatility now threatens the haven status of Treasuries themselves.
