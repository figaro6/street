Friday 27 Nov 2026 · 17:15 Tel Aviv
Afternoon edition · Wall Street's first 35 minutes · US data 15:30 · TASE Friday close 13:44

Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street came back from Thanksgiving with a jump and then gave half of it back. The S&P 500 opened 1.1% higher and the Nasdaq 100 1.6% higher; half an hour later both were up only about 0.5–0.6%, with Nvidia down 2% as Washington tightens rules on Chinese access to its chips. The 15:30 trade data was not encouraging: the US goods deficit unexpectedly widened to $149.5bn. In Tel Aviv the short Friday session closed with the TA-35 up 1.5%, but the shekel's morning gains faded with the Nasdaq: the dollar fell as low as NIS 3.28 and is back at about 3.33.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · the open
+0.5%

Black Friday rally loses steam within minutes

Investors back from Thanksgiving bought the beaten-down tech names at the open, but the gains shrank quickly in a session that lasts only three and a half hours. Bizportal says analysts see the bounce mainly as a technical correction, with the questions behind the panic, including AI infrastructure financing costs, still open. SA News notes the S&P 500 is on track for a fourth straight week of outsized moves, up about 3.9% this week.

US · data 15:30
$149.5bn

Goods trade deficit widens unexpectedly

The advance October goods deficit came in at $149.5bn, well above the $133bn consensus and September's $135.1bn. Imports rose $21.1bn to $365.3bn, while exports added $6.7bn. Wholesale and retail inventories also rose. A wider deficit trims GDP arithmetically, though the import surge may partly reflect buying ahead of the planned new tariffs.

US · flows
$100bn

Goldman: systematic funds still in selling mode

Goldman's trading desk estimates trend-following and volatility-targeting funds sold about $70bn of global equities last week and may sell another $100bn in the coming week. These are model estimates, not reported trades. A calmer VIX would slow the selling, and short positions could even force buying, but a new volatility spike would restart it; thin Friday volume magnifies the effect.

Bizportal · 27/11Read →
US · Nvidia
−2.0%

Nvidia sits out the chip rebound

Nvidia slipped after an early gain and is more than 37% below its October peak. US trade representative Jamieson Greer says new rules will close loopholes in Chinese remote access to its chips. Nvidia's halt to server shipments to south-east Asia looks small in revenue terms: customers outside the US, Taiwan and China made up just 1.4% of last quarter's sales.

US · neoclouds
−27%

Nebius hit hardest by the China cloud rule

Since the Commerce Department required data centres to vet customers linked to China, Nebius has lost about 27%, CoreWeave 20% and Iren 29%. Providers already serving such clients need a licence within 90 days, by mid-February. Bizportal notes that Nebius also cut its revenue forecast, so not all of the fall is the rule.

China · stocks
+4.2%

Beijing's support pledge lifts China most in two years

The CSI 300 rose 4.2% after the State Council, chaired by Li Qiang, called for stronger market stabilisation and more insurance and pension money in stocks. Unusually, the meeting was announced during trading. The STAR 50 tech index jumped 15%, and over 400 shares hit their daily limit. From Monday, margin collateral requirements fall from 100% to 80%.

Taiwan · flows
$33bn

Foreigners pull record sums from Taiwan

Foreign investors sold a net $33bn of Taiwanese shares in November, beating October's record, so more than $60bn has left in two months. Last week the Taiex had its worst week since the early 1990s. Investors are selling all AI exposure, even TSMC, whose third-quarter revenue grew 51%; Bernstein and Susquehanna still see $600 for its US shares.

Israel · shekel
3.33

Why the shekel rises and falls with the Nasdaq

The dollar fell as low as NIS 3.28 in the morning, when Nasdaq futures were up almost 3%, and is back at about 3.33 now that the rally has faded. Bizportal explains the mechanism: when foreign portfolios rise, Israeli institutions sell dollars to keep their currency exposure on target, and buy them back when stocks fall. The rate gap with the US still favours the dollar, with the Fed expected to hike in December.

Israel · earnings
−17%

Palram sinks on its side businesses

Palram fell about 17% after net profit dropped 65% to NIS 14.5m. Its core polycarbonate sheets actually improved, with operating profit up 30%, but home products and retail displays lost about NIS 30m of operating profit between them. Elsewhere, T&M's quarterly net profit rose 171% to NIS 13.3m, and Nextcom's revenue jumped 85% on solar farms.

Israel · defence
$33.6bn

Elbit is the world's tenth-largest defence stock

Bizportal ranks Elbit tenth among listed defence companies by market value, the only Israeli name, with its shares up 15% this year. Germany's Rheinmetall, eighth, has fallen 51% this year. It cut its 2030 organic sales target to €33–37bn from about €45bn today, yet its shares rose about 4% after Bernstein had flagged the cut in advance; Bernstein argues drones are replacing artillery.

2032

Turkey and Iran extend their gas deal

Turkey's Botas and the National Iranian Gas Company have reportedly extended their import contract, which expired in July, to 2032, with volume and price formula unchanged; no official announcement yet. Ankara says payments sit in a monitored account usable only for food and medicine. Separately, Australia will make LNG exporters reserve up to 20% for the home market from 2028.
