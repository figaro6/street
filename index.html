Friday 20 Nov 2026 · 06:11 Tel Aviv
Morning edition · US close 23:00 · after hours · Asia midday

Good morning, Tel Aviv.

The AI sell-off moved east overnight. Anthropic's record debut did not lift the mood, and Asia went into full risk-off: Taiwan fell 5%, heading for its worst week since 1991, while Korea and Japan lost more than 4%. Anthropic slipped 4.7% after hours, below its first-day low, gold dropped another 2% and money went into Treasuries and the dollar. S&P futures point a little lower, and Tel Aviv starts its short Friday session after Thursday's 1.2% fall, with the dollar above 3.31 shekels.

Asia takes the AI hit

Number of the day
−17%

Taiwan's loss this week, its worst since 1991

The Taiex fell as much as 6.4% on Friday, taking its weekly drop past 17%, even though the authorities have tightened short-selling curbs twice this month. Tech is more than 70% of the index, so it takes the full force of doubts over whether AI returns will justify the money being spent and borrowed. Korea's Kospi fell 4.6% and Japan's Nikkei 4.1%, and MSCI's Asia-Pacific index is heading for its worst week since March 2020. For Tel Aviv, it signals another risk-off day for chip and tech names.

The stories behind the numbers

Global · stocks
7 days

World stocks head for longest losing run since 2023

MSCI's global index fell for a seventh day, its longest slide since September 2023, as the AI trade cracked after this year's bull run. Its emerging-markets gauge is now 20% below its June peak, bear-market territory. Pepperstone's Chris Weston said AI sits at the centre of the selling. S&P futures are down 0.2% and Nasdaq futures about 0.4%.

$92.60

Anthropic slips below its debut low after hours

ANT fell 4.7% in after-hours trading, below Thursday's intraday low, a day after closing 17% above its $83 IPO price. Apple said it is building its own AI agent, which Saxo's Charu Chanana said feeds worries that frontier AI may be great technology but a hard business to profit from. Bloomberg says the listing made Anthropic the world's seventh most valuable company.

Asia · tech
−5.2%

Taiwan, Korea and Japan lead a full Asian rout

Taiwan and South Korea, the region's main AI proxies, took the brunt, with the Taiex down more than 17% this week. Japan's Nikkei is set for its lowest close since April, and the Hang Seng is 20% below its 2026 high. KCM Trade's Tim Waterer pointed to AI doubts and hawkish Fed talk. Japan Tobacco fell after booking an $8.6bn loss on Russia's seizure of its business.

Nvidia in talks to buy AI start-up Inception

Nvidia is in advanced talks to buy Inception, a two-year-old developer of diffusion-based language models, for more than $500m, according to The Information. Its three co-founders would join Nvidia. Separately, Bloomberg reports California is investigating whether Nvidia chips or confidential data were improperly obtained by China.

Ross Stores forecast fourth-quarter comparable sales growth of 4–5% and full-year EPS of $8.75–8.84. Gap guided adjusted EPS of $2.29–2.34 as it lowered its full-year outlook. Gold heads for its worst week since March

Gold fell 2.1% to about $3,550, on track for a weekly loss of nearly 9% and its lowest level since September 2025; silver is down about 14%. Investors preferred Treasuries and the dollar, which is heading for a fourth weekly gain. Bloomberg says foreign buyers converting cash to buy Anthropic shares added to dollar demand. Copper also slid as the AI rout spread to metals.

Migdal and Clal book billions on Anthropic

Migdal Insurance and Clal Insurance were among the first Israeli institutions to back Anthropic, Globes reports, and the IPO and private placement turned that bet into gains of billions of shekels in about 18 months. The money belongs to policyholders and pension savers. It is a rare bright spot for insurers, whose index fell 3.1% on Thursday.

FT · semiconductors
China

TSMC and Samsung halt some chip shipments to China

The two chipmakers have told Chinese customers they will suspend some processor shipments while they seek US licences, after a new Commerce rule closed a route through packaging and testing firms. Samsung's Chinese clients include ByteDance and Baidu's Kunlun unit. It deepens chip shortages in China, already hit by soaring memory prices.

FT · Middle East
9 months

Gulf flare-up revives fears of a new Iran war

A week of tit-for-tat strikes has brought Iran and the US closer to full confrontation. US jets hit a missile site on Qeshm island in the Strait of Hormuz, and Iran targeted Patriot systems at a US base in Kuwait. Trump is swinging between threats and calls for a deal while Washington moves in warships and aircraft.

Financial Times · 20/11Read →
FT · credit
12–13%

AI-exposed software pays up: Qualtrics bonds at 80c

JPMorgan is offering steep discounts to revive a stalled $2bn high-yield sale tied to Qualtrics' $6.75bn purchase of Press Ganey Forsta. The bonds are being pitched with an 8% coupon at about 80 cents, a yield of 12–13%. It shows the premium investors now demand from businesses seen as vulnerable to AI.

Financial Times · 19/11Read →
FT · AI debt
$43bn

Lenders want SpaceX's chip collateral kept on Earth

Lenders to SpaceX's $43bn debt package, secured on advanced AI chips, tried to stop Musk launching that collateral into orbit for space data centres, since they could not seize and sell it after a default. The bonds sit in an off-balance-sheet vehicle. Bloomberg adds that SpaceX is eyeing a further debt sale of at least $32bn.

Financial Times · Bloomberg · 20/11Read →
FT · opinion
5 years

Richard Waters: what Anthropic is really worth

Apple took more than 40 years to reach a $1tn value and Google about 20; Anthropic hit $1.5tn at its IPO price in five. Waters argues the speed shows how untested the business is, and that it is unclear where AI profits will settle. As growth matures, he expects it to move at the slower pace of an enterprise software company.
