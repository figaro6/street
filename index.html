Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

US inflation ran hotter than hoped: October CPI rose 0.4% and core 0.3%, both a tenth above forecasts. That all but locks in a December Fed hike, and traders now see a small chance of a half-point move. The 2-year Treasury yield jumped 17 bp to 4.73% and the S&P 500 opened 0.7% lower. Tel Aviv is ignoring the noise: the TA-35 is up 1.4% near 4,071, led by insurers and banks, even as the shekel weakens to 3.18 per dollar.

Key numbers

Hot CPI hits Treasuries; Tel Aviv shrugs it off

Number of the day
+17 bp

The 2-year yield jumps after hot inflation

The 2-year Treasury yield, the bond most sensitive to Fed expectations, rose 17 bp to 4.73% after October core CPI came in at 0.3% against a 0.2% forecast. Hours earlier, Fed governor Christopher Waller said a third straight 0.3% core reading would make a 25 bp December hike too timid. Traders now fully price a December hike, add bets on at least two hikes by mid-2027, and give a half-point move a small chance. The 2s10s curve flattened to about +25 bp.

US data at 15:30

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · CPI
+0.4%

October inflation cools less than hoped

Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, down from 0.6% in September but above the 0.3% forecast. Core prices rose 0.3% for the third month running; economists expected 0.2%. A Vanguard economist blames tariffs, energy and AI-driven demand. Bank of America calls inflation "relentless" and warns the Fed's credibility is at risk after nearly six years above target.

FT · Bloomberg · 10/11Read →
US · Fed
14.7%

A half-point December hike is now on the table

Waller said this morning that another 0.3% core reading would justify a 50 bp hike, the largest since 2022; the data delivered exactly that. Seeking Alpha puts the market-implied chance of a half-point move at 14.7%, with a quarter-point hike fully priced. Bloomberg says traders added bets on at least two hikes by mid-2027. The Fed already hiked in September.

FT · Bloomberg · Seeking Alpha · 10/11Read →
US · the open
−0.7%

Stocks slip as higher front-end yields bite

The S&P 500 fell about 0.7% in the first minutes, and the Nasdaq 100 dropped as much as 1.3% before recovering part of the loss. Franklin Templeton says higher short-term yields and a stronger dollar weigh on valuations. NewEdge notes positioning is at the 20th percentile, leaving room for a relief rally. Bloomberg warns global macro funds have barely cut their equity exposure.

Bloomberg · 10/11Read →
US · IPO
$1.4tn

Anthropic sets its IPO range at $70-78

Anthropic and some investors plan to sell 825mn shares, raising up to $64bn as the roadshow starts today. The fully diluted valuation is $1.26-1.41tn, up from $965bn in May but below the $2tn some had hoped for. A further $26bn private placement goes to Nvidia, AMD, Micron, Broadcom, TSMC, Google and Microsoft. Pricing is due after this week's meetings; the listing could come next week.

Nvidia slides again as brokers trim targets

Nvidia traded near $170.63, extending Monday's 4.3% fall. Citi and Wells Fargo cut price targets ahead of the 17 November results, according to Seeking Alpha. Bloomberg's Big Take says the board's probe into the spy affair has turned Huang's dominance into a succession risk. BofA also removed Nvidia, Micron and Lam Research from its US 1 list (headline).

Energy · oil
$89.72

Brent breaks below $90 for the first time since August

Brent fell as much as 3.7% to $89.16 after Trump said renewed strikes on Iran were highly unlikely and that "Iran militarily is over". It is down about 24% in November, set for its biggest monthly drop since 2020. Cheaper oil has supported stocks worldwide. The one-year US inflation swap has slipped below 2%, a sign markets expect energy to cool prices ahead.

Gulf producers start a price war for buyers

Adnoc cut the premium on its flagship Murban crude for December by more than 60%, right after Saudi Arabia's cuts, as Middle East supplies rebound. Other UAE grades were trimmed too. Bloomberg separately reports a liquidity squeeze is adding to economic strain in Saudi Arabia, and Russian crude loadings were halted at a key Pacific port.

Israel · TASE today
+1.4%

Tel Aviv climbs on insurers, banks and defence

The TA-35 rose to about 4,071, with insurers up 3.2% (Menora, Phoenix, Migdal, Harel), banks 2.6%, Leumi 3.1% on the day's biggest turnover and Elbit 3.5%. OPC jumped about 4% after profit rose 120% on US power demand. Azrieli fell 2.7% after Norway froze new grid connections for data centres until August 2027, which affects its Green Mountain unit.

Israel · earnings
−17%

Gilat plunges after cutting its revenue outlook

Gilat's third-quarter revenue was $116.2mn, about $10mn below forecasts, and it cut its full-year guide by about $45mn at the midpoint. It blamed satellite launch delays, postponed deliveries and weak business in Peru. Defence revenue grew 53% and margins improved, but investors focused on the guidance, which was reaffirmed only in August.

Revolut wins a conditional Israeli bank licence

The Bank of Israel granted a conditional licence and a permit to set up Revolut Bank Israel, part of its push to add competition to a market dominated by five banking groups. The new bank cannot serve customers yet and must meet further conditions first. Separately, Victory's controller wants to buy 30% of Carrefour Israel's operator in its TASE flotation this month.

FT · SoftBank
$7bn

SoftBank swings to a surprise quarterly loss

SoftBank lost ¥1.1tn in the September quarter, against an expected ¥84bn profit, its first loss in almost two years. A $3.7bn writedown covers advance payments for gas turbines and grid gear for its Ohio data-centre push, meant to be leased to OpenAI. The CFO says there are no talks about downsizing the Ohio site and rebates of up to $2.1bn are possible.

Financial Times · 10/11Read →
FT · Markets
+10.6%

Investors pile into defensive stocks

MSCI's global consumer staples index had its best month since 1998 in October, and healthcare its second best this century. Global tech has fallen over 13% since early October as AI spending doubts met the Democratic midterm sweep. Walmart is up over 15% since the Nasdaq 100's 6 October peak. Citi calls the rotation violent but sees room for it to continue.

Financial Times · 10/11Read →
FT · Fed
50 bp

Waller floats the Fed's biggest hike since 2022

Speaking in London before the CPI data, Waller said he leaned towards a 25 bp December hike even if inflation improved. But if core stayed near 0.3%, a smaller move would put the Fed's credibility at risk. He is the first Fed official to raise the idea publicly; October's data met his threshold.
