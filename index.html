Thursday 5 Nov 2026 · 17:05 Tel Aviv
Afternoon edition · Wall Street's first 30 minutes after the 16:30 open

Good afternoon, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street opened lower as the chip selloff entered a second day: the Nasdaq 100 is down 3.7%, the S&P 500 1.0% and the Dow 0.7%, with the VIX up 11% to 18.5. Investors are weighing what a Democratic Congress means for the AI and data-centre build-out, a day after the best session since August. Tel Aviv fell 3.2% with defence stocks leading, and the shekel weakened to 3.19 per dollar. Brent is back above $97, the Bank of England raised its rate to 4%, and US jobless claims came in lower than forecast.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 5 November

  • Iran warVice President Vance blamed the Republican midterm defeat on the Iran war and voter anger over inflation and America's ties with Israel. Trump told the Wall Street Journal the war was "a piece of it." Democrats won both chambers and may gain more than 50 House seats.
  • IsraelPresident Herzog began talks with party leaders on who should try to form a government. Netanyahu's bloc won most seats but fell short of a majority, and Eisenkot's coalition could muster one only with Arab parties. Electoral officials dismissed Netanyahu's "stolen election" claim.
  • MarketsThe brief said US shares recovered on Vance's comments; that text predates today's reversal, and Bloomberg now shows the Nasdaq sharply lower. Aramco's adjusted quarterly profit was $31bn, showing how the oil giant is coping with the war.
  • FranceRiot police fired tear gas in Lyon as unions and students held nationwide protests and a public-sector strike over the cost of living. Up to 700,000 protesters are expected outside Paris, five months before a presidential election.
  • Hong KongA court sentenced activist Joshua Wong to ten years under the national-security law, extending the six years he has already served.
  • Air travelAt least 14 bomb threats disrupted flights across Europe on Thursday, with planes diverted to Vienna and a military base in Normandy. Britain called the threats malicious hoaxes; no explosives were found in earlier cases.
