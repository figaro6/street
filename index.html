Morning edition · US close 23:00 · Nvidia after hours · Asia midday

Good morning, Tel Aviv.

Nvidia did what it had to: revenue more than doubled to $115.8bn, next quarter is guided to $130bn and next year's growth target was lifted to 75%, sending the shares up about 5% after hours. The board also shut the door on Jensen Huang's return. But Asia was not convinced: Nasdaq futures are up 0.5% while Hong Kong fell 1.1%, and gold slid below $3,800 as traders fully price a Fed hike in December. Today the AI trade faces its next test, Anthropic's IPO pricing, while Israel digests a $31bn defence budget hole left by the end of US aid.

Key numbers

Nvidia lifts futures, gold cracks $3,800

Number of the day
75%

Nvidia's new revenue-growth forecast for next fiscal year, up from 70%

Interim CEO Colette Kress raised the growth goal for the year to January 2028 to 75%, on top of a $130bn fourth-quarter guide (±3%) against $125.3bn expected. Third-quarter revenue rose 103% to $115.8bn and gross margin was 74.8%. The shares jumped 4.8% after hours. The caveats: free cash flow of $43.8bn missed the ~$57bn analysts expected, receivables rose by almost a third to $81bn, and Kress promised a more "disciplined" approach to financing customers.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · earnings
+4.8%

Nvidia beats, raises, and rules out Huang's return

Revenue more than doubled to $115.8bn and the fourth quarter is guided to $130bn; the growth goal for fiscal 2028 rises to 75%. New board chair Harvey Jones said the company does not intend to reinstate Huang, and the CEO search will include outside names. Nvidia has also stopped shipping even the older chips it was allowed to sell to China, worth $2.4bn last quarter.

An Asia-Pacific gauge slipped 0.1% and the Hang Seng fell 1.1%, while Chinese bank shares kept rallying as money leaves the AI trade. US · Nvidia risks
$81bn

The cracks under Nvidia's beat

Money owed by customers rose by almost a third in three months to $81bn, and free cash flow of $43.8bn fell well short of the ~$57bn expected. Total commitments reached $518bn, including $390bn for supply, mostly memory. Short seller Muddy Waters disclosed a position on Tuesday, and Huang and the general counsel testify to Congress on 1–2 December.

Gold · Fed
$3,789

Gold breaks below $3,800 on Fed hike talk

Gold fell 1.6% to its lowest since September 2025 after Fed officials argued for more hikes to fight stubborn inflation. Traders fully price a quarter-point increase at the 8–9 December meeting and more than 80 bp of tightening by September 2027. Silver lost 2.6%, taking its 2026 drop to about 36%.

Brent slipped to $92.5 even as Kuwait and Bahrain reported intercepting attacks and US jets struck an Iranian missile site on Qeshm Island. The IEA and Goldman see a global surplus, and US crude stocks rose 5.3mn barrels last week, according to the API. Brent has lost about a fifth of its value this month; official inventory data come out today.

Israel · budget
$31bn

End of US military aid leaves a $31bn hole

Finance Ministry officials told the Knesset the defence establishment has committed $7.6bn on the assumption the current aid deal runs to 2028, plus $24bn of contracts from 2029 meant to be covered by a future deal. Trump announced on election day that the $3.8bn-a-year aid will end this year. Officials admitted they were caught by surprise and are unsure when it really stops.

Israel · banks
NIS 3bn

Leumi posts a record quarter; Mizrahi slips

Leumi earned NIS 3bn in the third quarter, the most ever by an Israeli bank in a single quarter, and is paying out half: NIS 1.2bn in dividends and NIS 300mn in buybacks. Mizrahi Tefahot's profit fell 8% to NIS 1.37bn, with a NIS 683mn dividend. Discount named Mercantile CEO Barak Nardi to run its banking division and lead the merger of the two banks.

Tech layoffs mount: nearly 2,000 since October

Nice cut 500 jobs, Innoviz a fifth of its staff, and eToro, Axonius and SolarEdge also trimmed, many citing a shift to AI-driven work. Financial Times · key reads

FT · Lex
$750bn

Lex: Nvidia can trade drama for durability

Lex argues a post-Huang Nvidia will be duller but sturdier: it lost $750bn in value while rivals AMD, Broadcom and Cerebras gained about $320bn. Its governance never caught up with revenue that grew from $27bn to about $400bn in four years. A slower pace of investment pledges to customers may make true demand easier to read.

Financial Times · 18/11Read →
FT · Nvidia probe
$5bn

How Huang's family shaped the Megaspeed dealings

Huang's daughter Madison helped steer Nvidia's public defence of Megaspeed, the Singapore customer under US investigation for smuggling chips to China, while her husband was promoted to oversee Asian cloud deals, the FT reports. Megaspeed bought about $5bn of Nvidia hardware. The board has set up a new national-security compliance committee.

Financial Times · 17/11Read →
FT · Opinion
19.8x

JPMorgan: the AI sell-off makes sense, staying bearish may not

Grace Peters of JPMorgan Private Bank expects hyperscaler free cash flow to bottom in 2027 and turn positive in 2028, as capex growth slows and cloud backlogs swell 140% year on year. Local resistance to data centres makes existing ones more valuable. At about 19.8 times forward earnings, she calls the entry point compelling; risks are stalled adoption and regulation.

Rachman: Putin, Trump and Xi are no new "big three"

Gideon Rachman expects the Shenzhen meeting to be mostly a photo opportunity that all three leaders want for status. The risk is Trump echoing Russian lines on Ukraine or Chinese lines on Taiwan, possibly in exchange for help on Iran. Unlike Yalta, none of them can impose terms on the world.
