Good morning, Tel Aviv.

Wall Street closed sharply higher on Wednesday: the Dow gained 3.0%, its best day since April 2025, and the S&P 500 1.6%, as the Democratic sweep raised hopes of an end to the Iran war. Brent fell more than 8% and the 10-year Treasury yield dropped to 4.84%, the lowest since 10 September. Asia split in two overnight: the Hang Seng jumped 3.4% while Korean chipmakers sank, and Snap and CVS moved after the bell. Tel Aviv lost 2.0% on Wednesday and the shekel is at 3.156 per dollar. Today brings the Bank of England, Airbnb after the close, and Friday's US jobs report.

Brent crude drops more than 8% as the Democratic win raises hopes of ending the Iran war

Wednesday was the biggest daily oil fall for WTI since May. Vice President Vance blamed the Iran war for the Republican defeat, and Senate Democratic leader Schumer pledged to end a conflict now in its ninth month. Lower oil fed straight into bonds: every Treasury tenor fell at least 3 basis points and the 10-year touched 4.84%, a seven-week low. It also helped equities rotate away from AI-linked tech towards cyclical sectors and Asian markets such as China and Australia. Brent is near $94 this morning, still far above where it started the year, so the war premium has not gone.

Dow posts its best day since April 2025

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 5 November

  • Iran warVice President Vance blamed the Republican midterm defeat on the Iran war and voter anger over inflation and Israel ties, and Trump told the Wall Street Journal the war was "a piece of it." Brent slid to around $95. The Kremlin hopes the result will not derail US-Russia-Ukraine talks, and Iranian media gloated.
  • DemocratsThey are on track for more than 40 House gains but lack a veto-proof majority, so major laws are unlikely. Expect efforts to restore health funding, end the Iran war and cancel tariffs, plus heavy oversight aimed at winning back the White House in 2028.
  • IsraelPresident Herzog began talks with party leaders. Netanyahu's bloc won most seats but fell short; Eisenkot's coalition could muster a majority only with Arab parties, which many oppose. Officials rejected Netanyahu's claim that Arab parties "stole" the election, and opposition figures call him "toxic."
  • Bank of EnglandA rise from 3.75% to 4% is expected on Thursday, the first since August 2023, as war-driven energy costs lift inflation to above 4% by early 2027. The Economist calls it insurance, since wage growth is expected to fall; markets price at least three more quarter-point rises.
  • AI agentsAirbnb reports Thursday, expecting $4.7bn revenue (+15%). Investors will look for signs that AI assistants such as Meta's Muse are taking bookings away; the chief executive calls agents a source of referrals and is building his own.
  • CompaniesCVS will split retail pharmacy and insurance. Snap's daily users missed forecasts, and the shares fell after hours. Elon Musk, who spent about half a billion dollars on Republican campaigns, saw his companies' shares fall.
  • FranceUnions call strikes and marches in hundreds of cities on Thursday over the cost of living, disrupting trains. The interior minister wants to avoid a repeat of the disorder of 17 October.

Financial Times · key reads

FT · AI
3.6%

Martin Wolf asks: are we ready for an AI bust?

Citing a Columbia Business School paper, Wolf notes US AI-related capital spending could average 3.6% of GDP from 2025 to 2032, more than railways at 2.2% in 1870-1890 and highways at 1.1%. That makes it the biggest sector-specific investment boom ever, and so potentially the most disruptive if financing falters. Useful context for this week's chip and data-centre swings.

Trump's Russian diesel deal falters as US stocks hit 20-year low

Two of the first tankers meant for the US veered off course, and US diesel inventories fell below 100 million barrels for the first time in more than two decades. The deal, struck with Putin before the election to cut prices, did little for Republicans. A supply squeeze in diesel is a counterweight to the oil price fall.
