Tuesday 24 Nov 2026 · 23:50 Tel Aviv
Night edition · Wall Street close 23:00 · after-hours earnings · TASE close

Good night, Tel Aviv.

Monday's AI euphoria became Tuesday's rotation. The Nasdaq 100 fell 2% and Nvidia lost about 6%, yet the Dow rose 0.9% to close above 50,000 and most S&P 500 stocks ended higher. Tariff talk and fresh AI-safety worries hit tech, while US consumer confidence hit a near two-year high on the eve of tomorrow's PCE inflation data. After the bell, Dell beat, raised its outlook and showed a record $132bn backlog. In Tel Aviv, Tower led a 0.9% fall in the TA-35, the shekel weakened to 3.34 per dollar, and coalition talks swung towards a unity government.

Number of the day
$132bn

Dell's record AI backlog

Dell fell 11.8% during the session on fears that memory and component costs would squeeze AI-server margins, the worry that sank HPE. After the close it answered with a big beat: adjusted EPS of $9.45 against $6.59 expected, revenue up 103% to $54.9bn, a raised full-year outlook and an AI-server backlog Bloomberg put at a record $132bn. The shares rose about 2% after hours, a modest bounce against the day's drop.

News of the day

The stories behind the numbers

US · markets
−2.0%

AI doubts and tariff talk sink the Nasdaq 100

A day after the AI-led surge, the Nasdaq 100 fell 2% and Anthropic slipped below its IPO price, while the Dow rose and most S&P 500 members gained. An Axios report that some unreleased Anthropic and OpenAI models may be too powerful to release safely added to the unease. Consumer confidence jumped to the highest in nearly two years, which feeds bets on a Fed hike in December.

US · earnings
+103%

Dell crushes estimates and raises its outlook

Dell's third-quarter revenue doubled to $54.9bn, $5.7bn above forecasts, and adjusted EPS of $9.45 beat by $2.86. It raised its fiscal 2027 guidance on strength in its infrastructure (AI server) unit. The stock had fallen 11.8% in the session after HPE's margin warning, and recovered about 2% after hours.

US · after hours
+7%

HP jumps, Nutanix and Urban Outfitters slide

HP rose about 7% to near $30 after its fiscal fourth-quarter report. Workday's adjusted EPS of $2.82 beat the $2.72 consensus and it raised its subscription revenue forecast. Nutanix fell about 6% after saying CEO Rajiv Ramaswami will step down, Urban Outfitters lost 10%, and GoodRx gained 19% on a beat and higher guidance.

US · credit
76.6¢

CoreWeave bonds sink to record lows

CoreWeave's 9% notes due 2031 fell as much as 3.6 points to 76.6 cents on the dollar, and its 2032 notes dropped to 74.9 cents, both lows since issue. The losses wiped out Monday's Nvidia-driven rally and were among the day's biggest in corporate bonds. The shares fell about 12%.

−5.9%

A contrite Huang; Trump's Nvidia windfall

Huang apologised to staff at an all-hands meeting, said he has been in daily therapy and that Nvidia needs no succession plan. The FT found Trump may have held up to 370,000 Nvidia shares, worth as much as $15mn more after Monday's jump. Nvidia closed near $155; the attorney-general called it "a good corporate citizen".

Chip stocks drag Tel Aviv lower

Israel · politics & budget
6 Dec

Coalition talks tilt towards a unity government

The Democrats broke off talks with Gadi Eisenkot as his Yashar party moved closer to a unity deal with Likud, leaving his bloc at 38 seats; Yisrael Beytenu signed the first coalition agreement, with Liberman seeking defence. Netanyahu's refusal to give up the premiership for the first two years is the sticking point. Bizportal says Israel will almost surely enter 2027 without a budget, spending about NIS 58bn a month; debt repayments sit outside that cap.

Global view · FT & The Economist

The world beyond the tickers

The Economist · The world in brief, 24 November

  • Wall StreetThe Nasdaq 100 and S&P 500 gave back part of the Huang rally, with Nvidia down nearly 6% a day after its 31% jump. America's attorney-general praised the company's conduct.
  • AnthropicLess than a week after listing, its shares fell below the $83 opening price. Briefly valued above $2trn on debut, it is now worth about half that.
  • HuaweiA New York federal jury convicted the Chinese tech giant on ten counts, including theft of trade secrets and evading Iran sanctions. It was acquitted of racketeering.
  • ChinaBeijing opened a graft probe into Shanghai party boss Chen Jining, a Politburo member, the latest target of Xi Jinping's purges.
  • TradeChina extended some rare-earth exports to America until 27 January. But a US probe into subsidies by 16 partners, including China and the EU, could soon bring more tariffs.
  • SpainThe government extended its bank windfall tax for a year, worth about €1.4bn for housing, ahead of an election the Socialists look set to lose.

Financial Times · key reads

FT · trade
2.5–5%

Trump readies a new wave of tariffs

Trade chief Jamieson Greer's subsidy probe gives the White House a new legal basis after the Supreme Court struck down the "liberation day" levies. Partners with deals, such as the EU, Japan and South Korea, may face only 2.5–5% more, while Vietnam, Cambodia and Malaysia face steeper duties. Oil, gas and fertilisers are expected to be exempt; how China is treated is the big unknown.

Financial Times · 24/11Read →
FT · Nvidia
War room

How Huang's allies lobbied Trump for his return

A group of Silicon Valley allies pulled in Trump and JD Vance, arguing that Huang is essential to the AI industry and so to the stock market. The board's reversal surprised even senior executives. The FT stresses the risks that remain: FBI, SEC and congressional probes, governance questions and Huang's links to a suspected Chinese spy.

Financial Times · 24/11Read →
FT · China
−30%

China's push from homes into shares stalls

Beijing hoped rising stocks would absorb household savings and restore confidence after the property collapse. Instead the CSI benchmark is more than 30% below its 2021 peak, and the share of household assets held directly in equities has risen by just one percentage point despite years of reforms.
